## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the records are the card.** The house card has 15 blocks, and the screen draws exactly those 15 blocks as a physical card: a slate body with stepped corners and a keyed top-right cut, a paper label, an access light and a 5x3 grid. A save is not a row that lives somewhere else; it occupies 1 to 3 real blocks, allocated to the first free ones, so a save can be split across the card the way a real memory card fragments. Every block is in its game's hue and carries an 8x8 symmetric sprite generated from the player tag, so the staff can recognise "Ania's" blocks at a glance, and adjacent blocks of one save are joined by link pips. Capacity is never an abstract number: the four empty sockets are the free space, and the card refusing a save is a real validation path ("This save needs 3 blocks and the card has 2 free. Delete a save or use fewer blocks.").

- **Every hue has one job.** The ground is gunmetal slate `#27323E`, the grey translucent plastic of a memory card. A lighter slate (`--shell-hi`) is the card body and its back (the dialog); a darker slate (`--shell-lo`) is the empty socket, the input well, the scrim and the ink on light faces. Off-white `--label` is the readable layer: text, the paper label strip and the focus ring. Light slate `--pencil` is pencil marks: secondary text, rules and empty-block outlines. Each of the five games in the bar's library has one hue (`--vistula`, `--comet`, `--kiln`, `--moss`, `--orbit`), used only for that game's blocks, filter chips, row marks, dock pips and the closed write-protect tabs while that game is chosen in the form. Red `--busy` is the access light, and nothing else is red: the light while reading, writing or deleting, the "Do not close this tab" line, validation errors, the card-full state and the delete sticker. `--busy-text` is a lighter tone of the same red for busy text on the card body, where `--busy` would fall below AA.
- **The detail panel says what the card cannot.** Beside the card (900px and up) it runs to the card's height between a top and a bottom rule: the game legend, or the save's actions, settle on the bottom rule so both columns end together. With nothing selected it explains the card (saves, blocks used and free, a legend of the five games with their saves and blocks) and becomes "The card is blank" when it is empty. Hovering or focusing a block previews that save as a six-field definition list; a click, tap or Enter pins it and brings out "Edit this save" and "Delete this save". In preview the actions are hidden (and inert) and a line says how to pin, so the panel never offers a button for a save that will vanish as the pointer moves to it.
- **All saves, as a table.** Sortable by player, game, last played and hours (real `aria-sort` on the headers, the sorted column marked by a stepped pixel arrow), filtered by game with chips and by a "Finished only" toggle. The totals line counts saves, blocks used of 15, hours played and finished games, and says "Showing 2 of 7" when a filter hides rows. Each player name is a text link that pins the save and jumps back to the card with focus on its first block. Under 720px the table becomes stacked rows, and the header row stays as a bar of sort buttons.
- **Six types, parsed honestly.** Player tag (text, 2 to 12 characters, letters including Polish ones), game (choice of five), chapter (integer 1 to 99), hours (decimal with one place; the field is a number input, so a typed comma is refused with "Use digits and a dot, like 12.5.", while the parser itself also accepts "12,5" for stored or ported data), last played (date, not in the future and not before the bar opened on 1 March 2019), finished (boolean, a Yes / Not yet pair with no default, so it is a real answer) and blocks (integer 1 to 3, as a segmented choice). Beyond types, the form checks the card itself: one save per player per game ("Ania already has a Kiln Knight save. Edit that one instead.") and room on the card. Each message says how to fix the value.
- **The write-protect tab.** Every field has a tab at its right end, as a real memory card has. Validity is checked on every input: an open notch while the value is empty or invalid, and as soon as it is valid a tab slides shut across it in two steps, in the hue of the game chosen in the form (pencil before a game is chosen). Messages appear only on blur (once the field has been touched) or on submit; then the tab area turns `--busy` and the message takes the reserved two-line row. Every message, at its longest, fits those two lines at 360, 768, 1024 and 1440, so rows never move when errors appear or clear. The two radio groups (finished, blocks) carry their tab in its own socket at the end of the options: the same dark well and 2px pencil outline as a field, red in the error state. Their legends are floated so they lay out exactly like the field labels beside them, keeping the two columns in step.
- **Stickers.** Every action button is a label sticker with its own two colours: paper (off-white face, slate ink), slate (dark face with a pencil rim, off-white ink) and busy (red face, slate ink). Its corner peels to show the sticker's paired underside colour, and a press inverts face and ink. Filter chips, sort headers and dock pips are not stickers: they change state instantly, as the shared brief asks for small unspecified controls.
- **Different from the nearest designs in the ledger.** features-cyberdeck-kit and cta-indie-game are grids of cells too, but there the grid is a field or an assembly animation; here every cell is a real `<button>` holding a record, in a `role="grid"` with arrow keys. pricing-net-cafe prints a receipt on success; here success is the hardware writing the save in raster rows under a red access light, and delete is the same write played backwards from the bottom, with Undo.
- **Semantics.** A `<section>` with `aria-labelledby` on its `h2`; the dock is a `<nav aria-label="Card gauge">`; the block map is `role="grid"` with rows and gridcells (`aria-selected` on the pinned save's cells), one roving tab stop, and a hidden instruction line. The status line is `role="status"`. The dialog is a native `<dialog>` with `showModal()`, its title an `h2`, labelled by whichever pane is showing. Radios are grouped in `fieldset`/`legend`; every field has `label for`, `aria-describedby` to its message line and `aria-invalid` while wrong.
- **No dead ends.** Add, Edit, Delete and Reset all open the same dialog (form pane or confirmation pane). Saving shows "Saved to blocks 8, 10 and 12. 14 of 15 blocks used" in the card's status line, pins the save and moves focus to its first block. Deleting shows "Deleted. 2 blocks are free again." with an Undo link (focus moves to it) that puts the save back on its own blocks, or on the first free ones if those were taken. Reset asks first. With sessionStorage blocked, the storage notice says so plainly and the card runs from memory.

Deviations from the brief, and why:

- **The peeled corner's flap shows a third colour, the sticker's underside, not only its ink.** On the paper sticker the first version folded down a slate flap that disappeared into the slate ground, and the peel read as a cut corner. Each sticker now has a `--back`: pencil grey for paper, off-white for slate, the lighter red for busy. The hole shows whatever is behind the button.
- **A sticker that opens the dialog waits for its press.** On touch, a tap opened the modal at once and the inverted press was hidden under the backdrop. With motion on, the dialog opens when the 140ms press has been shown; without the libraries it opens at once.

## Do not touch

- **The card is exactly 15 real buttons.** Saves own block indices; nothing else is the source of truth. `allocate()` takes the first free blocks, and an update keeps as many of its own blocks as it still needs before taking new ones. Do not compact the card on delete: fragmentation is part of what the map shows.
- **Each hue's job.** Red only when the card is busy or says no. A game hue only for that game (and for the form's tabs while that game is chosen). Do not use a game hue for an accent, and do not turn the focus ring a game hue: it is `--label`.
- **Sizes come from the window.** `--blk-h` is `clamp(56px, round(down, (100svh - 430px) / 3, 2px), 104px)` (and `(100svh - 372px) / 3` from 52px in wide windows shorter than 720px), and `.card` takes the smaller of that and its share of the content width (58% from 900px). This is what keeps the card and the detail above the fold at 1440x900, 1366x650 and 1280x600. Adding height to the header or the card body breaks the short windows first.
- **Stepped corners live on inner spans** (`.face`, `.blk-face`, `.blk-fill`, `.paper`, `.card`, `.dlg-card`), never on the `<button>` or `<a>`, so focus outlines are never clipped. Block focus rings sit half a gap outside the block, outside the 3px inspect frame.
- **`--p` is a registered `@property`.** The peel animates it in `steps(3, jump-start)` so the corner shows 8, 16 and 24px and no in-between; the flap inherits it. Without `@property` the peel jumps straight to 24px, which is acceptable.
- **Hidden states exist only under `html.js:not(.go)`** (block contents and pips are unread sockets until the card check). With the CDN blocked the `js` class is never added; everything shows and every flow works in plain JavaScript, with the same status text and no animation.
- **Pure functions stay pure.** `P.*`, `allocate`, `addSave`, `updateSave`, `removeSave`, `restoreSave`, `totals`, `sortSaves`, `filterSaves`, `validateForm` and `deserialize` take state and return new state or a result. `runSelfTest()` asserts them; load the page with `#selftest` and it logs `SELFTEST PASS`.
- **User text goes in with `textContent`.** Player tags are never written with `innerHTML`.

## Motion

House stepping: everything moves in whole frames. `steps()` for every tween, discrete `call()`s for frame sequences, `scaleX` in fixed values, `clip-path` in 8 raster rows. No eased motion larger than a few pixels; colour changes are instant. Built in `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, fine pointer). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false` and `lagSmoothing(0)`, `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`, and is stopped while the dialog is open; touch keeps native scrolling. The card check waits for fonts (raced with a 1.5s timeout) and a visible page. The only loops are the icon loop (exists only while a save is hovered or focused, and stops when the card leaves the screen) and the link glyph (a CSS animation that exists only during hover or focus). The cleanup kills timelines and timers, removes listeners, disconnects the observer, destroys Lenis and hides the dock.

## Adapting

1. **Find your fixed-capacity object.** The design is right when the records occupy a visible, limited set of slots: lockers, seats, rack units, parking bays. Set `SIZE`, `COLS` and `ROWS` in the script (and the 5-column `repeat(5, ...)` and `/ 5` in `.grow` and `.card`), and keep every slot on screen at once.
2. **Map the categories to hues, one job each.** Replace `GAMES` (key and display name) and the five game tokens and `.g-*` classes. Keep each hue a light fill with `--shell-lo` ink on it at 4.5:1 or better. Keep `--busy` the only red and give it only "busy or no".
3. **Rename the record.** `P.*` holds one parser per field and returns `{ ok, value, msg }`; `validateForm` adds the cross-record rules (one save per player per game, room on the card). Keep all six types if you need them, and write messages that say how to fix the value.
4. **Re-skin the object.** The card body is `.card` (its polygon keys the top-right corner), the label is `.paper` with the wordmark in `.word`, and the dialog's back is `.dlg-back`. Change the wordmark text in both, and the identicon ink is `--shell-lo`.
5. **Copy.** The h2 is one line at desktop widths (about 32 characters at 64px in a half column). The lede is three lines. Status lines are short and literal ("Saved to block 8"). House rules: three items.
6. **Retime or remove motion.** Every duration is a `--d-*` token read by CSS and by the script. To drop the card check, delete the load timeline (blocks then show at once). To drop the icon loop, make `play()` return. The flip, writing and dock all fall back to instant states when `fx` is null.
7. **In a framework,** keep the pure record functions as a module with their self-test, render the grid, detail, table and dock from state, and port the motion branch as one effect that returns the `gsap.matchMedia` cleanup.

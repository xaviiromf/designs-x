## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the year drawn against daylight.** A 12-month chart with time running down the y-axis from 04:00 to 22:00, like a day planner. Sunrise and sunset close into a straw lens, wide in June and pinched in December. Brunswick gate bars hang from the 08:00 line, each with a white slot cut through it for glasshouse hours. The chart explains the opening rule without a sentence: the gates close at or just before dusk. It shows things a table hides. March and October are split into half-width bars because closing moves an hour after the clocks change. The lens uses a second sunrise and sunset pair for each split month, so the daylight steps an hour at the same x where the bar splits, and every half-bar closes at or before its own half's dusk. The December and January bars start above the lens because the gates open before sunrise: a white hairline crosses each bar where sunrise falls, and the readout says "Gates open 11 minutes before sunrise." Today's bar (or half-bar) is cyclamen, and a cyclamen tick marks the current time in its column. After it has drawn, the chart is an instrument. On a fine pointer, a crosshair snaps to month columns and follows the hour, and the readout says "June at 19:10: gates open, glasshouses closed." Inside March or October the crosshair snaps to the half-bar under the pointer and says which half it means: "October, from 25 October, at 16:50: gates closed." or "March, before 29 March, at 18:00: gates closed." The month radios read the half that contains the 15th, except today's month, which leads with today's half so the readout agrees with the today line (on 25 October: "08:00 to 16:30", then "18:00 before 25 October"). The other half is always the second line, for gates and for daylight. A finger dragged sideways moves through the months, and the keyboard uses 12 real month radios. Everything else on the footer is set in type and stays quiet.

- **The answer comes first, and it is live.** "Open today until 18:00. Last entry 17:30." is computed from the `DATA` table in the garden's own clock (Europe/London through `Intl`, so a visitor abroad still gets the right answer). It has five states: opens today at, open until, last entry was, closed now, and closed on 25 December. The clock-change note appears only within 14 days of a change. "Today, Saturday 24 October", the last Sundays of March and October, the split bars, the readout dates and the next Garden notes date (first Tuesday of the month) are all computed for the current year, so nothing on the page lies next year. The dates typed into the HTML are only the no-JS fallback. A one-minute clock moves the tick and recomputes the status with instant updates and no ambient motion.
- **The supporting kit is plain on purpose.** Links carry a painted 1px Brunswick underline at rest, which turns into a 2px cyclamen one on hover and focus (an opacity change, no sweep). Buttons gain a 2px inner ring on hover, like the lined border of an enamel sign, and press in on click; there is no fill sweep. The email address is a mailto link with no copy button. Sibling dimming happens only inside the chart, where it shows which column is being read. This keeps the footer from repeating footer-architecture-biennale's kit and leaves the chart as the only motion worth remembering.
- **Colour has a material.** White is the enamel of a park-gate sign; Brunswick green (#24513A, 9.08:1 on white) is the railing paint and the only ink, with no stand-in black. Lichen (#56655B, 6.16:1) is secondary text, axis labels and the sun curves. Straw (#E4DA8C) fills the daylight lens only and is never text. The lichen curves bound it at 4.31:1, so the shape reads at better than 3:1. Cyclamen (#B0246B, 6.34:1 on white, white on it 6.34:1) marks today, the now tick, the selected tier, the Join button and focus rings. A limewash tint (#EEF1EC) is only a selected surface: the chosen tier row, the chosen month and the dialog sheet.
- **Type has two clear jobs.** National Park was drawn from the routed wooden signs of the US National Park Service, which suits a garden's wayfinding voice for everything you read and use. Literata is a book serif with a real italic and an optical-size axis. It sets the today line at display size, the h2s and the prices, and it sets plant binomials in italic as botanical convention requires (*Cyclamen hederifolium*), never as a headline accent. National Park has no `tnum` feature: at 100px with `tabular-nums` on, "1111", "0000" and "7777" measured 187.6, 251.6 and 205.2px. So every hh:mm the script writes in the sans goes into fixed-width digit cells (`fig()`, `--fig-w: .63em`) that never wrap. Literata does have `tnum` (232.8px for all three), so the today line and the prices use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. The base is 17px on a 1.25 scale, with 400 for body, 500 for links and labels and 600 only on buttons and headings. There is no all-caps anywhere.
- **Rules only where they are true.** Blocks are separated by space (96 to 128px). The only lines are the chart's hour gridlines on a real time scale, the price-list row rules and one rule above the imprint. That is also why SC16 is honest here: what draws in is structure.
- **Membership is a price list, not a card kit.** It has four native radios in rows: name, who it is for, a right-aligned tabular price and a check badge. The selected row has a cyclamen left rule, a limewash surface and the badge, and its name stays in text, so selection is never colour alone. Joint is pre-selected. The Join button names the tier and price ("Join as Joint members for £64 a year") and keeps its width when the label swaps.
- **No dead ends.** The index links go to anchors in the footer (with focus moved to the target) or open a dialog with two real sentences and Close. The phone and email open with `target="_blank" rel="noopener"` and a hidden "(opens in a new tab)". Join opens a native dialog that collects a name and email only, with no payment details. It ends in "Welcome to Merrow Park. We've emailed a payment link to …" with Close. Garden notes ends in "Signed up. Your first Garden notes arrive on Tuesday 3 November." plus "Use a different email". There is no map link to the fictional address, no social links and no donate flow, because nothing real would back them.
- **Defaults we avoid, reasoned.** The light ground with a serif display is close to default 1, so it is a choice: the ground is white enamel, not cream; the accent is cyclamen, not terracotta; and the serif is there because binomials need a real italic (Literata is a moderate-contrast book face, not a high-contrast display). "Open today until 18:00" is a full sentence with no stat label or gradient, so it is not default 6. Membership rows have no shadows, no hover lifts and one radius (3px, like an aluminium plant label), so they are not default 4. Only the hours block has a load entrance (default 8). There is one text effect on the page (TX1), the readout rolls instead of scrambling, and the chart is the only bold element. There is no footer uncover (SC8): five designs already use it, and here its shade would have covered only empty padding. The plan's Castoro and Albert Sans clashed with gallery-botanical-illustration and nav-bakery, and its limewash ground sat within ΔE 1 of pricing-coworking and footer-type-foundry, so Literata, National Park and a white ground replaced them; limewash survives only as the surface of a selected item.

## Do not touch

- **The time scale.** `yOf(t) = (t - 240) / 1080 * H` maps 04:00 to 22:00 onto the plot height. The HTML axis labels use the same scale (`top: calc(var(--h) / 18 * 100%)`), and the crosshair time label and the now tick read it back. Change the range in one place and the labels drift off their gridlines.
- **The pixel viewBox.** The SVG viewBox is rebuilt in pixels at the measured plot size by a ResizeObserver. That keeps strokes at 1.5px, lens edges sharp and DrawSVG dash lengths true. Do not switch to a fixed viewBox with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, and do not add `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` (it breaks DrawSVG).
- **Labels are HTML.** The axis times, month radios and readout are HTML, so text never scales with the SVG.
- **The month radios are the accessible chart.** The SVG is `role="img"` labelled by the caption. All of its data is reachable through the 12 radios (visible label "Jan" or "J", accessible name "January") and the `aria-live="polite"` readout. Pointer previews switch the live region off so they are not announced, and it is restored on leave.
- **`DATA` at the top of the script is the single source.** Hours, glasshouse hours, the clock-change closing times, sunrise and sunset on the 15th (and just after the clock change for split months), closed days and the topic dialog texts (`DATA.topics`) all live there. The join dialog's titles and summaries are the `data-title` and `data-sum` attributes on the `.tier` labels in the HTML. The self-test (`index.html#selftest`, which logs `SELFTEST PASS`) asserts the clock-change Sundays for 2026 and 2027, the today line on five dates (open, before opening, after closing, Christmas Eve evening, Christmas Day), both sides of each clock change (28 March 18:00 closed against 30 March 18:00 open; 24 October 17:00 open against 26 October 17:00 closed), the clock note, the next send date across the new year, the two-line readout for October, December and January, the readout on 25 October (today's half first), the probe sentences for whole and half months, and that every half-bar closes at or before its own half's sunset.
- **Hidden states only under `html.js`, and never for text that matters.** If a library fails or loads after 2.5s, the `js` class is removed and the finished page shows, with every control still working. SC16 never hides text.
- **Focus.** It is a 2px cyclamen outline everywhere. Every hover has a `:focus-visible` twin, and every hover becomes a persistent or `:active` state on touch. Targets are at least 44px tall, and the month radios at least 24px wide at 360px.
- **The readout never changes height.** The gates and daylight values always render a second line with real content (the other half of a split month, "Closed 25 December", "Last entry 15:30", or how far the gates sit from sunrise or sunset), and the probe sentence has room reserved for its longest form (two lines, three in the narrow 992 to 1239px column). Changing month never moves anything below the chart.
- **The touch drag never blocks scrolling.** The plot is `touch-action: pan-y`, and a finger drag picks a month only after 8px of sideways movement; a tap picks the month under it.

## Motion

Three eases, named on `:root` and mirrored by name in the script. `expo.out` (`--ease-out`) is for entrances, dialogs, rule draws, rings and label swaps. `power2.inOut` (`--ease-draw`) is for the DrawSVG sun curves, the lens and dialog exits. `power3.out` (`--ease-follow`) is for the crosshair `quickTo`, the press release and the tier rule. `back.out(2)` is kept for the tier check badge and the now tick. Everything runs inside one `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, fine `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`, wide 992px). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false` and `lagSmoothing(0)`, feeds `ScrollTrigger.update`, and stops while a dialog is open. The cleanup removes the tick, destroys Lenis and removes the crosshair and its listeners. Only transform, opacity, clip-path (the lens) and stroke-dashoffset (DrawSVG and the checkbox tick) animate. Only the hours block has a load entrance. The chart draws at load only when the whole plot is on screen (from 992px; the stand-in host section is 16svh and the plot `clamp(300px, 38vh, 400px)` so this holds at 1440x900); otherwise it waits until the plot's bottom reaches 95% of the viewport. The desktop load ends at 2.4s: curves and lens by 1.35s, bars by 2.1s, the now tick last.

## Adapting

1. **Tokens first.** Map the brand onto the colour roles, keeping one ink (`--ink`), one accent used only for "today, selected, act" (`--accent`) and one quiet fill for the daylight shape (`--daylight`). Re-check `--ink-soft` at 4.5:1 on both `--ground` and `--surface`, and the curves at 3:1 on `--daylight`.
2. **Hours.** Edit `DATA` only: `opens`, `glassOpens`, `lastEntry`, `closed`, and per month `gates`, `after` (closing time from the clock change), `glass`, `sun` (sunrise and sunset on the 15th, local clock time) and, for a month with `after`, `sunAfter` (sunrise and sunset just after the change, in the new clock). A month with `after` gets a split bar from its last Sunday; the self-test fails if a half closes after its own sunset. For a southern-hemisphere venue, also change the `[2, 9]` clock-change months listed in `clockNote()`.
3. **Time zone.** `gardenNow()` uses `Europe/London`; change it to the venue's zone.
4. **Copy.** Keep the today line as one or two short sentences, with a no-break space before each time. The readout sentences follow "<Month> at <hh:mm>: gates open, glasshouses closed." and, for a split month, "<Month>, before|from <day> <Month>, at <hh:mm>: …". Keep tier names to one or two words; longer "who it is for" lines wrap under the name below 600px.
5. **Fonts.** Any wayfinding sans works for `--font-sans`. If it has `tnum`, set `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` and drop `fig()` and `--fig-w`. If it has none, set `--fig-w` to the width of its widest digit. The serif needs a true italic for binomials.
6. **Retiming or removing motion.** Durations live on `:root` (`--dur-*`) for CSS states (press, release, ring, rule, dim, roll, tick); the load sequence is one timeline at the end of `initMotion()`. The chart choreography is one timeline in `drawChart()`; scale it by changing the position numbers, keeping the order gridlines, curves, lens, bars (ending on today's), slots, tick. To shorten the load, move the `tl.add(drawChart, .3)` call earlier or make the chart always wait for scroll. To remove the crosshair, delete the `if (fine)` block (the radios and readout keep every answer). To drop the libraries entirely, remove the five CDN tags: the script falls back to the finished page with every control and form still working.

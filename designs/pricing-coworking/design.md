## Why it works

**The bold idea: the floor plan is the price list's consequence.** Choose a plan and its fob is read at the front door: the Walk drains back to the front door, then green runs east along it from the west end, and every room that fob opens fills from its own door as the flood passes it. Hover or focus another tag and the building previews the difference. Members of a coworking space buy access, not features, so the page shows access: which doors, and when (a week of day columns under the plan, 00 to 24 down each day, fills in the same pass).

- **Commit pours, preview diffs.** Choosing a plan (and the first load) is one pour: the Walk and any room the new fob does not open drain to their doors (0.25s expo.inOut), then the Walk floods east in `--d-walk` (1s, power2.inOut) and each room the fob opens starts its 0.5s expo.out fill at the moment the flood reaches its door. Rooms that are already lit and stay open are not blanked: each one dips to 30% toward its door (0.12s) and re-floods from the door edge (0.5s expo.out) at the moment the flood head passes it, with its door leaf swinging shut and open, so a commit after a hover preview is a visible re-pour in walking order, never a flicker of a lone corridor among solid rooms. The week pours in the same pass: the day bars empty to the 08:00 tick, then regrow to the fob's hours. `data-pos` is that door's position in metres along the 96 m Walk, so the order is walking distance and the far end (Reef, Sheet Bend) opens about 0.85s after the front desk; you can watch the fob's reach travel. A preview is a diff, so sweeping the pointer across five tags never strobes the building: rooms both plans open stay lit, rooms only the old plan opened drain, rooms only the new plan opens fill in walking order (0.045s a rank). The caption says the change in words ("Fixed desk adds the Quiet Bay and lockers, and opens at any hour."), and a "Chosen: Hot desk" line with the green check names the committed plan next to the building. Every flood starts from what is on screen, so an interrupted one never leaves a room half full.
- **One colour, one meaning, and why it is one.** The plan first gave each fob its own colour (yellow hot desk, blue fixed desk, red studios). Built, that failed three ways. A diff preview needs a room that both plans open to stay put, but with per-plan colours every shared room changed colour on every hover, so the building flickered on each tag and the change stopped being readable. Three saturated tag colours read as bronze, silver and gold tiers, the SaaS card kit this collection avoids. And red is 'denied' on a card reader, the opposite of what a red studio fob meant. Real access readers have one 'granted' light, green, so the page has one green, #50D47E, and it means 'your fob opens this door'. It appears only in plan fills, the bookable stripes, the hours bars, the legend swatches and the chosen check (in the tag's ring hole and on the plan's "Chosen" line, where it means 'this fob is active'). Buttons, NV4 pills, focus rings, links, rules and form results are tar. Open is solid green, bookable is green-and-limewash stripes, locked is tar hatch, so the key never relies on colour alone.
- **Not default 2.** Default 2 is a near-black page with one acid-green or vermilion accent on type and buttons. Here the ground is a cool limewash (#EEF0EC), the ink is a warm Stockholm-tar brown (#3A2A1E, 12.0:1, not a tinted near-black), and the green is a mid-saturation signal green, not acid chartreuse. It is never set on dark, never used for text, links or calls to action, and it covers areas of a drawing (rooms, hours) with tar text on it at 7.2:1. It is a material state in an architectural drawing, not an accent.
- **Tags are the real object.** Each plan is a tar plastic key tag cut to a key-tag silhouette: a narrow top (64% of the width) carrying the ring hole, with shoulders cut down to the top of the paper window (`clip-path: polygon(18% 0, 82% 0, 100% var(--shoulder), …)` over the 22px bottom radius), so a row of five reads as tags on a board, not rounded cards. All five share one shape, so Flip stays translate-only. The punched 18px ring hole sits centred in the taper (a CSS mask, so the page shows through) and a paper insert. All five share one material because colour now means 'open'; plans are told apart by name, contents and the drawn brass key on the ring of the three studio tags, which do open their own room with a key. Radii follow role: walls 0, tag 22px, window 10px, buttons 6px, pills round. No shadows.
- **Headcount reorders the shelf.** "Desks needed" re-sorts the tags by monthly total for that many people and moves studios that are too small behind a "Too small for 3 of you" divider. A too-small tag's plastic greys out, its paper keeps full-contrast text, and the disabled button reads "Seats 2" (labelled "Studio for 2, seats 2: too small for 3 of you"). Per-person plans multiply; studios show the price per head, and at one desk what the price buys ("The whole studio, £360 a desk for 2"). If the chosen plan stops fitting (a Studio for 2 when you need 3 desks), the choice moves to the cheapest plan that fits and the building re-pours for it, so a disabled button is never the chosen one. Yearly is 90% to the penny with the yearly bill stated.
- **Phones stack the tags, and a strip of the building rides under them.** Below 992px the tags stack in cost order, then the divider across the full row, then the dimmed tags that do not fit (one column on phones, two or three on tablets), and Flip moves them when the headcount changes, so the reorder and the divider are in the reading order rather than hidden off the edge of a sideways rail. The labelled plan follows the tags (24 rows by 3 columns, 58% of the small viewport), and it follows them in the DOM too, so Tab order matches what you see. So that the signature is never 2000px away, the script clones the floor once into a reach strip (aria-hidden; walls, hatch and fills, no labels, 24 by 3 and 52px tall) that sticks to the bottom of the tag list on every screen off the stage. From 992px, where the plan sits above the tags, the strip takes its own height after the last row, so at the end of the list it rests below the last Choose button instead of over it; `scroll-padding-bottom` is its measured height, so focus never lands under it. On phones the hero is tightened (two-line heading, legends beside their toggles) so the first tag's price and Choose button clear the strip at 390x844. Every fill drives both floors from the same state, so the first load and every Choose pour along the strip from the front door, right under the button you pressed. The strip is a link, "See the full plan", and steps aside once the labelled plan is on screen; that plan arrives already poured, and as it scrolls in the doors the chosen fob opens swing open in walking order (again only if the choice changed). Each tag has "See its doors on the plan" and the plan has "Back to plans".
- **The plan stays where you are.** At 1200 x 720px and up the plan is sticky above the tags, so every control shows its consequence in the building. It sticks with its title and legend scrolled off (`top: calc(-1 * var(--plan-cut))`), leaving the floor, the chosen line, the caption and the hours; at 1440x900 a whole row of tags with their Choose buttons fits under it. `html { scroll-padding-top }` equals the stuck height, so focus and anchors never land under the plan. Scrolling into the meeting-room list switches the plan to what a booking opens: the extra rooms drain, the four meeting rooms get an inset tar ring and their hourly rate; scrolling back up refloods the chosen fob. At the trial form the plan becomes a day fob ("What a day fob opens": the shared spaces, weekdays), and focusing or changing "Plan you are considering" previews that plan as a diff against your chosen fob until the select loses focus. The plan title names what the building shows; the "Chosen: Hot desk" line keeps naming your fob, and its green check cross-fades to an unlit tar ring (the same 18px, so the line keeps its left edge) while the building shows a booking or a day fob.
- **The plan transposes.** It is a CSS grid of divs (24 tracks along the length, 3 across). Each room carries `--at` (its span) and `data-side` (north, Walk, south). Desktop places `--at` on columns; under 992px the same markup places it on rows, west end on top. Door side is four multipliers per room (`--t --r --b --l`) redefined per breakpoint, and the fill's `clip-path` inset is computed from them and `--fill`, so every room fills from the wall its door is in. Door glyphs (a leaf and its quarter swing) open as their room fills and shut as it drains. Under 992px each day bar stays 56px tall (72px wider up), so the 08 and 19 ticks stay a clear line apart and a weekday band reads on a phone.
- **The slab is Zilla Slab 700, not the planned Rokkitt 800.** Rendered side by side, Rokkitt's heavy weight has round geometric bowls and a swash pound sign that reads as ornament at price size, the one place readers compare numbers, and its figures have no tabular set (`tnum` is ignored: '1111' is 55px and '0000' 93px at 40px). Zilla Slab's flat-cut slabs and squared joins sit closer to the stencilled and painted lettering of a working mill, its £ is plain, and it sets at the same width. One weight (700) is loaded.
- **Filled means a state on tags and a commit in forms.** On a tag, solid tar is 'Chosen', so 'Choose X' stays outlined and fills only on hover. The two form submits ('Book Reef for 2 hours', 'Book my free day') are solid tar at rest and wipe limewash in from the entry edge on hover, so the strongest mark in the booking sheet is its action, not the selected length.
- **Figures are chosen per job.** Display prices use Zilla Slab's proportional lining figures (`lining-nums proportional-nums`), because its tabular digits leave visible gaps ('£ 1 6 5') at 28 to 44px. While a price counts, the script holds the span at the wider of its start and end values as a `min-width` in `ch`, so "a month" does not jitter, and releases it when the count ends. Tabular figures are kept where numbers line up in columns: the room rows, the hours ticks and the dialog total.
- **Copy is specific.** Prices include VAT; meeting rooms are named after knots; the Ofcom drama number on a `tel:` link; results are computed from the inputs ("Reef is booked for Friday 23 October, 10:00 to 13:00."), with weekdays from `Intl.DateTimeFormat('en-GB')` on local dates. No eyebrows, no all-caps, no 'most popular'.
- **Cut at critique:** the masked line rise on the heading (the stock award-site entrance, a fifth load effect competing with the pour; the heading is set statically and SplitText is not loaded); a "Showing: a meeting-room booking" line that repeated the caption; the permanent divider track that left the tag row short of the measure (the divider is now a rule drawn in the gap); the wall draw and delayed pour on phones (the full plan is static and poured; the strip carries the pour); the commit band (a tar-edged pale-green band that ran over a still-lit Walk), the reader tick at the front door and the pulse ring on the chosen badge, three accessories on one moment that the drain-and-pour now says plainly; the phone snap rail of tags (it hid the reorder and the divider off the right edge); a pointer sheen on the tag plastic (it competed with the preview, the real hover answer); the price count on entrance (a static price counting up is a gimmick; numbers count only when desks, billing or booking length change them); a persistent focus ring on the chosen tag; the green rule on form results; sibling dimming on the meeting-room rows (it dropped live prices below AA).

## Motion

All JS motion lives in `gsap.matchMedia` with `motion`, `reduce`, `fine` (hover and fine pointer), `wide` (992px) and `stage` (1200 x 720px). Ease family: expo.out (`--ease-out`) for responses and entrances, expo.inOut (`--ease-inout`) for reflows and drains, power2.inOut only for the flood running the Walk (an even walking pace, so door order reads), back.out(2) only for the badge. Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` (`autoRaf: false`) and is stopped while the dialog is open. The heading has no entrance; the load is the chrome fade, the pills, the wall draw and the first pour.

Touch: no hover preview; Choose commits (and pours along the reach strip off the stage), 'See its doors on the plan' shows a tag's doors on the plan and 'Back to plans' returns and clears that view. On wide touch screens a tap on a tag's body also previews it, and 'Back to plans' appears under the plan whenever a tag's doors are shown, at every width. Load order is safe: the libraries are `defer`red so the inline script sets `html.intro` (first-frame states) before first paint; GSAP takes those states over on `DOMContentLoaded`. If the libraries are missing at `DOMContentLoaded`, `html.js` is removed and the page shows its finished state; if they are only slow, a 2s timer shows it. Either way every control, the dialog and both forms keep working with instant state changes.

## Do not touch

- **Green means open, and nothing else is green.** No green buttons, links, pills, rules, focus rings or success banners. If you add a state, give it a pattern as well as a colour.
- **Fill from doors, in walking order.** A commit drains the Walk and pours from the front door; rooms that stay open dip and re-flood from their door as the flood passes, never blank; a preview is a diff. Never fill from an arbitrary edge, never fill every room at once, never loop the flood as an ambient demo. `data-pos` is the door's position in metres along the Walk; if rooms seem to open together, lengthen `--d-walk` rather than shortening fills.
- **Walls above fills.** Partitions are `.room::after` at `z-index: 2` inside `.floor { isolation: isolate }`, and the outer wall is its own element; outlines on rooms get half-covered by neighbouring fills.
- **Tags keep one size during Flip.** Equal grid tracks, translate-only Flip. From 1200px the 'too small' divider takes no track: it is absolutely placed in the `--tag-gap` before the first tag that does not fit (`--div-col`), with its label set vertically. Animating scale or width makes text wobble.
- **Display prices are proportional; counts hold their width.** Do not put `tabular-nums` back on `.tag__price`, and keep `[data-price]`, `[data-each]` and `[data-total]` inline-block so the count's `min-width` applies.
- **Hidden states only in the first frame.** `html.intro` and `html.js` hide only what the load sequence animates; everything is real text and real controls (radios in fieldsets, buttons with `aria-pressed`, native `<dialog>`, `aria-live` caption).
- **One floor, two drawings.** The reach strip is a clone of `.floor` made at start-up; `ROOMS[id].fill` holds both fills, so anything that floods the plan floods the strip. Add rooms to the markup, not to the strip.
- **DOM order follows the drawing.** Below 992px the script moves the plan after `#plans`, and back above 992px; do not reorder with CSS `order` alone or Tab jumps 2,700px down and back.
- **The sticky threshold and the cut.** The plan is sticky only at 1200 x 720px and up (with shorter tags from 720 to 879px tall), where a row of tags with Choose buttons fits under the stuck plan. `--plan-cut` and `--plan-h` are measured by a ResizeObserver; keep `scroll-padding-top` tied to `--plan-h`. A URL with a hash lands again once fonts and the page have loaded, because the browser's first jump uses an unsettled `--plan-h`.
- **Contrast:** tar #3A2A1E is 12.0:1 on limewash and 12.7:1 on paper; weathered tar #5A4C41 is 7.2:1 and 7.6:1; tar on the access green is 7.2:1. Room labels on green and stripes stay tar; meeting-room labels sit on a limewash chip. Never dim text with opacity: too-small tags grey their plastic only.
- **Dates are local.** `parseDay()` builds `new Date(y, m - 1, d)`; never `new Date('2026-11-03')`, which is UTC and shifts the weekday west of Greenwich.

## Adapting

1. **Tokens first.** Change `--c-ground` (the building's wall finish), `--c-ink` (a chosen dark that is not near-black) and `--c-ink-2`, then `--c-access` to the colour your access system really uses for 'open', keeping ink on it at 7:1 or better. `--c-paper`, `--c-hatch` and `--c-scrim` derive from them. Swap `--ff-display` and `--ff-body`; check the display face's proportional figures at price size and keep a tabular set for the room rows.
2. **Your building.** Rooms are `.room` divs inside `.floor` with `--at: start / span n` along a 24-track length, `data-side` n, w or s, `data-pos` (door position in metres along the Walk, which sets flood order and timing), `data-name`, optional `data-seats`, and `room--meet` for bookable rooms. Keep spans per side summing to 24. If your corridor is not 96 m, change the `/ 96` in the pour. A building with two corridors needs a second Walk row; doors then belong to whichever corridor they open onto.
3. **Your plans.** Edit `PLANS` (per-person `pp` in pence, or a fixed `price` with `seats`), `SHARED`, `MEET`, `RATE`, `KIT` and `HOURS` in the script, and the matching tag markup. Captions and diff sentences are generated from that data. Three to six tags fit the one-row layout from 1200px; below that the grid wraps and phones stack.
4. **Copy.** Plan names short (about 12 characters), one-sentence descriptions of three lines or fewer, an 'Opens' line written from the member's side, hours as people say them.
5. **Retime or remove motion.** Durations are `:root` tokens. The script reads `--d-fill`, `--d-walk`, `--d-drain`, `--d-step`, `--d-hours`, `--d-flip`, `--d-count`, `--d-swap` and `--d-intent`; `--d-press`, `--d-fast`, `--d-ring`, `--d-pill` and `--d-sweep` are used only by CSS transitions. Lengthen `--d-walk` for a slower, more legible pour; shorten `--d-step` for a snappier preview. To drop an effect, delete its block in the motion layer: the `fx` object holds instant defaults for every effect (`count`, `caption`, `flood`, `reflow`, `badge`, `swap`, `dialogIn`, `dialogOut`, `scrollTo`), so removing a motion override never breaks a flow. Removing GSAP and Lenis entirely leaves a working page with CSS transitions only.
6. **Stack translation.** Keep the state (`S`), the pure functions (`quote`, `order`, `caption`, `diffCaption`) and the effect layer separate; in React, run the GSAP layer in a `useGSAP` hook with `gsap.matchMedia` and call the same `fx` functions from effects. Open the page with `#selftest` after changes (pricing, order, captions, local weekdays).

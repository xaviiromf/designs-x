## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the movement comes apart under your scroll and goes back together.** Calibre R.30 is drawn once, in inline SVG, as six flat plan layers: main plate, barrel, gear train, escapement, three-quarter plate, and balance with cock. Each layer goes through the same isometric matrix (`matrix(.866 .5 -.866 .5 0 0)`), so the plates read as one stacked object. At the top of the sticky stage the movement is closed, and its balance beats at 2.5 Hz (five ticks a second, true to 18,000 vibrations an hour). The first scroll lifts the layers apart along the arbor axis in 60 snapped frames, and the balance visibly loses amplitude with each frame until it stops. The open stack holds for a moment with its labels. Five chapters then follow the power path, from the crown to the barrel, train, escapement and balance. In each chapter the part being described redraws itself in ruby while the rest dims, the drawing reframes on it, and the part turns under your scroll at its real rate: the reader's scroll becomes the power travelling through the movement. At the end of the pin the stack closes again, and once the last frame seats the balance restarts with an amplitude ramp under the line "Back together, it runs.", followed by a link on to the regulation readings. Everything else on the page is quiet so that this reads.

- **The order is the content.** The chapters follow the real power path, so the numbering ("1 of 5" on the rail) encodes a true sequence. Ruby lights a part, not a layer: Winding redraws only the crown, stem, crown wheel, ratchet and click (they sit on two layers, tagged `.wind`), while the three-quarter plate they are screwed to dims with everything else.
- **One value drives the stage.** A single ScrollTrigger gives progress `p`. Every state is derived from `p`: the frame, the balance amplitude while the stack opens, the chapter, the lit part, the rail fill and each part's angle. Chapter changes (ruby redraw, camera, caption) are time-based transitions, never scrubbed opacity, so the stage is always readable at rest; only the mechanism itself is scrubbed, because that is the point. The last chapter is about 15% longer than the others.
- **The mechanism moves at its real ratios.** In its chapter the ratchet turns 60° while the crown wheel drives it the other way, the barrel turns an eighth (one of its eight hours), the centre wheel turns 6° while the third wheel turns 7.5 times as far and the fourth wheel a full minute's turn, the escape wheel steps in 24° jumps (15 teeth) while the pallet fork rocks, and the balance swings twice. Each part rotates on its own `g[data-rot]` inside the iso group, so the ruby copy turns with it.
- **Even gaps along the arbor.** The exploded depths are computed from each layer's measured projected extent (`EXT`), so every gap between neighbours is the same 18 units at full width. Where the tighter box cannot hold clear gaps, neighbours overlap by an even 10.
- **Hidden-line drawing.** Every plate has an outline (drawn) and a fill (in the stage colour), each on its own element. Upper plates therefore hide the parts beneath them, as in a printed exploded view. On load the outlines draw from the bottom layer up, and each fill fades in halfway through its outline, so the movement visibly assembles.
- **Colour comes from materials.** The ground is the vivid royal blue of a heat-blued screw (`--steel-night` #1E348C), with the stage and paper one temper deeper (`--steel-plate`) and labels on fills in the darkest temper (`--steel-ink`). Text and linework are cool rhodium-plated German silver; there is no cream anywhere. Ruby is the jewel colour and gilt the chaton colour, the only two accents a real movement has. Ruby means "the part we are talking about" everywhere: the lit layer, the jewels, the primary button, the chosen position. At 3.6:1 on the ground it is used only for graphics and fills; ruby text and focus rings use the lighter `--ruby-lite`. Gilt appears only on chatons and on the timegrapher's tolerance fan. This is a chosen blue, not the near-black-plus-one-accent default: the ground itself is the material.
- **Lettering from the dial.** Gloock's hairline-to-stem contrast is the contrast of printed dial numerals and of the engraving on a balance cock, so the headings and the drawing's engraved "Ravoire R.30" share one voice. Ysabeau Office, built for UI text, carries everything that is read in quantity or measured.
- **Instrument data, not hero numbers.** The rate, amplitude and beat error are readouts under a timegrapher trace, set in Ysabeau Office 600 at h3 size with tabular figures, so they never jitter while they tween. The trace is computed from the same data: slope comes from the rate, separation from the beat error, and the gilt fan shows the 0 to +6 s/day tolerance.
- **Contrast, checked (WCAG relative luminance):** silver on night 9.2:1, on plate 10.6:1. Rhodium on night 6.2:1, on plate 7.1:1. Ruby-lite (errors, focus rings) on night 5.0:1, on plate 5.8:1. Ink labels on ruby 5.4:1 (the primary button, the filled pill, the chosen position) and on silver 14.0:1. Ruby itself is 3.6:1 on night and 4.1:1 on plate, which passes the 3:1 for graphics and is never used for text. Gilt on night 6.4:1, on plate 7.3:1. Inactive callouts switch to rhodium instead of fading, so dimmed layers never take their labels below AA.
- **Subject truth.** 7.5 barrel turns at 8 hours each give the 60-hour reserve. The fourth wheel turns once a minute and carries the small seconds at six. The escape wheel has 15 teeth. The drawn balance swings ±146°, which is the 292° dial-up amplitude the timegrapher reports. The initials field allows 4 letters, which is the real space on a cock engraving. There is no slipping bridle, because that part belongs to automatic movements. The atelier is fictional, so the only contact is a mailto to an `.example` address, with no street address and no telephone number. The hold date is computed when the form is sent, so the confirmation stays true whatever day it is opened.
- **No dead ends.** Every link points to an existing id (`#top`, `#tour`, `#rate`, `#specs`, `#reserve`, `#ch-1` to `#ch-5`) or to a mailto that opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener"`. The form is `novalidate` and handled in JS. It confirms in place, and "Change details" returns to the filled form. `check-flows.mjs` passes.
- **Accessories removed.** The plan's scroll cue (L6, MI8) was built and then removed: its arrow loop competed with the balance, and once the first scroll visibly opens the stack it said the same thing as the "Take it apart" button above it. A closed movement also stays rigid under the pointer; the parallax grows with the explode, so the plates only shift once there are gaps to look into. Earlier, the "2 of 5 Mainspring and barrel" line above each chapter heading was hidden on desktop, because the rail directly below says the same thing. It stays on phones, where the rail is bars only, and in the stacked reduced-motion layout.
- **Short and sideways screens.** On phones the drawing row is `clamp(28svh, 100svh - 460px, 50svh)` (`- 400px` and at most 40svh under 760px tall), so the caption always gets the room it needs. At 360x640 the longest chapter fits in full with the drawing at about 34svh. Only in the stacked phone layout under 600px tall do the lede and chapter text stop at three lines (`line-clamp`). Phones on their side (under 500px tall) switch to the desktop arrangement, with the drawing on the left and the caption on the right, and show every caption in full. The full text is always in the DOM, and the reduced-motion layout shows all of it.

## Do not touch

- **The stage geometry.** The six layers must share one iso matrix. Pointer parallax moves the inner `g.par`, the explode (and the preview lift) moves the outer `g.layer`, the chapter camera moves `g.cam` around the whole stack, and turning parts rotate their own `g[data-rot]` inside `.iso`; keep each on its own group or they will fight. The SVG ships exploded (`translate` on each layer) so that no-JS and reduced-motion readers get the informative view. Only the motion branch closes it.
- **Time-based state on a scrubbed value.** Frames are snapped (`Math.round` over 59). Chapter light, caption swap and callouts react to index changes. Never scrub caption opacity or the ruby redraw.
- **Ruby means active, gilt means gold.** Do not use gilt as a second accent. `--ruby` is for graphics and fills only; anything that must be read in ruby uses `--ruby-lite`, and labels on ruby use `--steel-ink`.
- **Readouts in Ysabeau Office.** Gloock is a display face with proportional figures. Moving the readouts to it would make them jitter while they tween.
- **Figures in the copy.** 18,000 vph, 2.5 Hz, 15 teeth, 60 h, 7.5 turns and 8 h each must keep agreeing with each other and with `POS` and the specification. Fix the copy if you change the movement.
- **Hidden starting states only under `html.js` and `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`.** The script removes `js` if any library failed to load.
- **The Index sheet holds the page and is not modal.** It stops Lenis and adds `sheet-lock` (overflow clip) to `html`, so the page stays still under reduced motion too. Escape returns focus to the button, a link sends focus to its target, and focus landing anywhere outside the sheet and its button (Tab or Shift+Tab) closes it.

## Motion

Tier: detailed. GSAP 3.15.0 (core, ScrollTrigger, SplitText, DrawSVGPlugin) and Lenis 1.3.26 load from jsDelivr, pinned as in `research/motion-techniques.md` section 3. The `lenis.css` rules are inlined. Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` (`autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`), and `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)` keeps the two in sync. Everything sits in one `gsap.matchMedia` with these conditions: `motion`, `reduce`, `fine` (`(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`), `wide` (992px) and `roomy` (1280px, room for callouts). Cleanup aborts every listener the branch added, removes the ticker callback, destroys Lenis, removes the ruby layer copies and the `staged` class, and resets the balance. Only transform, opacity, clip-path and stroke-dashoffset animate. The native cursor is never hidden.

## Adapting

1. **Your object's layers.** Each `g.layer` holds `g.par > g.iso > plan geometry`, drawn as flat circles and paths around a plate of radius 100. Class `ln` marks an outline that draws, `fill` a plate fill in `--steel-plate`, `teeth` a circle with `pathLength` = 2 x teeth (fades, because DrawSVG cannot draw dashes), and `jewel`, `chaton` and `stone` the coloured details. The rotating part is `g.bal`; its timing lives in `osc` and `drawBal`. Keep four to seven layers. Measure each layer's projected top and bottom once (iso space, translate 0) into `EXT`; `spread(gap)` turns them into exploded depths with even gaps, and the markup's `translate` values must equal `spread(18)` so the no-JS and reduced views match. A part that turns goes in its own `g[data-rot="name"]` inside `.iso`, with its arbor in `AX` and its angle in `mech()`.
2. **Chapters.** Edit the five `article.chapter` blocks, the five rail links, `LIT` (chapter to the layers it lights; a chapter about parts spread over several layers lights only children tagged `.wind`, which you can rename) and `CH` (progress ranges, with the last one about 15% longer). The camera computes its framing from what each chapter lights. With more or fewer chapters, change `--stage-chapters` and the ranges together. The chapter copy should fit 4 to 6 lines at 390px wide.
3. **Timegrapher.** `POS` holds `[rate, amplitude, beat error]` per position, and `trace()` turns rate into slope and beat error into line separation. For another instrument (a dyno sheet, a frequency sweep), keep the paper-feed draw and the readouts, and rewrite the trace function.
4. **Tokens.** Map your brand onto the colours by role: one chosen dark ground, one stage surface, a darker ink for labels on fills, linework and text, secondary, one state colour (the "active part") with a lighter text-safe version for errors and focus, and one material accent used only in the drawing. Recheck the ratios listed above. Swap fonts through `--font-display` and `--font-text`. If the new text face lacks default tabular figures, add `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` to `.readouts dd`.
5. **Retiming or removing motion.** Load timing is in the `load` timeline. The explode window is set by `frameAt`, and its speed by `--chapter` (scroll length per step). Camera timing is in `camTo`. Light and caption durations are in `lightTo` and `swap`, and CSS-driven micro-interactions use the `--ease-*` and `--dur-*` tokens. To drop the parallax, delete the `if (fine)` CU3 block. To drop the beating balance, never call `setRunning`. To drop the libraries entirely, remove the five CDN tags: the page then renders like the reduced-motion version, and the sheet, timegrapher and form keep working.

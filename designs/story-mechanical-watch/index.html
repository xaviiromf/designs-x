One movement, taken apart.

Calibre R.30 is the hand-wound movement we make 24 of each year in Sainte-Croix. Here it comes apart part by part, in the order the power from your thumb travels to the balance.

Take it apart Reserve no. 14 of 24

Scroll to take it apart

1 of 5Winding

Forty turns of the crown

Turning the crown spins the crown wheel, which drives the ratchet wheel on the barrel arbor. A click lets the ratchet move one way only; it is the soft rasp under your thumb. Forty turns take the spring from empty to full, and then the crown stops. Never force it further.

2 of 5Mainspring and barrel

Sixty hours in a coiled strip

The mainspring is a strip of cobalt-nickel alloy 0.10 mm thick and half a metre long, coiled inside the barrel. As it lets down, the barrel turns once every eight hours, seven and a half turns from full to stopped.

3 of 5Gear train

From hours down to seconds

The barrel drives the centre wheel, which turns once an hour and carries the minute hand. The third wheel steps the speed up again; the fourth wheel turns once a minute, and the small seconds at six o’clock sits on its arbor.

4 of 5Escapement

Letting go five times a second

The escape wheel has 15 teeth. The pallet fork rocks across it, its two ruby pallets locking and releasing one tooth at a time, and each release gives the balance a small push. That release is the tick: 18,000 of them an hour.

5 of 5Balance and hairspring

The part that keeps time

An 11 mm screwed balance swings two and a half times a second on a Breguet overcoil hairspring. A swan-neck spring under the cock lets us move the regulator by hundredths of a millimetre.

Back together, it runs.

Regulated in five positions, by hand, for three weeks

Every R.30 sits on the timegrapher for three weeks before it is cased. These are the readings for no. 13, signed off on 9 October 2026. Our tolerance is 0 to +6 seconds a day in every position.

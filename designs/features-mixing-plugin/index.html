A 1974 broadcast desk, one strip on every track.

Brack 2408 is a mixing-console plugin modelled on the Lindqvist B-24, a broadcast desk we spent two years restoring. Put it on every channel and your mix runs through the same preamp, EQ, dynamics and bus. Play the loop and switch stages to hear each one.

Play the loop and switch stages to hear each one. A 92 BPM loop made in your browser. It starts quietly.

Below the meter, a drawing of the strip's four stages mirrors the controls in the tabs: the transformer, air and ratio knobs turn and the drift lamp lights when you change those settings. The other knobs are fixed at the factory preset.

Push the input until it thickens

Three input transformers from the original desk's spares drawer. Nickel stays fast and clean, steel thickens the low mids, and iron adds the most harmonics when you drive it. Peaks are level-matched as you turn, so drive shows up as density on the meter, not as loudness.

Transformer

0.8% THD with iron at +12 dB drive.

Open up the top without the hiss

Four bands with proportional Q, so boosts stay broad and musical and deep cuts get surgical without touching a Q control. Frequencies click through the same 11 steps as the original's switched pots, so a setting is easy to repeat.

+2.0 dB

Low shelf 35–440 Hz, low mid 120 Hz–2 kHz, high mid 600 Hz–8 kHz, high shelf 4.5–16 kHz.

A compressor and a gate on every channel

Feed-forward VCA compression with four ratios and a sidechain filter at 60, 120 or 240 Hz, so the kick stops pumping the whole bass. The gate has a 40 dB range and a hold you can set by ear.

Ratio

Attack 0.1–30 ms, release 50 ms–1.2 s or auto.

Sum the channels the way the desk did

Every channel carries the tolerances we measured on the restored desk, with −62 dB of crosstalk between neighbours. Turn drift off and all 24 strips match exactly. Turn it on and the stereo image widens slightly, because no two channels sum quite the same.

Channel drift

Bus compressor at 2:1, 4:1 or 10:1, attack 0.1–30 ms.

## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the What's on menu shows how many people each concert puts on the platform.** Next to the eight featured concerts is the stage manager's plan of the hall: the lit platform floor, three riser bands, the podium, the organ console and five rows of choir seats behind it. Every chair is always drawn as a faint outline, 216 in all, and one pool of 168 player dots moves between them, each dot keeping its identity. Pointing at, focusing or tapping a concert reseats the platform: players who stay keep their section and walk from their old chair to that section's matching front-desk chair in the new concert (0.7s expo.out, staggered outward from the podium by distance, at most 0.3s); players who are not needed walk off through the stage door in the left wing (0.5s power2.in, then fade); new players come in from that door, and singers fill or leave the choir seats from the ends of their rows, front row centre out. When the wings run short, a leaving player turns round at the door and comes back on as a new one. The Bryza Quartet is four dots round the podium; Schubert one at the piano; Mahler 6 fills the platform and leaves the choir dark; the Brahms Requiem uses all 168 (76 players, 90 singers, two soloists). You see the ensemble's size, and how it differs from the last one, before you read a word. The first time the panel opens (not on a switch from Visit, and not on a reopen within 2s) the active concert walks on from the wings, 0.6s with the stagger capped at 0.35s, starting 0.12s into the panel fade, so nobody meets the menu as a static diagram. Everything else on the page stays quiet so this can be the moment.

- **The plan reads as a stage manager's plan.** The strings sit on five shared concentric rows round the podium (radius 56 to 128 in steps of 18), with one 16-unit chair spacing on every row and a fixed angular span per section (first violins -90° to -48°, seconds -48° to -6°, violas 6° to 48°, cellos 48° to 90°), so the rows run unbroken across section boundaries. The basses stand on an outer row behind the cellos, the harps at the outer end of the first-violin row; woodwind and brass sit in straight rows on the risers.
- **The plan is honest data.** The grid is one `SECTIONS` list (each section's chairs in front-desk-first order) and each concert in `PLATFORM` says how many chairs of each section it uses, with its own label where the music needs one ("Flute, the bird", "Viole da gamba and cello"). Every count in the caption is computed from that data. The Requiem uses Brahms's own wind (two oboes, two clarinets, two trumpets; flutes with piccolo and bassoons with contrabassoon), and the legend lists the harps on their own line, so "Strings 58" for Mahler counts bowed strings only. `#selftest` asserts the totals (108, 4, 19, 18, 35, 1, 168, 1), that the pool holds 168 players, that the quartet seats four and the Requiem 168 (92 of them voices), that the leader of the first violins keeps her chair from Mahler to the Requiem, that every string chair is on one of the five shared rows, that no section or family asks for more chairs than the grid has, that no two chairs are closer than 10 units, that the quartet sits at the four front desks, that every row points at a concert in the list and every in-page link and `aria-controls` target exists, and the choir-seat rule in the form.
- **216 chairs, 168 players.** The chairs are positions; the players are a separate pool. The grid holds the largest need of every section (Mahler's 108 on the platform, the Requiem's 90 in a 100-seat choir block, four soloist chairs), so it has 216 chairs, while the pool has exactly 168 dots, the Requiem's full strength. The empty outlines keep the comparison between concerts: you see what each one leaves empty.
- **Nothing is hover-only.** The caption under the plan carries the date, the title, a sentence and a legend with every family's count (strings filled ink, woodwind ink rings, brass filled velvet, percussion and keyboards velvet rings, voices filled grey-violet). Keyboard focus on a row reseats the plan and announces the caption (`aria-live` is polite only for focus and tap, off for pointer sweeps). On touch and on any `(hover: none)` device each row has a Platform button with `aria-pressed`, and tapping the drawing shows the section label. The SVG is `aria-hidden` because the caption says everything it shows.
- **Fine pointers get a readout.** Over the drawing, the section nearest the pointer stays lit, the others drop to 45% fill and stroke opacity, and a small ink label ("Second violins, 14", "Nabrzeże Chorus, 90") follows the pointer 14px right and 28px above it through `gsap.quickTo` (0.25s), clamped inside the frame. The native cursor stays. (An earlier build anchored the label above the section; the brief's pointer-following label won.)
- **A plan that keeps up with the pointer.** Sweeping down the list would restart the reseat eight times, so a row becomes active only after 70ms of intent. Each player owns one tween; a new concert kills it and retargets from wherever the dot is, so the platform never queues behind an old walk. Players already off stage are reused first. The list, the plan and the caption change together from one `setActive(i)`. Closing the panel mid-entrance jumps the plan to its seated state.
- **The menus are calm.** Both mega panels share one fixed height (600px) and one backing layer, so switching from What's on to Visit crossfades content and nothing animates height. Hover opens after 80ms, or instantly if the other panel is open; leaving trigger and panel starts a 140ms close that is cancelled if the pointer comes back; an invisible bridge covers the ruler. Click, Enter and Space toggle; ArrowDown opens and focuses the first row; Escape closes and returns focus; an outside click, the scrim or focus moving to something outside closes (a tap on the drawing, which focuses nothing, does not). The scrim is ink at 55%: the page is the lit foyer and opening a menu turns the house lights down; the scrollbar gutter takes the same colour while it is open. From 1200px the list and the plan share the panel 5 to 7 with a 64px gap, the plan up to 680px wide; on short screens the plan shrinks so it and its caption fit without the panel scrolling. Lenis stops on every open path and starts on every close path, with `html.menu-open` as the overflow fallback when Lenis is off. Menus are disclosure buttons and panels, not `role="menu"`.
- **The season ruler is the header's one moving mark.** Under the nav, a hairline runs from September to June with a tick on the first of each month, the months as links, and a velvet dot on the month you are reading. When the month at the reading line (30% down the viewport) changes, the dot moves to it in 0.4s; it does not scrub. That month gets `aria-current` and turns ink. Below 992px the months do not fit, so the ruler shows ticks and one label, the current month, riding with the dot and swapping under a mask. The current section in the main nav is a static 2px velvet underline set by the section spy; there is no sliding indicator.
- **Hide-on-scroll with guards.** The header bar slides up by its own height while reading down past 120px and returns on the first scroll up; the season ruler stays pinned with its backdrop, so the marker is seen moving while you read the season. It never hides while a menu is open, while keyboard focus is inside it, or during an anchor glide and the 600ms after it, and it comes back as soon as anything in it gets focus (a fixed header never scrolls into view on its own). `scroll-padding-top` matches the header, so Shift+Tab never leaves focus under it; in the phone sheet the scroll padding matches the sticky plan. At the very top its backdrop disappears; elsewhere it is plaster at 94% with a 10px blur.
- **The form knows the hall.** Choir seats cost 39 zł except when the chorus sits in them. Choosing Messiah, the Requiem or the Ninth disables the Choir seats radio with the reason in text, moves a choir selection to the circle and updates the total. The seat count and the total ("2 circle seats, 220 zł") roll in place; a Reserve link in the season list preselects its concert and lands focus on the select, whose velvet rule draws in. Errors appear under each field with `aria-invalid` and a velvet field rule, the field is described by its error only while invalid, and focus moves to the first one. The stepper buttons use `aria-disabled` at 1 and 8, so a focused button keeps focus. The confirmation wipes up over the form, focus moves to "Reserved", and "Reserve more seats" brings the form back with its values. Nothing is sent. There is no phone link (no fictional Polish number range exists); the box office is reached by email and its hours.
- **Type from a Polish programme book.** Brygada 1918 is a revival of a Polish typeface from 1918, and it sets the hall's own name, Sala Nabrzeże, with its native ż. Composers in 600, works in 400, italics only for named works (*A German Requiem*, *The Rite of Spring*), never for generic titles (Symphony No. 6). Afacad is narrow and plain enough to work as the listings register: dates are 600 with tabular figures, and in headless Chromium "1111" and "0000" measure the same width, so the date column aligns without a monospace face. No other design in the collection uses Afacad.
- **Palette checked.** Plaster `#E4E8E8` (cool, not cream), sheet `#F7F8F7`, ink `#24203A`, programme `#5A5670`, velvet `#A64B62`. Contrast: ink on plaster 12.67 and on sheet 14.69; programme on plaster 5.67 and on sheet 6.58; white on velvet 5.52 and on the hover velvet 7.03. Velvet on plaster is 4.47, so velvet is never text: it is the button fill, the row bar, the ruler marker, the nav underline, brass marks and invalid-field rules. Sibling dimming in the menus is done with colour (programme), not opacity, so dimmed rows stay AA.
- **Cut at critique:** the earlier build's night palette and font, the scrubbed ruler, the hero line split and tilted nav labels, the fill-from-edge button hover and masked Menu/Close swap, and last, a "Reserve seats" link in the What's on panel footer that repeated the header button sitting just above the open panel.

## Motion

Detailed tier: GSAP 3.15 with ScrollTrigger, and Lenis 1.3.26 on GSAP's ticker (`autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`, `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`), all inside one `gsap.matchMedia` with `motion`, `reduce`, `fine` and `wide` conditions and a cleanup that removes the ticker, restores lag smoothing and destroys Lenis. Menus, the plan (players set in place), the spy, the ruler, anchors and the form are plain JavaScript and work if the CDN fails; GSAP only animates. Hidden start states exist only under `html.js:not(.loaded)`, and `js` is added only when the libraries loaded. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every CSS transition is removed, Lenis is not created, scrolling is native, and every state below is set instantly.

## Do not touch

- One fixed grid of chairs, always drawn, and one pool of 168 players that keeps its identity. Moving chairs, drawing a different plan per concert, or replacing the walk with a cross-fade loses the comparison that is the whole idea.
- Players stay in their section between concerts (matched by section, k-th chair to k-th chair); matching across sections would send violinists to the trombones.
- Players move by transform only, and each owns one tween that the next change kills.
- `setActive(i)` is the only way the active concert changes, with the 70ms intent. Driving the plan from hover and the caption from focus separately makes them disagree.
- The caption must keep every family count and the legend. Without it the plan becomes hover-only content.
- Both panels keep one fixed height and one backing layer. Animating height between them causes layout thrash and a jumping scrim edge.
- `lock()` is the only place that stops and starts Lenis; every open and close path goes through it.
- The reading line at 30%: moving it makes the ruler mark the wrong month after a jump.
- Dimming by colour, not opacity, in both panels; velvet never as text.
- The choir-seats rule for choral concerts, and the `#selftest` assertions that cover it.
- Hidden start states only under `html.js:not(.loaded)`.

## Adapting

Change the `:root` tokens first. `--plaster` is the page and the plan sheet, `--sheet` every raised surface and the lit platform floor; keep them one step apart in the same cool hue. `--ink` and `--programme` must reach 4.5:1 on both, because dimmed rows use `--programme`. `--velvet` is the only colour: keep white text on it at 4.5:1 and keep it off text.

For another venue, redraw the static plan in the SVG (floor, risers, podium, organ console) in the same 640 x 400 units, then rewrite `SECTIONS` (one entry per section, chairs in front-desk-first order; curved sections come from the shared rows and `SPAN`, straight ones from `row()`) and `PLATFORM` (one entry per featured row, in row order, with `n` and `say` matching the chairs it uses). Size each section for the largest concert that needs it. Run `#selftest`; it fails on overlapping chairs, wrong totals, a section asked for more chairs than it has, or a row pointing at a missing concert. A theatre can use the same mechanics with a cast on a stage plan; a dance company with dancers on a marked floor. Keep eight or so rows; titles longer than about 34 characters wrap below about 1060px.

Copy: dates as "Thu 12 Nov" in the menu and "Thu 12 Nov, 19:30" in the list; composer, then work, with italics only for named works. The form's prices live in `PRICE`, and choral concerts carry `data-chorus` on their option.

Retiming: every CSS transition uses the duration tokens (`--d-press` 0.12s to `--d3` 0.6s) and easing tokens (`--ease-out`, `--ease-wipe`, `--ease-fade`, `--ease-sweep`, `--ease-roll`); the walk, entrance, caption, readout, ruler and load timings are in the script (`reseat`, `enterPlan`, `setCaption`, `readAt` and the `quickTo` pair in `mm.add`, `readMonth`, the load timeline inside `mm.add`). To drop the walk to a plain swap, call `reseat(c, 'set')` from `setActive`; to drop the entrance, remove the `enterPlan` calls. To move the stage door, change `DOOR` and the door path in the SVG together. To drop the load sequence, delete that timeline and add `loaded` to `<html>` straight away. To drop the month-rule draw-in, delete the `months.forEach` block in `mm.add`. To drop smooth scrolling, remove Lenis from the `mm.add` branch; anchors fall back to native smooth scrolling. To remove motion entirely, remove the library tags: everything still works, instantly.

From survey pegs to raising day

We build green-oak frames for houses in the Welsh Marches. This is what happens between your first call and the morning your frame stands up.

Most frames take 15 to 23 weeks from survey to raising.

Surveys within 40 miles of Hereford are free.

  1. Survey the site

    We walk the plot with you, take levels, note where the sun and the weather come from, and dig two trial holes so the engineer knows what the ground will carry.

    1 day on site.

  2. Draw the frame

    We draw every bent, plate and brace at 1:20, then each joint at full size. You see the frame from every side before anything is cut, and you can still move a window.

    4 to 6 weeks.

  3. Choose the oak

    We go to the sawmill at Leominster and pick each beam ourselves: straight grain for posts, a natural curve for the braces. Every piece is tagged with its place on the drawings.

    About 2 weeks.

  4. Cut the joints

    Carpenters lay out each mortise and tenon from the drawings and cut them by hand and machine. Peg holes are bored slightly offset so the oak pegs draw every joint tight.

    8 to 12 weeks.

  5. Test-fit in the yard

    We lay each bent out flat, check it square, then cut the same numeral into both halves of every joint, like the ones down this post. On raising day the crew matches them to find each piece.

    1 to 2 weeks.

  6. Raise the frame

    A crane lifts each bent upright, the crew pegs in the plates and rafters, and by the last evening the whole house stands. We nail a sprig of green to the ridge, and you are invited.

    1 to 3 days.

Keys that ride on magnets

Every key on a Nordpol board carries a small magnet over a Hall sensor. Nothing touches, so nothing wears, and the board knows how far down each key is, to a tenth of a millimetre. We build two boards for old computers and arcade play, forty at a time.

    Set the press point per key

    0.2–3.8 mm, in 0.1 mm steps

    The sensor reads the magnet's height 2,000 times a second, so a key can fire a hair below the top or only at the bottom. Light fingers on the arrows, a deep press on Return.

    Rapid trigger on the pad

    0.1 mm to reset

    A button lets go the moment it rises a tenth of a millimetre, not at a fixed point. Two quick taps on the Lodestone Pad are two inputs, even if your thumb never lifts off.

    Reset and scan rate on the spec sheet
  • NS

    Nothing inside to wear out

    80,000,000 presses

    No metal leaf touches another; a magnet only passes a sensor. The test rig on our bench has pressed one key eighty million times and it still reads the same height.

    Bench test on the spec sheet
  • NS

    Speaks to old machines

    6 adapter boards

    The Needle 64 drives the keyboard matrix of an 8-bit home computer directly through a swap-in adapter board and ribbon cable. Unplug the adapter and it is a USB-C keyboard for anything newer.

    Ports and adapters on the spec sheet
  • NS

    Clean opposites

    2 modes, no software

    When left and right are held together on the pad, you decide what the game sees: the last one pressed, or neither. Hold Menu and tap a direction to switch; the pad remembers.

    5 years of repairs

    Switches pull out by hand and every magnet comes out with a pin. Schematics and firmware are published. Send a board back within five years and we fix it; you pay the postage one way.

## Why it works

**The one bold idea: you meet him by finishing his room.** The first screen is his tile editor with a level he has started: 40 by 18 tiles of 6 art pixels each, a spawn, two keys, a ladder to a mossy ledge, two pools and a door nobody can reach yet. The pointer aims the brush: hovering moves a cell cursor, and clicking or dragging with the button held lays the chosen tile along the whole path between two pointer samples (a Bresenham line, so a fast hand leaves no gaps), and the tiles stay. Solid ground is auto-tiled: each solid tile draws a bright edge on every side that is exposed and a bright pixel in every inner corner, so a painted block joins the ground it touches as if he had drawn it. A palette of six tile buttons (solid, ladder, water, moss, key, eraser) picks the brush, "Reset the room" restores his layout, and a readout counts what is in the room. The headline says the first room teaches the whole game; the page's first room teaches what a level designer does, before the lede is read.

Everything else on the page is made of the same parts, drawn by the same `tile()` function: the dungeon's props, the radio swatches, the brush icons, the layers panel's collision layer and the blocks of his name.

- **Ten tile hues, ten jobs, plus one for faults.** The ground is vermilion (`--ground` #B8300F), with a darker well (`--well`) for everything tiles are laid on (every canvas, fields, cards, the dialog, the sign-off band), a lighter plate (`--plate`) for rules between rows, rooms not reached yet and the dialog's tile backs, and a deep tone (`--deep`) for ink on bright faces, hovered plates, the lighting layer's shadow and the scrim. Text is one neutral (`--white`), with `--sand` for secondary text, rims, dotted underlines and dust. The hues: yellow `--spawn` is the player (the start tile, the marker that walks and hops), the primary action and focus; cream `--solid`, cyan `--ladder`, blue `--water`, green `--moss` and pink `--key` are the five editable tile types and chapters 1 to 5, the same five in the editor, the rooms, the chapter tags, the swatches and the letters of the name; black-red `--spike` with a sand edge is hazards in the chart and the room; orange `--coin` is collectibles (in the chart, the finished room and over the confirmation); violet `--door` is doors and exits (in the editor, the rooms, the chart, the flag at the exit) and the camera on the minimap; mint `--light` is the lighting layer and nothing else; pale pink `--fault` is form errors, always with a hatched bar. Large areas stay on the ground and its three tones, and a hue that falls under 3:1 on the ground (`--water`, `--key`, `--door`, `--spike`) is only ever drawn on a well.
- **One typeface as a voice.** Departure Mono on its 11px grid sets every size: 13.75px for labels, captions, table heads and hints only, 16.5px for every running paragraph (the chapter cards keep 16.5px on phones too), 22px for the lede and the chapter and rule titles, 33px for every section heading, and the headline at 33, 55 or 66px by breakpoint (44px on one line beside the editor when a window of 1280 or wider is under 760px tall). Line heights are multiples of 5.5px. There is no bold: emphasis is colour, inversion (the pressed lock button, the held Solo), a selection frame or position. The character cell is the layout unit: `--ch` (10.5px) spaces the link dots one per character, and term columns, rule numbers and the minimap's labels sit on cells. There are no prompts, no logs and no ASCII art.
- **The structure is a level.** Top: the editor. Now: three lines in a `dl`. The story so far: five rooms walked by scroll. By the numbers: the difficulty curve of three shipped games. A toy: one finished room in its four layers, so the visitor can strip it back to the grey box they painted at the top. Six rules I work by: disclosures whose links point back into the page. Say hello: links, a form that ends with "Level request logged." and a flag at the exit. Sign-off: his name built from the five tile types.
- **The camera.** A minimap of the page (seven blocks for the seven parts, fixed at the right edge from 1024px, a strip of seven along the bottom on phones and tablets) carries a hollow `--door` rectangle that cuts from block to block as the part under 40% of the window's height changes. The blocks are real links with `aria-current`, so the marker is also the page's navigation, and it works without the libraries.
- **Data is in two constants.** `DATA` holds three games, each with twelve levels of median attempts, hazards, collectibles and exits, a year, a kind and a one-line note; the tabs, the stepped line, the three marker rows, the readout and the table are built from it. `CONTACT` holds the links (email, Mastodon, itch.io, GitHub and a talk on Vimeo here); delete a line to remove one, and any other key with a full URL becomes a link labelled with the key.
- **Flows.** "Hire me for a level" goes to the form. "Read the short bio" (and the text button beside the contact links) opens a native dialog, closable by Close, Escape or a press that starts and ends on the backdrop, with focus returned to the opener and Lenis stopped while it is open; it caps its height and scrolls inside itself on short windows. The form validates in JavaScript on submit with messages that say how to fix the problem (name, email with an example, a kind of game, an optional whole number of levels from 1 to 99, a message of 20 to 1,200 characters that says how many more characters it needs), reserves its error lines so nothing shifts, moves focus to the first bad field and clears an error as soon as the value is valid. Nothing is sent. The confirmation echoes the name, address, kind, levels, build and message, takes focus on its heading, and "Change this" brings the form back with everything kept.
- **Semantics.** One `section` labelled by the `h2`; part headings are `h3`s, chapter titles `h4`s; the minimap is a `nav` with an ordered list; Now and the echoes are `dl`s; the chapters and rules are ordered lists, the rules are `details`. The editor canvas is focusable (`role="application"`) with its help and its live count as its description, and a polite live region reports every keyboard move and placement. The dungeon and the wordmark are `aria-hidden` (the chapters are real text, and the name is in the line below it); the layers room is a `role="img"` with a full description and a live readout of what is shown. The chart's levels are buttons with roving tabindex inside a labelled group, the games are radios in a `fieldset`, and there is a readout plus "Read the figures as a table". The kind of game is four radios in a `fieldset`; the build is a native checkbox drawn as a lever.
- **What is different from its nearest relatives.** cta-indie-game lights cells in a wake that fades; here nothing fades, the pointer builds, and the tiles know their neighbours. crud-cartridge-stall packs tiles first-fit into a crate; here the visitor places every tile. nav-game-museum walks a marker to the room you point at, over a floor plan that is always drawn; here scroll is the walk, the route is fixed, and rooms are revealed from outlines as the marker reaches their doors. story-speedrun-marathon scrolls a side-on run; this is top-down. features-cyberdeck-kit assembles a device from falling blocks; here the blocks spell a name, fall column by column per letter, and can be knocked.

Deviations from the brief, and why:

- **The press squash is 4px, not 2px.** The face of the primary button is drawn 4px shorter and 4px wider while pressed (scale computed from its size), because 2px did not read in a frame at a glance.
- **Tuned hexes.** `--solid` #FFE8BD (brief #FFF1D6), `--water` #86A8FF (brief #4D7CFF, which was 2.3:1 on the well), `--spike` #450C00 (brief #3A0A00), `--sand` #FFDCC6 (brief about #FFD3B8) and `--fault` #FFD9E2, to reach the ratios in the token comments. The jobs are the brief's.
- **The curve's height follows the window.** On desktop the plot is 264px under 700px of window height and grows in 64px steps (320, 384, 448, 512px) with the height, so the chart fills its screen at 1440 by 900 and still fits at 1366 by 650 and 1280 by 600.
- **The walked dots turn yellow.** On a breadcrumb link the dots the marker has already passed take the player's hue, so the hop reads as a route travelled, not a block flickering.
- **On narrow windows the dungeon is a camera view.** Below the width that holds 24 columns at scale 2 the map shows the part of the dungeon around the marker, moving in whole tiles. Under 540px of height, or when the map and one chapter card cannot fit the window, the scene is not pinned and the whole map is shown with all five cards.

## Do not touch

- **Whole-number scales.** The editor is 6 art pixels per tile, shown at 4, 3 or 2 CSS pixels per art pixel (editor column at least 840, 500 or less); the dungeon (258 by 108) at 5 to 2; the layers room (160 by 96) at 4 to 2; all with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Free scaling blurs every edge and breaks the auto-tile edges into smears.
- **One tile function.** `tile(g, t, x, y, T, n)` draws every tile on the page from its type and its eight neighbours. The editor, the dungeon props, the collision layer, the brush icons and the radio swatches all call it, so a change to a tile changes it everywhere. Keep the neighbour order `[N, E, S, W, NE, SE, SW, NW]`.
- **Painted tiles stay.** `cells` only changes by painting, the keyboard or Reset. Do not add decay or a timer; the building is the point. The spawn tile and the door's two tiles are fixed and cannot be painted.
- **The route is computed once.** `bfs()` runs leg by leg through `STOPS` at start-up and gives 84 steps; `doorStep` is where each door sits on it. A door shows its half-open frame on the step before it and its open frame from its step on, and the room behind it cuts from outline to drawing at that step. Move a stop and every threshold moves with it; move a door and keep it on a leg.
- **The scroll budget.** The walk is `N x --step-px` (84 x 30px = 2,520px on desktop, 84 x 22px = 1,848px below 1024) plus one window height, and the last 4% of the walk holds the final room. `fitPin()` decides the scale and whether the card sits under the map or beside it (beside only from 1100px and when the whole map fits); if you add anything to the pinned frame, measure it there.
- **Hues keep their jobs.** `--spawn` only for the player, the primary action and focus; the five tile hues only for their tile type and chapter number; `--spike`, `--coin` and `--door` only for hazards, collectibles and exits (and `--door` for the camera); `--light` only in the lighting layer; `--fault` only for errors, always with the hatch. Text is `--white` or `--sand`. A hue under 3:1 on the ground is drawn on a well.
- **Errors are hatch plus words.** The ground is red, so a red error would vanish. Invalid fields get a `--fault` border, a 6px 135-degree hatched bar under the field (the kind of game gets one under its swatches) and the message in the reserved line below. Keep all three.
- **Stepped corners live on `.face` and `.inner`, never on the `a` or `button`,** so the 2px yellow focus outline is never clipped. The key, the lock and the dust are inside the control or absolutely placed beside it, so the outline still wraps the face.
- **No loops.** Nothing on the page animates on its own. The editor redraws once per animation frame only after a change; the dungeon only when the step changes; the layers room only on a dither step; the wordmark only on scroll, a knock or the rebuild. The only ticker function is Lenis.
- **Hidden states exist only under `html.js:not(.go)`** (the copy marked `.cut`, the headline and the editor canvas) and are undone under reduced motion. With the CDN blocked the `js` class is never added: every canvas shows a composed still (the full room, the whole dungeon with every room drawn, the lit room, the built name), and the editor, chart, toy, form, dialog and minimap all work.
- **Press holds.** Every `.btn`, `.u`, brush, eye, chart column and minimap link keeps `.is-down` for at least `--d-press` (0.14s) from `pointerdown` or Space/Enter, and Solo holds for at least `--d-solo` (0.2s), so a quick tap still shows the press.

## Motion

House rule: hard cuts on an 80 ms frame (`--frame`). Nothing eases. GSAP is used for timing (`timeline().call()` at fixed times, one linear tween for the rebuild), ScrollTrigger for the walk and the drop, and Lenis for wheel smoothing on `gsap.ticker` (`lerp: 0.1`, `autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`, `anchors: true`, `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`; touch keeps native scrolling). CSS keyframes and transitions use `steps()`. The load sequence waits for `document.fonts.ready` plus `document.fonts.load('16px "Departure Mono"')` raced with 1.5s, and for a visible page; if the timeout wins, the layout is refitted when the font arrives. It is skipped if the page opens more than 120px down. Only transform, visibility, clip-path and canvas pixels change over time; colours switch in one cut. Everything is built inside `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, fine pointer, height 540, width 1024), and its cleanup removes listeners, timeouts, ScrollTriggers, the rebuild tween and Lenis, and restores the stills.

## Adapting

1. **Find the editor.** The page works when the person's craft can be tried in a grid: tiles, stitches, glyph pixels, circuit pads. Keep the editor's contract (`fit()`, `scroll(n)`, `vis`) and replace `LEVEL` (run-length rows: `#` solid, `H` ladder, `~` water, `=` moss, `k` key, `.` empty; the digits after a character repeat it), `SPAWN`, `DOOR`, `NAMES` and the tile drawings in `tile()`. Keep the brush count at five types plus the eraser, or change the `1` to `6` key range with it.
2. **Map the colours, keeping the jobs.** Change `--ground`, then derive `--well` (darker), `--plate` (lighter) and `--deep` (darkest) from the same hue, and recheck `--white` and `--sand` on all of them. Keep one hue for the player, the primary action and focus, five for the tile types and chapters, three for the chart's categories, one for the toy's special layer and one pale tone for errors, and recompute every ratio in the `:root` comments. Any hue under 3:1 on the ground must only be drawn on a well. The canvases read every colour from the tokens at start-up.
3. **Replace the copy.** The headline is about 40 characters: two lines at 66px across the top, or one line at 44px on short wide windows. Now takes three `dt`/`dd` pairs. The story takes exactly five chapters of about 230 characters (five rooms, five hues). The rules take any number of rows with an optional in-page link each. The bio dialog takes a paragraph and four rows.
4. **Replace the dungeon.** Edit `ROOMS`, `DOORS`, `HALLS`, `PROPS` and `STOPS` in the dungeon module (map 43 by 18 tiles). Each door must lie on a leg between two stops, and each hall must end in an opening of the next room's wall. The route, its length and every door threshold are recomputed from them.
5. **Replace the data.** Edit `DATA.games` (any number of games, each with equal-length arrays) and `DATA.max` (the top of the scale; the grid has four steps). Edit `CONTACT`. The layers room is `ROOM`, `LAMPS` and the four `mk()` drawings.
6. **Replace the name.** The five letters and their 5x7 glyphs are in `GLYPH`, the accent rows in the letter loop, and the grid is `letters x 6 - 1` columns wide (29 here), so a longer name gets smaller blocks; above seven letters, use two rows.
7. **Retime or remove.** Every duration is a `--d-*` token read by CSS or by the script. To remove the pin, delete the `layout()` call and the `.pin` rules: the static map and the five cards are already complete. To drop the load sequence, add `go` to the root at once and skip the timeline. To keep the name still, delete the `stW` ScrollTrigger and call `word.draw(1)`.
8. **In a framework,** render the markup statically, run the plain-JS half of the script once on mount, and return the `gsap.matchMedia` cleanup from the effect.

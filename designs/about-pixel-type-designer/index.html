The story so far

Five drafts of one letter, a lowercase g, one for each stretch of my working life. Each draft replaces the last, and the earlier ones stay behind it as outlines in their own colour.

  1. Chapter 1, 2011 to 2014

    Copying what I saw

    I redrew shop signs on graph paper and the letters on my calculator's display, at an evening class in lettering. I learned that a pixel is on or off: there is no half.

    The g: two storeys copied from print, one pixel thick. At 14 cells its lower loop filled in.

  2. Chapter 2, 2014 to 2017

    Menus for a handheld game studio

    I set menus and dialogue for Lanternfish, a four-person studio making handheld games, 8 pixels tall. I learned that at small sizes the simplest structure reads first.

    The g: one storey and an open hook. Readable, but thin enough to vanish on a dim screen.

  3. Chapter 3, 2017 to 2020

    Departure boards

    I drew the type for Velto, who make bus and tram displays read from 30 metres. I learned that light bleeds, so every stroke went to two pixels.

    The g: two-cell stems and two-row bars. It survived the glare, and its counter closed up.

  4. Chapter 4, 2020 to 2023

    On my own

    I opened my studio in Ghent and released Lieve 7 and Kouter 9. I learned that tight line spacing is paid for by the descenders.

    The g: the bowl lifted and the tail cut to two rows, so lines could sit closer. People read it as a q.

  5. Chapter 5, 2023 to now

    Schelde 14

    My largest family so far: 14 pixels, 640 glyphs, every Latin accent and the full box-drawing set. I learned to give each part its own row.

    The g: two-cell stems, one-row bars, the bowl on the baseline and a full three-row tail.

Things I believe

Six rules I draw by at small sizes.

  1. Draw at the size it will be read. A 9-pixel e is not a shrunken 90-pixel e.

    Edit the e at the top

  2. Give each part its own row. When two parts share a row, one of them loses.

    See how the g got there

  3. Accents are letters. I draw them with the base letter, not after it.

    Count the accents I drew

  4. A missing glyph should look missing. A hollow box is honest; a fallback typeface is not.

    Type a character I haven't drawn

  5. Spacing is half the font. I space the n and the o before I draw anything else.

    Read the spacing tables on GitHub (opens in a new tab)

  6. Ship the box-drawing set. Someone will draw a table with it on the first day.

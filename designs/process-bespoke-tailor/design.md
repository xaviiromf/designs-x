## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the timeline is a tape measure graduated in weeks, and scrolling slides it under the cutter's needle at true scale.** `#fittings` pins for exactly the tape's length. A yellow tape runs along the bottom of the screen from 0 "First measure" to 10 "Collection", with a tick every half week and an eighth-week scale on both edges. One week is a fixed width (`--week`: 32vw on desktop, 48vw on tablets, 72vw on phones). As you scroll down, the tape and the four panels on it slide left under a fixed needle of tailor's blue chalk, with the reading ("Week 4½") written beside it in white chalk. The panels sit at weeks 1, 3, 6 and 9, centred on their week mark, so the empty stretch between the first and second fittings is three weeks of tape going by. As each panel's week reaches the needle, its coat drawing (the largest object on screen) is basted: white tacking stitches draw along the seams. That is the skeleton baste, the forward fitting and the chalked buttonholes, which are the real stages of a bespoke coat. Over the last week the finished chalk-stripe cloth, framed with its left edge at the tape's brass end clip, opens to the whole screen, so week 10 lands on the finished suit and the pin releases straight into `#collection`, which is the same cloth.

- **Why a horizontal pin after all.** An earlier pass cut this pin because a horizontal tape under a fixed reader was features-coffee-roaster's signature. That design has since cut its pin (its row is a native scroll-snap row) and story-cargo-sailing cut its horizontal track, so the technique is free, and it is the version of the idea where waiting reads as tape going by rather than as an empty page.
- **The tape is also navigation.** The fitting numerals 1, 3, 6 and 9 are real buttons ("Go to week 6, second fitting"). They scroll until that week mark sits exactly on the needle, then focus the panel's heading. 0 links back to the measure book and 10 to collection. Tabbing to a numeral also brings its week to the needle, so a keyboard user always sees what has focus. "Skip the fittings timeline" sits beside the heading, before the pin. The pin length equals the tape length, and Space, Page Down and the arrow keys keep working through Lenis.
- **It degrades to a vertical tape.** With reduced motion, without the libraries, or when a panel's week line or a readable coat (140px tall) would not fit above the tape (short landscape windows, a 360x640 phone), the same tape hangs down the left of the section past a sticky needle at 45% of the viewport, the panels hang off it by a drawn dressmaker's pin, and the page scrolls natively. There the empty weeks are plain reading.
- **Copy counts correctly.** The H1 promises three fittings. The lead says five visits in total (first measure, three fittings, collection), the H2 says "Three fittings in ten weeks", and week 1 says "No visit." because the cutter drafts the pattern without you.
- **Materials as palette, with each material in its one role.** Flannel `#4F5150` is the ground, with a twill texture under 4% contrast. Basting white `#F8F6EF` is text and every mark on cloth (7.4:1). Manila `#D9C49B` appears only on card objects: the hanging pattern pieces, the measure book, the week-1 pattern card and the dialog. Tailor's black `#232326` is ink on card (9.2:1) and on the tape (9.8:1). Tape yellow `#F0C93A` is the tape and the keyboard focus ring on cloth (5.0:1), and nothing else. Tailor's blue chalk `#3A63A8` is the needle line and its wedge only; it never carries text (3.7:1 on the tape). Brass `#B08A3E` is the tape's two end clips. The cutter's red pencil `#B0302A` marks the low right shoulder on the forepart ("−½") and form errors. It only reaches 3.7:1 on card, so it is never the text colour: errors are black text beside a red pencil stroke and a red underline.
- **Why grey and not manila.** The first plan put the whole page on manila card (`#D4BE94`). A warm tan page under a serif is the collection's first default to avoid, and that tan also sat beside features-coffee-roaster's sack tan. So the ground is the suit cloth, mid-grey worsted flannel, and manila is kept to the paper objects that really are card. The mode is dark because the cloth is.
- **The hero is the object.** Mr R. Ahmadi's paper pattern hangs on a hook in three manila pieces (back, top sleeve, forepart). Each piece carries pencil grain lines, notches and darts, and the forepart carries his name, "Cut 2. Page 211" and the red alteration. The pieces are fanned so the sleeve head and the back neck show above the forepart. On load each settles once about its punched hole (no fade, no pendulum) and then hangs still. "Page 211" is the measure book page you meet next, and the collection copy brings the pattern back ("Your pattern stays on its hook").
- **Measurements are shown where they are taken.** The measure book (eight of twenty-six measurements plus two posture notes) is a list of `aria-pressed` buttons. Values are in Sorts Mill Goudy italic, the hand-written-in voice, set `lining-nums` and right-aligned. Checked in Chromium: Goudy has `lnum` but no `tnum`, and its lining figures are all one width ("1111", "0000" and "6789" each measure 101.2px at 48px upright, 97.5px italic), so the column aligns. Its default old-style figures stay in running text, where they belong. Choosing a row draws its white chalk line on a grey dress form, and a live caption says where and how it is taken. Chest is chosen by default, so the figure is never blank and nothing depends on hover.
- **Type.** Sorts Mill Goudy, a revival of Goudy Old Style, the face of early-twentieth-century outfitters' and tailors' shop cards, carries everything in one weight. Hierarchy comes from size alone on a perfect-fourth scale from 1.125rem: H1 at 1.02 leading and -0.012em tracking, body at 1.6. `font-synthesis: none` stops the browser faking a bold. Headings use lining figures, because Goudy's old-style "10" reads as "Io" at display size. Italic is reserved for written-in values (the customer's name, the measure-book values, the measurement date). Fjalla One, a heavy condensed sans like the numerals printed on a Hoechstmass tailor's tape, appears only where something is printed on the tape: numerals, labels, the needle reading and each panel's week line. It replaces the brief's Antonio on purpose: Antonio is already testimonials-surf-school's face. The reading's half is its own figure (Goudy `½` at .7em, raised), so "Week 9½" never reads as "91/2", and the numeral sits in a fixed-width slot so the roll never shows two values. Each numeral is printed once: a reverse-edge copy, as on a real Hoechstmass, was removed because an upside-down 6 or 9 reads as the other fitting week. There are no all-caps eyebrows, no middle-dot meta strings, no arrows on links and no monospace.
- **The cloth answers a hand.** In `#collection`, a fine pointer (listeners attach only under the `fine` condition) moving over the finished chalk-stripe worsted carries one soft band of light with it, the way the nap catches light when you smooth cloth with your palm. A finger dragged across the cloth on a phone does the same. It is a `soft-light` radial layer inside the cloth frame, so the chalk stripes brighten more than the ground, and the text above it is untouched. The native cursor is kept.
- **Radii mean objects.** Buttons and card objects are 2px. The measure book and the week-1 card have one clipped corner (clip-path), like a cut card. There are no shadows except the 2px offset edge under the hanging pattern pieces, which separates card from card.

## Motion

Tier: detailed. Every time-based move uses `expo.out` (CSS `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`), every scrub uses `ease: 'none'`, and drawn marks use `--ease-draw: cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1)` in and out. Everything is built inside one `gsap.matchMedia` with `motion`, `reduce`, `fine (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`, `wide (min-width: 992px)` and `tall (min-height: 560px)`. Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` (`autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`, anchors handled by the page), and `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`. Hidden first states exist only under `html.js` inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`.

## Do not touch

- **The scale is the content.** On the pinned tape each panel is centred on `--needle-x + --w * --week` and is `--pw` (1.8 weeks) wide, the tape is exactly `10 * --week` long from `--needle-x`, and the ticks are gradients on the same token. On the vertical tape the panels sit at `top: calc(var(--w) * var(--week))` in a ruler exactly `10 * --week` tall. Do not let panels push the tape or space them with margins. If you change `--week`, check the vertical fallback: each panel must be shorter than the gap to the next one (the tightest are weeks 9 to 10 and 1 to 3; the phone week is `max(48svh, 22rem)` and drawings float beside the heading so week 9 fits).
- **Layers on the pinned tape.** The handoff cloth sits above the track but under the tape and the panels (`z-index` 1 and 2), so the week-9 text stays readable while the cloth opens, and the needle (4) stays on top of everything.
- **The needle reading is geometry.** On the pinned tape it is the pin's progress times ten, because `x` runs linearly from 0 to `-10 * week`; tape jumps use the same map (`pin start + week * W`). On the vertical tape it is the sticky needle's position on the ruler. Either way it matches where the chalk mark visibly sits. The per-panel scrub ends with the week mark on the needle, so a tape jump always lands on a finished drawing. The needle is hidden when the script never ran (`html.run`).
- **The pin only runs when it fits.** After `html.trk` is set, every panel's week line must sit inside its panel and every coat must be at least 140px tall, or the class comes off and the vertical tape is used. Keep that check if you change copy length or `--tape-h`.
- **Stitches are dashed paths revealed by a mask.** A dashed path cannot be drawn with its own dash offset. Each stitch path has a twin solid path inside a `<mask>` (ids `m3`, `m6`), and DrawSVG animates the twin. Keep the two `d` attributes identical.
- **CSS-drawn marks use `pathLength="1"` with `stroke-dasharray: 1 2` and a hidden offset of `1.01`.** The long gap stops round caps from leaving a dot at the end of an undrawn line. Do not add `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` to these paths: it puts the dash pattern in screen units and breaks the draw.
- **Accessibility.** The tape is a labelled group with six real controls (two links, four buttons with explicit labels). The plain numerals and printed labels are `aria-hidden`. Measure rows are toggle buttons with a polite live caption. Every drawing is `aria-hidden` or `role="img"` with a text alternative (the hanging pattern, the woven label). Focus rings are tape yellow on cloth and black on card, and the dialog returns focus to its opener. Focus also comes back if the browser force-closes the dialog on a repeated Escape, and Lenis restarts.
- **The nap band never carries meaning.** It is decorative, sits inside the `aria-hidden` frame, has `pointer-events: none`, and is off under reduced motion. Keep it under the text. The collection paragraph is full basting white, not muted, because a chalk stripe under the brightest part of the band (`--nap` at .45) would take muted text below 4.5:1; full white stays above 5:1 there.
- **Robustness.** If any library fails to load, `html.js` is removed and everything is visible and drawn. Anchors, tape jumps, the needle reading (a passive scroll listener), the measure book, the dialog and the form all still work in plain JS. Under reduced motion there is no Lenis and no hidden state, and the reading still updates.
- **No horizontal overflow** from 360 to 1600px, measured with nothing clipping the body (`scrollWidth` equals `innerWidth` with and without motion). The pinned track is wider than the screen, but it sits inside `#ruler`'s `overflow: clip`.

## Adapting

Change the `:root` tokens first, then the scale, then the copy.

- **Another process on a tape.** Keep the ruler and change the unit. Set the tape length (the `10` in `grid-template-rows: calc(10 * var(--week))`, in the `html.trk` track and tape widths, in `setWeek`'s clamp, in `readNeedle`, and in the track tween's `-10 * W`), the marks (`style="--w:N"`), and each panel's `--w`. Use any unit that fits in about ten to fifteen steps: days for a sourdough course, months for a timber frame. Rename the reading prefix ("Week") in the `.rw` span.
- **Retiming or removing motion.** CSS durations live in `--t-in`, `--t-out`, `--t-press`, `--t-release`, `--t-draw-in`, `--t-draw-out`, `--t-chalk`, `--t-ring`, `--t-field`, `--t-rule`, `--t-err` and `--t-mark`. GSAP timings are literals in the matchMedia callback: the load timeline offsets (.1, .35, .55, 1.0 s), the S1 timeline (1.2 s, .8 s, .55 s), and the per-panel scrub (`start: 'left 85%'` pinned, `'top 75%'` vertical). To drop the basting scrub, delete the `$$('.panel').forEach(baste)` line and the drawings show finished. To drop the pin, delete the `if (motion && tall)` fit check and the `if (trk)` block; the vertical tape then runs with motion too. To drop the cloth handoff, delete the second timeline in `if (trk)` and the `.handoff` element. To drop the cloth nap, delete the "Cloth nap" block and its `unNap()` call, and the `.nap` element; retime it with the two `quickTo` durations (.6 s) and the fade tweens (.4 s in, .6 s out). To drop the load sequence, delete the first `gsap.timeline()` and the `fontsReady.then` SplitText block, and remove the matching `html.js` hidden states in CSS. Never remove the reduced-motion branch.
- **Copy.** Panel paragraphs are about 35 to 50 words. Longer text needs a larger `--week` on phones. Week lines are full sentences ("Week 6. About 30 minutes."), not meta strings. Keep the visit count consistent across the H1, the lead and the H2.
- **Headings.** The intro uses an `h1` because the section opens the page. Inside a larger page, demote it to `h2` and the section H2s and the dialog title to `h3`, and keep the order.
- **Layout.** At 992px and wider, the hero text spans 7 columns and the pattern 5. The measure intro and dress form take columns 1 to 5 and the book 7 to 12. On the pinned tape the needle is at 38vw (50vw below 992px), the tape is `--tape-h` tall along the bottom, and each panel puts the coat (`--coat-w`, capped by the screen height) beside its text, stacked under the text on phones. In the collection section the cloth is always full bleed once you reach it (the handoff or the unroll has already opened it), so the text never straddles a frame edge; the woven label moves beside the text from 992px. On the vertical fallback the tape sits in column 2 with the reading in column 1 and the panels in columns 3 to 12; below 992px the tape is a 44px strip and the reading is printed on the tape between two blue needle lines.

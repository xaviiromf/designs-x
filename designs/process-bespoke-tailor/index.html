Three fittings between your first measure and your suit.

A bespoke coat is cut for you, tried on you half-made, taken apart and cut again. Here is what happens at each visit, and how long it takes: about ten weeks, five visits and sixty hours of handwork.

See the fittings

The first measure

About an hour at the shop. We take twenty-six measurements, then watch how you stand, because posture changes the pattern more than numbers do. Wear what you'd normally wear to work.

Three fittings in ten weeks

  1. Week 1. No visit.

    Cutting your pattern

    Before any cloth is cut, the cutter drafts your pattern by hand on manila card from the measurements and posture notes. The pattern is yours. It hangs on a hook in the cutting room for every suit after this one.

  2. Week 3. About 45 minutes.

    First fitting: the skeleton baste

    Your coat is held together with long white tacking stitches: one sleeve, no collar, no pockets. It is meant to be wrong. We pin and chalk it on you, then unpick it and recut the cloth.

    Bring the shirt and shoes you'll wear with it.

  3. Week 6. About 30 minutes.

    Second fitting: the forward

    The coat comes back recut, with the collar on, both sleeves set and the pockets cut. We watch how it hangs when you sit, reach and turn, and set the sleeves to how your arms rest. Trousers are tried for the first time.

    Bring your watch, so we can set the cuff over it.

  4. Week 9. About 20 minutes.

    Final fitting

    Finished except for the buttonholes. We mark the sleeve length, hem the trousers to your shoes and note anything that still pulls. Most suits need nothing more; a few need one small adjustment.

Coat unpicked and recut, 9 hours

Canvas and lapels padded by hand, 14 hours

Week 10: collection.

The buttonholes are sewn by hand, about forty minutes each, and the suit is pressed one last time. Your pattern stays on its hook: the next suit starts from it and usually needs one fitting fewer.

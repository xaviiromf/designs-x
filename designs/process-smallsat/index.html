From booked slot to first contact

We fly 1U to 12U CubeSats on shared launches from our integration room in Glasgow. Here is what happens between your deposit and your satellite’s first beacon, and what we need from you at each stage. Plan on twelve months. If your satellite is already tested and licensed, stages 3 and 4 close as soon as we have your papers, and it can fly in six.

1 of 7: Book a slot

  1. 1 Book a slot

    12 months before launch

    Pick a shared launch with an orbit that suits your mission. We hold one slot for 14 days while you check dates; a 20% deposit turns the hold into a booking.

    You send

    • Form factor and mass
    • Target orbit
    • Earliest ready date

    We send

    • Launch window and price
    • Draft contract

    Passes when: deposit received.

  2. 2 Agree the interface

    10 months before launch

    The interface control document fixes the satellite we will load: envelope, mass, centre of mass, rail finish, deployment switches and the remove-before-flight pin. It follows the CubeSat Design Specification (opens in a new tab) within our dispenser’s limits.

    You send

    • CAD envelope and mass budget
    • Switch drawing

    We send

    • ICD rev A
    • Dispenser drawing and load levels

    Passes when: ICD signed at rev B or later.

  3. 3 File the frequencies

    9 months before launch

    Your satellite may not transmit without a licence from your national regulator, which files your frequencies with the ITU (opens in a new tab). We give you the orbit and launch date they ask for. Start now: coordination takes months.

    You send

    • Licence application reference
    • Frequency plan

    We send

    • Orbit parameters letter
    • Launch date confirmation

    Passes when: copy of your licence received, 3 months before launch at the latest.

  4. 4 Prove it survives

    4 months before launch

    Your flight unit is shaken on three axes at the levels in the ICD and cycled in thermal vacuum. Run the tests yourself, or book our shaker in Glasgow.

    You send

    • Vibration and thermal-vacuum reports
    • Post-test functional check

    We send

    • Test level sheet
    • Shaker booking if you need it

    Passes when: reports accepted with no open non-conformances.

  5. 5 Ship and fit-check

    6 weeks before launch

    Ship the flight unit sealed, with a humidity indicator and the pin fitted. We unpack it in our ISO 8 cleanroom, weigh it, measure the rails and test the deployment switches with you on a call.

    You send

    • The flight unit
    • Battery charge procedure

    We send

    • Fit-check report with photos

    Passes when: mass and rails within ICD limits.

  6. 6 Load the dispenser

    4 weeks before launch

    We charge the batteries to the agreed level, pull the pin, load your satellite and close the door. Nobody touches it after that. The dispenser travels to the launch site and is mounted on the vehicle.

    You send

    • Sign-off on the door-closed photos

    We send

    • Door-closed photos
    • Shipping confirmation

    Passes when: dispenser mounted on the vehicle.

  7. 7 Launch and deploy

    Launch day

    The dispenser door opens 60 to 90 minutes after lift-off, on the launch provider’s timer. Your satellite switches on and waits the 45 minutes the specification requires before its first transmission.

    You send

    • Your ground station’s pass schedule

    We send

    • Deployment time within six hours
    • First orbital elements

    Passes when: dispenser door opens.

Panels out, beacon on

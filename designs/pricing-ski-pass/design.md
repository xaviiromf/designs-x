## Why it works

**The bold idea is the break-even lift line, and the skier drives it.** The section answers the question people ask at the ticket window, lets them test their own number, then sells the answer.

- **The lift line.** Under "How many days will you ski?", the cheapest cost of skiing 1 to 20 days is drawn the way Ferntal's own trail map draws a lift: a cable with one tower tick per day skied and a terminal at each end. The cable climbs at € 62 a day, nearly levels off at day 6 where one week pass takes over, climbs again, and at day 13 meets the flat gentian season line.
- **The chair is the control.** A small orange chair hangs from the cable and is the thumb of an invisible range input lying over the plot. Drag it with a mouse or a finger, step it with minus and plus, use the arrow keys, or press a pass row: Day sets 3 days, Week 9, Season 13, and Family turns the family toggle on (moving to day 10 if you were below it).
- **The break-even beat.** When the chair reaches day 13, a circled lift-number badge "13" pops on the cable (back.out(2), .35s), the label "Day 13: the season pass is cheaper" opens with a clip (.4s expo.out) and the losing day and week lines fade back. Each tower after it is labelled "free". When the chair rides across it (the scroll ride or a single step), the explanation follows at .25s and the button label at .4s; a drag or a jump answers at once. Below 13 it all reverses.
- **The ride is scroll-driven.** With motion on, the board first answers for day 1 (readout, rows and button), so the station's pop is the first time anyone sees it. As you scroll the board through the screen, the chair rides tower by tower to 13, where the station pops, on past three free towers to 16, and back to park on 13; scrolling back rewinds it. If the whole board is already on screen it plays once by itself, paused while out of view. Your first drag, key, focus, step, row or toggle ends the ride and hands you the chair where it is.
- **Family mode.** "Family of four" reshapes every line in place into two adults and two children: each path is recomputed between the two scales over .7s expo.inOut, the gridline numbers swap at the midpoint, and the station slides from day 13 to day 10. The chair stays on its day. Below day 10 the button reads "Load a week pass for each of you" (or "day passes"), and the form adds a line saying each person needs a pass on their own KeyCard.

- **Every point is arithmetic.** `PRICES` sits at the top of the script. `single(n, age)` tries every mix of week and day passes against the season pass, and `family(n)` does the same for each family member against the family pass. `console.assert` checks that the season pass wins from day 13 for one adult and the family pass from day 10 for two adults and two children, along with every other figure the copy states (€ 310, 504, 636, 806, 1,512 and € 53 a day), that the range starts on the break-even day and that each pass row lands in its own pass. The hidden 20-day table, the readout, the slider's `aria-valuetext` and the chart are all generated from the same functions.
- **Structure encodes truth.** Each tower tick is one day skied. The cable is the minimum of all ways to pay. The dashed day-pass line and the whole-week staircase show why the cable bends where it does. Gridlines sit at the same heights in both modes (adult y-max 1,300, family 2,600), so only their numbers change when you switch. The hero's seven lifts use the same notation quietly, so the chart reads as Ferntal's own map language rather than a generic chart. The KeyCard is the only raised object, because it is the only physical thing you take away.
- **One trail-map vocabulary.** Pole orange (#D65A00) is the orange of the poles that mark piste edges. It is used only for the cable and its towers (and their legend glyph), the chair, the selection badge, the focus ring, the input focus rule, the invalid-field border and the error marker, and never for text. Gentian (#2C4FA0, a deeper, more violet blue than the collection's tap and bike blues) marks the pass that wins. Fir ink is the dark green of the trees on the map. The ground (#E8EDEF) is groomed snow in shade, cooler and greyer than the other light grounds in the collection.
- **Type from ski posters and wayfinding.** Krona One is a wide grotesque in the spirit of 1950s and 60s Swedish posters. It sets the h2, the h3s, the brand, the readout lead, the stub names and the KeyCard's pass name, never body text or figures. The answer rows and the pass control use Geologica 600, so they read as a ledger and the totals lead. Geologica, from the Oslo foundry Monokrom, sets everything else: its large x-height and open shapes read well in snow glare. Font check, 2026-10-24, in Chromium against the served Google Fonts builds, rendering "1111" and "0000" at 100px with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`: Albert Sans measured 122.4 and 251.6px and Gantari 155.6 and 256.8px (neither build has a working `tnum`, so both were rejected); Geologica measured 259.2px for "1111", "0000" and "7777" at weights 400 and 600, and its figures are lining by default ("3", "5" and "9" stand at cap height with no descent). So every price, total and axis label is set in Geologica (600 for prices) with tabular figures.
- **The headline names the four passes.** "Ski Ferntal" stays put while the second line turns once through "for a day.", "for a week.", "all winter." and "as a family.", in the same weight, width and colour as the rest of the heading, and rests on "all winter.". It is the only ambient motion on the page; it runs one cycle and, on the first wheel, key or touch, rolls straight to "all winter.", and the full sentence is what screen readers, reduced-motion users and no-JS readers get.
- **The rows are the proof.** Beside the chart, four rows give the total of each way to pay for the chosen days, computed from the same functions: at 13 days for one adult, day passes € 806, week passes € 698 (two weeks and a day), the season pass € 689, and the family pass € 1,590 for four. The cheapest row is pressed and its total is the answer's; in family mode every row is for four. The figures roll as the day changes. The stubs at the top stay the price list.
- **Quiet around the loud thing.** The heading, lede and four price stubs are plain and left-aligned. The stubs are separated by rules rather than boxed as cards. The pass choice in the form is a four-segment control with name and price only, like the age control, rather than a third listing of the passes. There is no pinned stage: the chart sits in the page flow, and the scroll moves only the chair.
- **Prices are written "€ 62".** That is how an Austrian ticket window, trail map and resort site write them. The euro sign is followed by a no-break space, and the figures stay tabular.
- **Revisions made against the collection's defaults.** The accent moved from a terracotta-like #E0480E to piste-pole #D65A00 on a cool ground. There are no eyebrows, no monospace, no arrows on links, no big-number hero and no single accented headline word. The brief's pinned, scroll-scrubbed stage "Which pass pays for itself" (the same device as other designs' pinned charts and vehicles riding a route) was replaced by this instrument the person drives, under "How many days will you ski?"; the scroll still drives the chair's ride through the board, without pinning, and the change is recorded in research/plan.json. A later fix pass made the ride scroll-scrubbed, replaced the family crossfade with a morph, kept the break-even hidden until the chair reaches it, split the readout from the slower answer, set the rows in Geologica and turned the pass radio cards into a segmented control. Later critique removed the lede fade, the fieldset and "includes" rule draw-ins and the magnetic buttons' 3D tilt, so the load sequence and the lift line carry the motion. At the final critique the hollow ring on the fine-pointer day cursor was removed as the one accessory too many: the dotted hairline already marks the tower the tag reads.

## Do not touch

- **The arithmetic and its asserts.** Never hardcode the cable, the break-even day, the readout or the table. If you change `PRICES`, keep the asserts and change the copy to match them, never the other way round.
- **The range input is the real control, and the chair is its thumb.** The `<input type="range">` lies over the plot (one day per thumb width), `opacity: 0` and `pointer-events: none`; the board's pointer handlers only set its day. It keeps `aria-labelledby="q"` and the readout as `aria-valuetext`, arrow keys, Home and End work natively, and its focus ring is drawn around the chair (`.board:not([data-pointer]):has(.days:focus-visible) .chair-ring`; a mouse press sets `data-pointer`, the next key clears it, so a drag never shows the keyboard ring). The minus and plus buttons use `aria-disabled` at 1 and 20, so focus never drops. The polite live region announces drags, steps, rows and the family toggle after 600ms; the slider announces itself.
- **`touch-action: pan-y` on the board.** On touch, a vertical swipe still scrolls the page and a horizontal drag moves the chair. A touch sets the day only on a tap (under 10px of travel) or once the move is clearly horizontal (more than 6px, and wider than tall).
- **The resting day is the break-even.** The markup, `S.day` and the static readout start on day 13, the form starts on the season pass, and reduced motion, no CDN and no JS stay there with the station and label shown. With motion, before the ride the board answers for day 1 (readout, rows, button, no station), so the station is first seen when the chair pops it; the ride ends parked on 13. A person's input hands over for good. A change of the motion preference keeps the day and the scroll position.
- **The chart is drawn whenever it is on screen.** Only the one-time line reveal (S2) starts hidden, under `html.js` and motion, and it fires as soon as any part of the board is in view, at load, after the fonts settle or on scroll. The ride is scrubbed from `top 75%` to `bottom 55%` of the board (scrub .6s); when the whole board is already on screen it plays by itself, only while the board is in view. `G()` treats a frame longer than about 160ms (a tab back from the background, or a screenshot tool's virtual time) as "show end states at once": reveals and the ride jump to their end, and badge pops, label swaps and the TR4 switch apply instantly. Keep that guard, or static captures show an empty board.
- **€ 0 is the baseline, the rail is below it.** The € 0 line is the solid shade axis; one hanging chair (31 units at the chair's scale, plus 6px) below it, a faint dotted rail carries only the day ticks and numbers, so the gap reads as the chair's clearance and the chair on day 1 never crosses a line.
- **The cable notation.** One tower tick per day, lift terminals (a Fir block with a snow bar) at day 0 and day 20, dotted gridlines so the cable reads as the line, and nothing crossing the top gridline (the day-pass line and the staircase stop there with a 4px chevron in their own stroke). The chair hangs below the cable and moves along it, never straight across corners. Keep the break-even marker as a circled lift-number badge standing on the cable.
- **The "free" labels.** One per tower after the break-even, 12px above the cable; on boards under 560px a single counting tag ("3 free days") right-aligned above the flat cable at day 20, never beside the chair.
- **The reserved cells.** Every possible readout sentence (20 days in both modes) sits invisible in the readout's grid cell, and the four explanations share one cell, so nothing below moves between day 1 and day 20. The age cell crossfades in place (CD9). The first-day field is always there: disabled on the season start for season and family passes (it keeps the person's own date for when they switch back), editable for day and week, and only its hint crossfades. The markup matches the default season state, so nothing crossfades on load.
- **Pole orange is graphics only** (3.3:1 on snow, 3.8:1 on card). The badge glyph on it is Fir (3.6:1, a graphic). The segmented controls are outlined in Fir, and the checked segment is inverted (Fir ground, card text) with the badge on the pass control, so the state never depends on orange.
- **Only the KeyCard is raised.** It keeps the ID-1 ratio (85.6 / 54), the 3.7% / 5.87% radius, a 1px Fir hairline and one soft shadow.
- **Hidden starting states only under `html.js`** (added only when GSAP, ScrollTrigger and Lenis loaded) **and inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`.** Without the CDN, the chart, slider, stepper, rows, form and dialog all work instantly from plain JavaScript. Without JavaScript, the board, legend, readout, rows, family switch, drag hint and buy form are hidden; the 20-day table, all four pass explanations (each with its pass name) and a line pointing to the pass machines and ticket office are shown.
- **The hidden table sits inside a wrapper `div.daytable`.** Tables ignore `overflow: hidden`, and the table would widen the page on phones.
- **The native `<dialog>`** with `margin: auto`, Escape and backdrop click handled through the closing animation, Lenis stopped while it is open, and two ways back. The form keeps `novalidate` with JavaScript validation and `preventDefault`, because GitHub Pages answers a POST with 405.
- **Heading level.** The section starts at an h2 (then h3) and should sit under the host page's h1.

## Adapting

1. **Prices and rules first.** Edit `PRICES` (day, week and season by age, plus family, deposit and week length) and `SEASON`. Update the asserts to the new break-evens, the range's `value`, the static readout and the `STAY` days for the rows, then the copy in the stubs, the `#why` paragraphs, the hints and the static table (regenerate it from `how()`). Set `MODELS.*.yMax` to about twice the flat pass price and keep both grids at the same fractions of their y-max.
2. **Another usage-priced product.** The pattern works for any per-use price against bundles and a flat pass: transit (single, 10-trip, monthly), pools, coworking. Rename the units in the readout, keep one tick per unit of use, and redraw the chair as that world's object (a tram, a swimmer, a desk chair), small and hanging from or sitting on the line. Redraw the hero ridge as that world's map (a tram network, a pool plan) in the same quiet notation.
3. **Tokens.** Change the colour tokens to the brand's map or signage colours, keeping the roles: ground, card, ink, secondary, one graphics-only accent for the cheapest path, and one "winner" colour for the flat line. Keep secondary text at 4.5:1 or better on ground and card, and the accent at 3:1 or better as a graphic.
4. **Fonts.** Any wide display face can replace Krona One for headings and names. The text face must pass the same check: "1111" and "0000" the same width under `tabular-nums` in the served build, with lining figures.
5. **Layout.** From 992px the hero splits text (columns 1 to 6) and ridge (7 to 12), the chart takes columns 1 to 8 and the answer 9 to 12, and the form takes 1 to 7 with the sticky checkout 9 to 12. Below that everything stacks, and the ridge stands on the price rule at the right, at most 30rem wide. The stubs are 2 by 2 under 900px. The board is 16 / 10 from 600px and 10 / 9 below; the chair scales with it, `clamp(1.15, width / 420, 1.7)`. The slot is as wide as its longest phrase; "as a family." must fit the column at `--fs-xxl` (checked at 390px).
6. **Retiming or removing motion.** Durations and easings are `:root` tokens (`--t-*` in seconds, `--ease-*` for CSS, `--gs-*` for GSAP ease names the script reads); the chart-reveal staggers and offsets are literals in `revealChart()`. To remove one effect, delete its line in the load timeline or its `gsap` call; the vanilla state functions (`swap`, `station`, `showWhy`, `ride`) fall back to instant changes when `G()` is false. To drop the rotating slot, delete the slot timeline and the `.title-anim` markup, and the static sentence becomes the heading. To drop the libraries entirely, remove the three CDN tags: the page then behaves like the reduced-motion version.
7. **Stack translation.** Keep `single`, `family`, `breakEven` and `how` as pure functions. Render the SVG from their output on resize, keep the range input as the source of truth, and keep the dialog native.

## Motion

House easing: expo.out for entrances and settles, power3.out for pointer follows, power1.out for crossfades. Only transform, opacity and clip-path animate: hover darkening on buttons, rows and cards is an opacity layer, the HV2 underline is a scaleX pseudo-element, lines draw with a clip-path inset (no DrawSVG), the family morph rewrites the paths' data each frame from the same arithmetic, and colour-only state changes (input borders, segment labels, the checkbox fill, the switch track) switch instantly. Nothing is pinned; only the signature ride is scrubbed. Everything runs inside `gsap.matchMedia`: a `motion` / `reduce` context for the choreography, and a separate fine-pointer context for the day cursor and magnetic buttons, so crossing a width breakpoint or plugging in a mouse never resets the page. When the motion context reverts, its cleanup restores the chosen day and the scroll position. Lenis (lerp .1, `autoRaf: false`) runs on `gsap.ticker` with `lagSmoothing(0)`. In-page links go through `lenis.scrollTo` with an expo curve and then move focus to the target heading; Lenis's own `anchors` option is off, so this handler owns them. The cleanup removes the ticker callback, destroys Lenis, kills the slot, ride, family and TR4 timelines, removes the slot's input listeners and the fine-pointer observer and listeners (board day cursor and magnetic buttons), shows the chair, and turns the motion flag off, so every later change is instant. The ScrollTriggers are reverted by `gsap.matchMedia`; the board's ResizeObserver that rebuilds the chart is intentionally permanent.

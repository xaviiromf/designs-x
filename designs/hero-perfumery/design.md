## Why it works

**The bold idea: a formula lens held up to the shed.** The hero picture is a dark forcing shed with fourteen forced-rhubarb stalks, and each stalk is one material of the real formula, drawn to its parts per thousand (Iso E Super 220 is the tallest, cis-3-hexenol 8 a stub; the list sums to exactly 1000). Behind it sits a second drawing with the same geometry: the perfumer's formula sheet, blotter white, with outlined bars, a 0-250 parts scale and labels. A circle of that sheet (a lens with a primrose rim) shows through the shed, so wherever it sits, the bar under it lines up exactly with the stalk it replaces, with its parts number on top. The picture becomes measurable without leaving the picture. On load a small candle on the soil catches (its flame and the glow flicker up to full together) while the headline lines are forced up out of their masks with an uneven push, so the text column has a beat from the first frame (header, lede and buttons are already there, still); then the stalks are forced up out of the soil by the same push in order of their share, each crown unfurling as its stalk lands, and the lens opens on the rhubarb accord with its tag above it ("Rhubarb accord, 150"). A mouse carries the lens: over a stalk it leans towards that bar's reading spot (60%, and never so low that the bar's number leaves the disc), and over empty shed it shrinks to a small paper loupe (45% of its radius) instead of carrying a blank disc. Picking a material in the list glides it there; and simply scrolling the list does the same, because the row at the reading line (55% of the viewport; on phones one full row below the sticky shed's fade, so the lit name is never washed out) lights its stalk. "Show the formula" grows the same lens until the sheet covers the frame, where the names appear.

**What supports it: the shed burns down.** Scrolling through "How it wears" runs time on skin from 0 min to 8 h. Every stalk shrinks to its residue at its own evaporation time (hexenol is down to a sliver by 10 minutes, the rhubarb accord drops to a quarter by 3 hours, the musk and woods stay near full), the readout appears (it is hidden above the drydown, where time does not move) and counts the time, the candle glow sinks and the current step turns carmine. Each step lights the material it names first (grapefruit, beeswax, orris, vetiver). On the sheet the outline stays (the formula) and only the blush fill shrinks (what is left on your skin); the lens follows the lit bar, centred between its outline top and its fill top and grown just enough to hold both, and the tag says "Orris: 13 of 20 left". So the lens reads formula against residue at any point of the scroll.

**Not a repeat: the collection's only signature-level pointer lens.** Elsewhere a lens is cut in review or plays a supporting role (contact-brand-agency, contact-expedition-outfitter, gallery-botanical-illustration, footer-architecture-biennale, and a CU4-style beam in features-ebike). cta-vineyard-harvest moved off its pointer lens because it sat too close to this one and now uses a fixed eyepiece, so no other design repeats the parked-lens-plus-rows pattern: a lens parked on load that a list of row buttons moves. Keep it that way; later designs must not adopt a parked lens that rows move. Here the hidden layer is data registered to the drawing (a chart whose bars sit exactly on the stalks), not a photo, a blueprint or decoration, and the lens also changes over time with the drydown. There is no ring icon, the lens opens to full frame instead of a separate panel, and the tag sits above the lens as its caption, the same in every state.

- **Two layers, one geometry.** The shed and the formula sheet are separate inline SVGs with the same 600x700 viewBox (offset to x 25 so the stalk field, not the box, is centred), x positions and lean, stacked in the frame and drawn in its inner area (the bleed on wide screens is only room); the sheet is clipped with `clip-path: circle(var(--lr) at var(--lx) var(--ly))` and a radial-gradient rim follows the same variables. The candle glow leans a little towards the lens (30% of its offset), so the frame has one pointer response, not two.
- **Reading without a pointer.** The fourteen formula rows are real buttons (`aria-pressed`, `aria-controls` the frame). Hover, focus or tap lights that stalk, quiets the others (bodies to .35, crowns stay at .8 so the leaves never go khaki), lifts the lit crown by 12%, moves the lens to it and sets the tag above the lens, clear of the neighbouring crowns (under it when the lens top is within the tag height + 12px of the frame top, and beside the lens when either spot would sit within 8px of the toggle, which is what happens to Iso E Super and Habanolide on phones); a click pins it. Under reduced motion a mouse over a stalk jumps the lens there. Priority: pin, then a focused row, then the row or step being read, then the parked rhubarb accord. Entering the drydown (from above or below) releases any pin, so each step lights the material it names even after someone has tapped through the list. On a fine pointer, hovering empty shed lights nothing and hides the tag. On touch, tapping a stalk pins it too, but the rows are the primary touch path (each stalk's hit area is about 17px wide at 390px). The full formula is always plain text in the list, so the drawing is `role="img"` with a short label and the sheet is `aria-hidden`.
- **The shed carries the page.** On desktop the shed column runs from column 6 to the right edge of the viewport, square on the bleed side and rounded on the inner corners, and stays sticky while the formula and drydown pass on the left. Below 992px the frame sits straight after the headline, followed by the caption, the hero button and its note, then the lede (CSS `order`), so the load, the lens and the offer are all on the first phone screen (the button ends near y 684 at 390x844), and it sticks to the top at `min(38svh, 82vw)` with a 24px primrose fade under it while the rows and steps pass beneath (the drawing is width-limited, so a taller frame on a tall phone would only add empty dark). The caption has a one-line touch version ("Tap a stalk or a material below to read its share.") swapped in by `(pointer: coarse)`, and a third version for the full sheet ("The formula as written, in parts per thousand; pick a material to light its bar.") while "Show the formula" is on.
- **Commissioner as material.** One family. The display uses weight 760, FLAR 100 (flared, engraved-label terminals) and VOLM 100; headings FLAR 60; body and controls FLAR 0. Not used anywhere else in the collection.
- **Palette from the shed, five colours.** `--primrose` #EEEBC0 is the pale yellow of rhubarb leaves grown in the dark (not cream, which would be default 1); `--shed` #2A1820 is a rhubarb-crown aubergine; `--stalk` #B8183F the carmine of the stalk (5.3:1 on primrose; underlines, fills, the tag, focus rules, errors); `--blush` #F1A7B6 only for stalk highlights, the candle glow, sheet residue and the vial liquid; `--blotter` #FCFBF6 for the lens and sheet, the confirmation and the dialog. The candle glow is a blush radial with only a small primrose core (a primrose stop over aubergine reads as grey haze), not gold; the candle itself is a primrose wax column with a blush flame.
- **Specific copy.** The headline is one plain sentence about the smell. The action keeps one verb: "Post me a sample" (header), "Post me a 2 ml sample, £4" (hero), "Post my sample" (submit). The offer, price, credit window, post days and allergens are all concrete. The confirmation computes the next Tuesday or Friday after today, the price for the chosen set, and a SHED- code valid for 60 days.

**Reasoned defaults and collection notes.**
- Default 1 (cream with a serif and terracotta): the ground is primrose, measured off forced-rhubarb leaves, the type is a flared sans and the accent is carmine.
- Default 2 (near-black with one accent): the dark appears only as a framed object (the shed and the vial) and as ink on a light page. `--shed` #2A1820 sits near footer-record-label's #2E1219; it is kept because here it is ink and a framed room, not a page ground. Do not drift it redder.
- Default 3 (broadsheet rules): the formula rows have no rules. The only lines are the parts bars (data) and the time axis beside the drydown (a real sequence).
- Default 5 (template chrome): no eyebrows, no middle dots, no arrows, no monospace; tabular figures in the same family.
- Pink highlighter sweeps under text belong to testimonials-mountain-hotel, so rows here signal with the bar filling carmine, the bar thickening and the name gaining weight.
- In-page nav is hidden under 640px on purpose: it is a hero, the sections follow directly, and the header button still reaches the sample form. No hamburger menu.

## Do not touch

- **The data.** Row `data-parts` sum to 1000; `data-evap` (minutes) and `data-floor` (fraction left at 8 h) drive the drydown; stalk heights are `parts x 2.1` viewBox units above the ground line at y 660, and the sheet's 0-250 scale uses the same factor. The vial's 14 liquid bands use the same table (heights = parts / 1000 x 266 units, list order from the top). If you change a number, redraw the stalk, the bar and the band together (they were generated from the same table) and rerun `#selftest`.
- **The time mapping.** The four steps sit at scroll progress 0, 1/3, 2/3 and 1 = 0 min, 20 min, 2 h and 8 h (piecewise linear, `minutesAt` and its inverse `progressAt`). The scrub starts when the steps list reaches 60% of the viewport (70% on narrow screens) and ends when the last step does. The first three steps keep `--step-h`; the last has none, so the scrub (steps top to last step top) spans three step heights, the labels line up with those thresholds, and the time axis is exactly three steps long. `DD_LIGHT` maps each step to the material it names first.
- **The two SVG layers must stay aligned:** same viewBox, `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMax meet"`, same x and `rotate()` per stalk. Boards and soil run past the viewBox so a wide frame never shows their ends.
- **The lens rests on the rhubarb accord** (`PARK` in the script) and returns there, or to the pinned or read material, when the pointer leaves. Its radius is 18% of the inner frame width, clamped 56-150px; `aim()` puts its centre 0.35r below the top of the measured bar (so the disc holds the bar end, its number and the neighbouring tops), never closer than r + 12px to the frame bottom; during the drydown it centres between outline and fill and grows up to 1.4r.
- **Sheet labels are 13px at any frame size** (`--lab-size` is set from the frame's scale). Every bar carries a horizontal parts number, and a number (or gridline value) shows only when it sits wholly inside the lens, 10px clear of the rim, so the rim never slices a figure (`cutNums`); the rotated names and the title show only on the full sheet, so the lens never cuts a word, and the 0-250 values sit at the right end of each gridline, clear of every bar. When stalks sit closer than 30px apart (phones and tablets) the sheet shows numbers only and the names stay in the row list.
- **Accessibility:** rows are buttons with visible focus (2px ink outline) that light the stalk and move the lens on focus, and carry `scroll-margin-top` so a focused row never hides under the sticky header or shed (on phones min(38svh, 82vw) + 3rem, past the sticky shed, its 24px primrose fade and the focus ring) and rows, fields, options, the submit, the aside controls and "Change my request" carry `scroll-margin-bottom: 1rem` at every width so the ring is not cut at the screen's bottom edge (checked by tabbing from the logo to the allergen button at 1440x900 and down the list at 390); the wordmark link is padded to 27px tall and the text buttons have a 24px minimum; the toggle changes its own label ("Show the formula" / "Show the shed"), so it carries no `aria-pressed`, and the hidden label is `aria-hidden="true"` (set and removed, never empty); on narrow screens it is 44px tall; the shed's stalk hit areas are about 17px wide at 390px, below the 24px target, so the rows are the primary touch path; the readout and tag are `aria-hidden` because the steps and rows say the same thing; the toggle's focus ring is primrose with an aubergine halo so it shows on the dark shed and the light sheet; text fields show focus with a 2px ink outline plus the carmine rule; a visible line says all four fields are needed; errors use `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` and stay `aria-hidden` until their field is invalid; a polite status line announces how many fields need fixing and recounts (or clears) as fields are corrected; focus moves to the first invalid field, which scrolls to the centre so its message is on screen, and the form never shakes; the dialog is a native modal that closes on Escape, the backdrop or "Back to the sample" and returns focus to its opener; opening it mid anchor-glide finishes the jump before Lenis stops.
- **No dead ends:** every anchor targets an existing id and moves focus there (`#top`, the sticky header, scrolls to 0); the form handles `submit` in script and swaps in a confirmation with "Change my request" (values kept); the mailto opens with `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`.
- **Progressive enhancement:** without JavaScript, or if the CDN fails, everything is visible, the toggle and readout are hidden, and the list carries the formula. Hidden start states (headline lines, the flame and glow, stalks below the soil, the closed lens, the bars, the readout, the vial liquid) are set by the script only. Switching reduced motion on or off mid-visit clears the once-only end states and keeps the scroll position in both directions (each branch's cleanup restores `scrollY`).

## Adapting

Map the brand onto the `:root` tokens first. The dark frame colour (`--shed`) doubles as ink, so pick a dark that reads as both. Keep the five-colour discipline: one light ground, one dark, one accent, one tint for light and liquid, one paper white.

For another product, keep the structure (a measured list drawn as an object, a time story that scrubs it, a trial offer). Regenerate the stalks from the new table: x positions about 36 units apart from x 92, lean at most 3 degrees and smaller for tall items so neighbours never cross. Up to about 16 items fit at 600 units wide; beyond that the phone layout loses legibility. Write each step as something true at that time, and set `STEP_MIN` to the times the steps name.

To change the offer, edit the radio values, the confirmation sentence and the price map in the submit handler. The post days are Tuesday and Friday in `nextPostDay`. Validation is a UK postcode regex; replace it for other countries.

**Retiming or removing motion.** Durations and eases live in the tokens (`--dur-*`, `--ease-*`) for CSS and inline in the script for GSAP; the script uses `expo.out` for arrivals, `expo.inOut` for wipes and `none` for the scrub. To drop a moment, delete its block inside `mm.add(...)`: the load timeline, the bars (`.pbar i`), the row reading line (one ScrollTrigger per row), the drydown (`dd` and its ScrollTrigger; the timeline is shared with the reduced-motion cuts), the vial pour, or the pointer-carried lens (`if (fine)`). The page stays complete without any of them. Never remove the reduced-motion branch or the `html.js` guard.

The vial tag's type uses `--fs-s` and `--fs-m` as SVG user units, so it scales with the vial drawing rather than staying at 15 and 17px.

## Motion

Everything runs inside one `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, fine, wide). Lenis and ScrollTrigger share the GSAP ticker (`autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`); cleanup removes the tick, restores lag smoothing and destroys Lenis. GSAP animates only transform, opacity and clip-path. Accepted CSS exceptions, all small state changes rather than motion: the catalogue's `grid-template-rows` error reveal; the lit row's name weight (560 to 640, which can rewrap a long name); and colour or paint transitions on the lit bar fill, the current step label, floating labels, option and toggle borders, the checked option's background, the tick (`stroke-dashoffset`) and the resting link underline colour. The native cursor is never hidden. Technique ids refer to `research/motion-techniques.md`.

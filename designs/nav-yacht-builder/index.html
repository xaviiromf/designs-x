How a hull is built

Every hull goes through the same five stages, in the loft and then the build shed. The times below are for a Hope 34, about 11 months from the first line on the floor to launch. The bigger boats take longer at every stage, mostly in fit-out: about 15 months for a Hope 41 and 22 for a Hope 52.

  1. About 3 weeks

    Lofting

    We draw the lines full size on the painted floor, fair them with a spruce batten held by lead ducks, and lift the mould shapes straight off the floor.

  2. 2 weeks

    Strongback and moulds

    A level strongback goes down in the build shed, and the moulds are set up on it upside down, one every 600 mm.

  3. 6 weeks

    Strip planking

    Western red cedar strips, 19 by 25 mm, are glued edge to edge over the moulds and held with staples until the glue cures.

  4. 5 weeks

    Sheathing and fairing

    The hull is sheathed in glass and epoxy inside and out, then faired with long boards until a batten laid on it finds no hollows.

  5. 5 to 9 months

    Fit-out and launch

    Joinery, engine, systems and rig go in. The boat is launched at high water from the North Quay slip, with you aboard.

Refit and winter storage

We haul boats up to 16 tonnes on our hydraulic trailer and keep 22 under cover from November to March. Refits run through the winter, so book before the end of September.

    How a box is handled

    1. 01

      You post the box with the media standing upright and no rubber bands or magnets near them, and I write to you the day it arrives.

    2. 02

      I read each medium three times on a drive made for its format, and keep the raw signal as well as the data.

    3. 03

      Where the reads disagree, I rebuild the sector from the reads that agree and note every repair.

    4. 04

      You get everything back: the media, the files on a USB stick and a report with the outcome of every medium.

      Six checks every medium gets

      Whether it is one family disk or a museum's 400, every medium goes through the same six checks, in this order.

      1. Look before reading. Mould, creases, loose shutters and leaked batteries are found under a lamp before any drive touches the medium.

      2. Clean the drive head before every box, and again after any disk that sheds its coating.

      3. Read three times on a drive that matches the format, recording the raw signal, not only the data.

      4. Compare the reads sector by sector. A sector counts as good when two reads agree with its checksum.

      5. Repair weak sectors from the reads that agree, and write each repair into the report.

      6. List the files with sizes and dates, and give the medium its outcome: clean, repaired, partial or unreadable.

      Your report lists all six for each medium, so you can see why a file is missing.

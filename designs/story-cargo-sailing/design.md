## Why it works

**The bold idea: scrolling sails the ship across the Atlantic.** The crossing chapter pins a full-screen chart, 80° W to 5° E and 55° N to 5° N, equirectangular with longitude scaled by cos 30°, a 10° graticule and about 300 hand-placed coastline points in inline SVG. The chart is framed like an Admiralty sheet: a neatline with a tick every degree and a longer one every five, and on wide screens the latitude scale in the margin. The planned route is dashed. As the page scrolls, a top-down ship marker (a 48 to 80 px hull, `--hl`) moves along it. Its heading follows the route's tangent, and the route behind it is redrawn in Q-flag yellow. Ahead of the bow, clear of the wake, a wind station turns to the latest noon report's wind, with a feather for every ten knots of its Beaufort force, and gives a short gust (scale 1.15 to 1) when the wind changes. A readout gives the day, the miles sailed out of 5,068 and the latitude. The wake is a V of two foam arms and the prop wash: it stretches to 1.9 times its length, widens and brightens when the reader scrolls fast, and settles when they stop. In Santa Marta the wake dies and a Q flag goes up at the masthead. Everything else on the page supports this chapter.

- **Distance, not scroll, drives the ship.** Each noon report is a paper slip in normal flow. The page measures where each slip comes to rest and maps those scroll positions to the miles logged by that noon, from 24 daily runs in `DATA`. The ship therefore reaches each noon fix exactly as its slip settles, the readout shows that slip's own latitude, and long legs take more scroll than short ones.
- **The slips never cover the ship.** On wide screens the slips run up a column over West Africa. The script finds the lowest point where the route passes near that column and sets a line just below it. Slips rest low on the screen and then slide under that line (a scrubbed `clip-path` with a fade), so the column only ever shows below the route. A scan at 1024×768, 1100×900, 1180×820, 1280×720, 1280×800, 1366×768, 1440×900, 1536×864, 1600×900 and 1920×1080 found no overlap of the marker and a visible slip. Below 992 px, or under a 4:3 aspect ratio, the chart band sticks under the bar at about half the screen and follows the ship: the chart is drawn at 1.8 times the band's width or more and moved so the marker stays at about 60 % across and 55 % down, clamped to the chart's edges. Each report waits right under the band until the next one pushes it away, so a report is always on screen.
- **The sailed line costs nothing to draw.** The route heads west at every point (the script checks this), so everything right of the ship has been sailed. The yellow line is the same path in a second SVG clipped with `inset(0 0 0 Xpx)`. That is exact, with no dash maths and no path rebuilding.
- **The numbers add up, and the script checks them.** `console.assert` checks that the 24 runs total 5,068 nm, that Day 12 is the best run, that each leg between noons is at least as long as its great-circle legs, that every slip's printed position and run match `DATA`, that the slip spacing matches the miles, and that the manifest totals 352 positions, 304 t and 88 squares. A failed assert is a console error, so `check-flows` would catch it.
- **Honest corrections to the brief.** No route from Le Havre past La Palma and through the St Lucia Channel to Santa Marta is shorter than about 4,900 nm, so the brief's 4,730 nm could not be true. The voyage is 5,068 nm (24 days, about 8.8 kn), and runs and positions were rebuilt to fit it. The best run is 268 nm noon to noon (11 knots over a day lengthened as she sails west). Day 1 counts from the lock, which reads "Run from the lock". Day 6 has Madeira 215 miles to the south-west. Day 20 is west of the St Lucia Channel. The Day 16 chafe is a jib halyard, renewed aloft.
- **Light pages, one dark sea.** The voyage is told in daylight documents on fog paper (`#E6ECEE`). The only dark ground is the Atlantic itself, and the chapter bar turns atlantic while it is at sea. Q-flag yellow (`#F4C21B`), the flag a ship flies on arrival to ask for free pratique, marks only state: the sailed route, the current noon, the selected lot, the arrival, the fill of the ink buttons and focus on the dark ground. On the chart, yellow means the current noon and nothing else: day links preview in spray.
- **The hold floods into the sea.** When the loading is done, the two hatch covers on the stowage plan unfold shut, then the Atlantic rises from the bottom of the screen faster than the page scrolls, over the manifest, until the screen is the same blue the crossing opens on. The chapter bar turns atlantic as the flood completes.
- **The load sequence introduces the ship.** The waterline draws, the headline rises, the hull comes up, the masts step, and the four sails hoist on a CustomEase called `hoist`: three hand-over-hand surges with holds. The arrival Q flag goes up on the same ease. On a mouse, the pointer is the wind: the ship heels away from the side the pointer is on and the pennant streams away from it. On touch the ship heels as the hero scrolls away.
- **Template chrome avoided.** There are no eyebrows: place and date are in the H2s. There are no middle dots, no monospace (data uses Special Gothic at wdth 87.5 with tabular figures), no arrows on links, no shadows and no decorative gradients (the only gradients draw the link underline, the chart's degree ticks and the wake's fade), and two radii for UI (3 px cells, 6 px controls), plus a round fix dot. Only the H1 splits into lines. Place names never break across lines. Each chapter enters through its own moment (the flood, the chart, the flag, the bars), not a generic fade-up.
- **Departures from the first brief, and why.** The brief asked for a horizontal noon-report track (SC4), a latitude-against-distance profile, a pixel-cell hold cover (SC17) and 4,730 nm. The art director's required changes in the approved plan replaced the first three: a pinned horizontal track with a data line and a live readout is already the signature of features-coffee-roaster (and process-bespoke-tailor uses a third SC4), and the cell cover is the signature of process-smallsat and faq-esports-arena. The crossing became a vertical sticky chart, which also lets a westward route read with the scroll. The cover's job, closing the hatches and flooding the screen with the crossing's blue, is done here with the ship's own hatch covers and a rising sea. The 4,730 nm is corrected below. `research/plan.json` still lists the original techniques for this slug.
- **Cut on the way:** magnetic buttons, a wordmark pennant flick and, as the last accessory, a yellow ring round the current fix. The yellow dot, the slip's yellow rule and the yellow day button already say which noon is current. The wind station keeps the meteorological convention (a full feather is ten knots, a pennant fifty), so force 7 shows three feathers, not a pennant, which would claim a storm.

## Motion

Tier: detailed. Everything sits in one `gsap.matchMedia` with `motion`, `reduce`, `fine` (`(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`) and `wide` (`(min-width: 992px) and (min-aspect-ratio: 4/3)`). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` (`autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`) with `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`. Under reduced motion there is no Lenis, scroll is native, the chart is not pinned, the route is fully drawn with the ship alongside in Santa Marta, and the slips sit in a grid. `html.js` is added only when all five scripts loaded (GSAP, ScrollTrigger, SplitText, CustomEase, Lenis), so a blocked CDN gives the same static page. `html.intro` hides the H1, ship, waterline and bar items only while the load sequence is about to run, and holds the lead and the two buttons at opacity .001 (painted, so they still count for LCP) until the first headline line rises; a 4 s timer removes it whatever happens. The open Chapters menu locks the page with `html.menu-open { overflow: clip }` whether or not Lenis runs, and makes the wordmark inert so the Tab loop is the menu and its Close button. In-page links go through `lenis.scrollTo` with `duration: min(1.3, .65 + distance / 4000)` and move focus to the target heading. Cleanup removes the ticker, the pointer listeners and the refresh hooks, destroys Lenis and resets the bar. Only transform, opacity and clip-path are animated, plus CSS colour transitions on hover and state.

## Do not touch

- **One map from three places.** The SVG viewBox (`0 0 73.61 50`, `xMidYMid slice`), the CSS chart positions (`--s`, `--ox`, `--oy` from the `.cam` container's units) and the script's `measureChart` all use the same projection and fit. Change one and the fixes, labels, ticks and marker drift off the route. Everything that moves with the chart lives inside `.cam`; the readout and day links stay outside it.
- **A westward route.** The sailed line is revealed by longitude. A route that turns back east breaks it, and the assert will say so.
- **Slips in normal flow, spaced by miles.** Each slip's `--gap` is its leg's share of 5,068 nm. The ship's position comes from where the slips actually rest, so a taller slip only stretches its own leg. Never position slips absolutely.
- **The slip line on wide screens** is computed from the route each refresh. If you widen the slip column or move the chart, run an overlap check at 1024×768 and 1280×720.
- **`DATA` and its asserts**, and the copy they guard: 24 runs totalling 5,068 nm, the eight printed positions and runs, 352 of 576 positions, 304 t, 88 squares.
- **Q flag for state only**, and only behind ink or on the atlantic sea: never yellow text on fog, never decoration, never a headline word.
- **Accessibility wiring.** The chart is a `figure` with a text caption. The readout, marker, fixes and labels are `aria-hidden`, because every value is printed in the slips. The ship and Q flag are `role="img"` with titles. Lot rows are buttons with `aria-pressed` and a live caption. Day links carry the full date in `aria-label` and `aria-current="step"`. Headings run h1 > h2 > h3.

## Adapting

1. **Tokens first.** Change `--fog`, `--ink`, `--slate`, `--atlantic`, `--spray` and `--qflag`. The chart colours (`--land`, `--coast`, `--grat`) and `--spray-2`, `--rule`, `--rule-2` are `color-mix` derivations. Keep `--spray-2` at 4.5:1 on both `--atlantic` and `--land`, and keep the state colour behind ink or on the dark sea.
2. **Another journey.** Replace `DATA.runs`, `DATA.way` and `DATA.noon` (with `at`, the index of each report's point in `way`). Regenerate the route path (x = (80 − lon W) × cos 30°, y = 55 − lat), the fix and place `--x`/`--y` values, each slip's `data-pos`, `data-run` and `--gap`, and the `--gap0` on the list. A different region needs a new viewBox and coastlines in the same projection. Keep the asserts.
3. **Scroll length.** `--sea` sets how much scroll the whole voyage takes (`max(500svh, 3800px)` on wide screens, `max(560svh, 4200px)` on phones). The shortest leg must stay taller than the tallest slip.
4. **Retiming or removing motion.** The load sequence is the one timeline under "Load" (delete it and the `html.intro` rules to start on the final state). The wake is the `wake` timeline and the `pace` ticker. The slips' slide-out is the `cards.slice(0, -1)` block. Hover and focus timings are the `--t-*` and `--e-*` tokens. Removing the `motion` branch, or the `no-preference` media blocks, leaves the working static chapter.
5. **Copy.** Keep slip entries to about 100 characters, or raise `--sea`. Replace `freight@halyardandhold.example` with a real address, and the Voyage 15 dates and `DATA.voyage15Open` in the form copy.

Every March since 1986, we walk to the end of Lenga Glacier and mark where the ice stops.

Forty-one surveys from the same rock cairn. Scroll to lay them down in order, one line a year.Here they are in order, one line a year.

1986The first line

The front sits against cairn C3, 410 m above the sea. We built the cairn so every later survey starts from the same rock.

1993The tongue thins

The front is 180 m behind the cairn. Stake S2, drilled 12 m into the ice in 1991, now stands 9 m clear of the surface.

1999A lake opens

Meltwater ponds behind the 1986 moraine. We map it at 0.12 km² and start sounding it from a packraft.

2008The front floats

The ice now ends in water 40 m deep. It lifts off its bed and retreats 93 m this year, the most we have measured.

2016Calving season

An 80 m slab breaks off on 9 March, the day before the survey. We measure the new front from the cairn by laser.

2026The forty-first line

The front is 1,870 m behind cairn C3. The lake covers 2.3 km² and is 64 m deep where the ice stood in 2008.

March 20261,870 m behind cairn C3

Forty-one lines, one map. In 1986 the ice touched our cairn. Today you cross 1.9 km of rock and water to reach it, and the valley floor is younger than the station.

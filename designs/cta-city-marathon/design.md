## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the wordmark runs.** MERRITON is set as one line at bib weight (wdth 75, wght 900, tracked -0.025em) and fitted exactly to the measure. A stride bulge moves through it: the letter under the bulge stretches wider and squashes shorter, and the other letters give up width, so the word always fills the margins exactly. On load the bulge leaves the start line like the first wave, accelerates through the word, crosses the N and leaves, and the word and the top-bar facts are revealed behind it. After that, scroll position moves it from M to N (the course), scroll speed lengthens the stride, and a fine pointer pulls it to the letter under the cursor. On phones the wordmark is a sticky running header, so it runs the course while the reader works down to "Reserve my place". At rest the lockup is flat, so a still frame, the reduced-motion page and the preview are the same clean wordmark.

**How it differs from the other moving wordmarks in the collection.** hero-doc-festival flies the camera through its title, footer-architecture-biennale raises its name as a section cut, and cta-indie-game builds its name from cells. MERRITON is a horizontal stride that only redistributes width between letters. It never crops the word at the page edges, never moves anything vertically (no vertical scrub, no rise; the squash stands on the baseline) and always fills the same measure. The only clip is the one-time start-gun reveal at load.

- **Built from transforms only.** Each letter is a block with a cached natural width `w_i`. The stretch is a gaussian around a fractional letter index `c` (sigma 1.1 letters), `r_i = 1 + A * g_i`, normalised by `k = W / sum(w_i * r_i)` so that `sum(w_i * s_i) = W`. Each letter gets `translateX(sum over j<i of w_j * (s_j - 1))`, `scaleX(s_i)` and `scaleY(clamp(1 / sqrt(s_i), .72, 1))` from its bottom-left corner, so the word stands on its baseline. The amplitude is capped in closed form so that no letter ever exceeds `scaleX 2`, and the squash never takes a capital below 40px: the scaleY floor is `max(.72, 40 / cap height)`, so it stays .72 on desktop but rises to about .81 on a 360px phone (cap 49px) and .75 at 390. Measured in the 390 and 360 scroll and tap probes, the smallest capital was 43.6px mid-scroll and 40.9px on a tap. `stretch()` is a pure function, and `#selftest` sweeps `c` from -2.2 to 9.7 (the scroll range plus the speed throw) and `A` from 0 to 3 to assert that the word always sums to the measure and never passes 2.
- **Squash and stretch, not the width axis.** PRINCIPLES limits motion to transform, opacity, clip-path and filters, so `font-variation-settings` is never animated. The wdth axis sets only the static widths (75 wordmark, 87.5 headings and data, 100 text). The moving letters are a deliberate squash-and-stretch of the bib-weight glyphs.
- **The course is drawn to scale.** The five posts are flex items whose grow factor is the distance to the next post (5, 16.1, 13.9, 7.195), so the gaps are true to the course. The painted line is the column's rule. On wide screens the column stretches to the form, and its bottom is nudged so the 42.195 km finish tick sits level with the submit button: the finish line is the button. After submitting it lines up with "Change my details" instead.
- **Colour has race meaning, and sits outside the collection's crowded royal-blue-and-warm-yellow cluster.** Course blue `#0070A3` is the cyan-leaning paint of the racing line: the line, the ticks, passed km figures (5.1:1 on tyvek), field and link underlines, and the buttons, which carry the page's only white (`#FFFFFF` on course is 5.5:1). The line runs into the blue submit button. Hi-vis `#D4F53C` is the EN ISO 20471 fluorescent yellow-green of a marshal's vest. It appears only on the selected wave row and the bib stripe, always with asphalt text (12.1:1). Because hi-vis on tyvek is only 1.16:1, the selected row also gets a 3px asphalt bar and an asphalt wave disc, so selection never relies on colour. Flag red `#D40E00` (5.1:1 on tyvek), a marshal's red flag, is for errors only. Asphalt `#23272D` is the ink (14.1:1) and the 3px focus ring, because hi-vis fails 3:1 on the ground.
- **One family, by width.** Wordmark wdth 75/900. H2 wdth 87.5/700 at -0.01em. H3 and button labels wdth 100/700. Body wdth 100/400 at 17px. Km figures, field labels, paces and start times wdth 87.5/600 with tabular figures. There is no monospace, no all-caps label (the wordmark is the only caps), no eyebrow and no middle-dot meta string.
- **Labels like a printed entry form.** Each label sits above its box at wdth 87.5 / 600, 14px, never inside it. Floating labels were dropped: `type="date"` never matches `:placeholder-shown`, so its label would either stay floated or sit on top of the native dd/mm/yyyy text, unlike the other fields. On focus the box border goes asphalt and a 2px course underline runs in from the left.
- **Errors never move anything.** Each field reserves its hint-and-error line. The error text is already in the line, hidden, so the reserved height fits it. It is revealed with a clip-path wipe while the hint fades, the field's underline turns flag red, `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` are set, and focus goes to the first invalid field. Nothing alerts or shakes.
- **The confirmation is the artefact.** A Tyvek bib card with four pin holes, the hi-vis wave stripe, the bib number in the wordmark's cut and the start time. It is pinned at -1deg. Bib numbers come in equal, non-overlapping blocks of 3,200 for a 16,000 field (A 1001, B 4201, C 7401, D 10601, E 13801), plus a hash of the name. Nothing is sent anywhere; the payment link in the copy is part of the fiction.
- **One left edge, one lockup.** On wide screens "Marathon" sits under the M as the lockup's second line (wordmark width 75, weight 800, H3 size), and the date and countdown stack on column 7, the same edge as the lede and the form below them.
- **One verb.** The action is "reserve" from start to finish: "Reserve a place" in the top bar, "Reserve your place" over the form, "Reserve my place, £68" on the button and "Place reserved for Ana Ruiz" on the bib.
- **No dead ends.** "Reserve a place" goes to #enter and focuses the form heading (on phones the scroll clears the sticky wordmark). "Open the entry rules" opens a native dialog that returns focus to its opener. "Ask about charity places" is a mailto: with `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`. "Change my details" brings the form back with the values kept. "Back to the start" goes to #top and focuses the H2.

## Do not touch

- **The normalisation.** The bulge must always satisfy `sum(w_i * s_i) = W` with the cap at `scaleX 2`, and the transform origin must stay at the bottom left. Change `stretch()` only with `#selftest` passing. Never move letters with flex-grow, width or margins: that is layout animation.
- **Widths are measured, not guessed.** `fit()` clears the transforms, measures every letter and sets the font size so the natural word equals the measure. It runs on font load and resize. The CSS fallback `calc(100cqi / 5.17)` is the ratio measured at build, so the word fits even without JS.
- **Proportional posts.** The `--km` value on each post is the distance to the next post. The finish post has no grow factor. Below 992px the column has a minimum height so the 5 km gap can still hold its text: 40rem on phones (under 600px), 52rem from 600 to 991px.
- **The sticky phone header.** Under 600px `.mark-wrap` is `position: sticky; top: 0` with a tyvek background, and the scroll mapping runs over the whole page, from the section top to the moment the course paint reaches the 42.195 km tick (75% of the viewport, or lower where the page ends first), so the bulge crosses the N exactly as the finish tick turns blue and the page ends on the action. While a text, email or date field has focus, the header slides up out of the way (`yPercent -100`, .3s expo.out, transform only) so the on-screen keyboard leaves room for the field, and slides back when focus leaves the text fields. Anchor scrolls on phones subtract the header height so targets are never hidden under it. Wider screens keep the wordmark in flow and map `c` from `clamp(top bottom)` to `center top`, so the N is reached while half the word is still visible. At 1440x1000 the wordmark's line box sits at about y 32 and is about 205px tall (the glyph ink starts a little lower), so the bulge runs M to N over roughly the first 135px of scroll. Where the section enters from below in a composed page, the run starts when the word enters the viewport and ends when its middle reaches the top. Never end it at `bottom top`: the N would arrive with the word gone.
- **The ticker sleeps.** The wordmark loop runs on `gsap.ticker` only while the wordmark is on screen, skips DOM writes when nothing changed, and reads ScrollTrigger velocity (not Lenis), so native touch scroll gets the stride too.
- **Accessibility.** The wordmark letters are `aria-hidden` with an sr-only "Merriton Marathon", and they ignore the pointer (the glyph boxes overhang the top bar). Wave rows are real radios inside labels, so arrow keys work. Every field, row, checkbox, link and button has a visible focus state: a 3px asphalt outline, plus the course underline on fields and the darker course fill on buttons. Hidden prices carry `aria-hidden`. Headings that receive focus have `tabindex="-1"`.
- **Headings.** The section starts at h2 and expects the page's h1 above it; the dialog has its own h2.
- **The wordmark has no keyboard equivalent on purpose.** It is `aria-hidden` decoration with sr-only text. The pointer pull and the tap are extras; the scroll-driven bulge is its non-pointer equivalent, and making it focusable would add a tab stop that does nothing.
- **Anchor scrolls resync Lenis first.** Swapping the form for the shorter bib lets the browser's scroll anchoring move the page before Lenis hears about it, so `go()` calls `lenis.scrollTo(scrollY, { immediate: true })` before the animated scroll. Without it, the confirmation heading lands 670px above the viewport on phones.
- **Rebuilds keep your place.** When a matchMedia condition flips (width 600 or 992, reduced motion, pointer type), ScrollTrigger's revert scrolls to 0. The callback reads the old position (still current, because scroll events fire on the next frame) and restores it after the rebuild refresh. It also clears the inline clip-paths, bar opacities and letter transforms the reverted load timeline leaves, and stops a fresh Lenis if the rules dialog is open.
- **Test hooks.** `#selftest` (or `runSelfTest()`) logs SELFTEST PASS or FAIL to the console. It changes nothing on the page and is safe to strip when porting.
- **Required attributes with `novalidate`.** Names, email, date of birth, the five wave radios and the rules checkbox are `required` so automated fills (and `scripts/check-flows.mjs`) complete the form, while the script does the validating. The date field's `min="1920-01-01"` and `max="2009-04-18"` match the age rule.

## Motion

House easing: `expo.out` for entrances and state changes, `power3.out` for pointer following, `none` for scrubs. Everything is built inside `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, fine pointer, width 992 and phone, max 599px). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false` and `lagSmoothing(0)`, feeds `ScrollTrigger.update`, stops while the rules dialog is open, and is destroyed in the cleanup. Hidden start states exist only under `html.js` (added only when the libraries loaded) and only with `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. The page-load sequence waits for the wordmark font (capped at 1s) so the letters are measured before they move. Under reduced motion there is no Lenis, every final state shows, the wordmark is a flat lockup and every CSS duration token is 0s.

Every `:hover` rule sits inside `@media (hover: hover)`, so a tap on a phone never leaves a button lit or a wave row tinted; `:focus-visible` and `:active` stay outside it. Short colour state changes (button fill .2s, field and checkbox borders .15s, the error underline, passed ticks and km figures .3s, the selected row fill) are a deliberate exception to "transform, opacity, clip-path and filters only": they are single-property state changes on small elements, never scrubbed or looped, and an overlay per element would add markup for no visible gain.

Deviations from the original score, on purpose: the sprint uses power1.in on `c` from -1 to 10 with the amplitude derived from `c`, because on expo.out it crossed all eight letters in about 0.2s, and an earlier power3.out version parked a stretched N on screen for most of a second. The wave now accelerates off the start, crosses the N and leaves the word. The top-bar facts are revealed by the reveal edge rather than a stock staggered rise, and the course and form wait until the pack has passed, so the eye reads the headline first. The resting amplitude is 0 instead of .25, because a resting bulge made the M look misprinted in a still frame. The floating labels in the original score were replaced by static labels (see Why it works). The asphalt bar that once swept along the bottom of every button was removed when the buttons became course blue: the colour shift, the press and, on the two primary buttons, the magnet are enough.

## Adapting

1. Map the brand onto the colour tokens and keep the roles: one line colour that also fills the buttons (`--course`, at least 4.5:1 with `--on-course` and on the ground), one ink that is also the focus ring (`--asphalt`), one high-visibility selection fill that always carries ink text and an extra non-colour cue (`--marshal`), and one error colour (`--flag`). Re-check `--kerb`, `--course` and `--flag` at 4.5:1 on the ground.
2. Replace the wordmark letters in `.mark`, one `<span>` per letter, and update the sr-only line. Then measure the new ratio (word width divided by font size at wdth 75) and put it in `--t-mark`; `fit()` does the rest at runtime. With another typeface, re-measure `CAP` (cap height over font size) so the 40px `MIN_CAP` floor still holds. The scroll mapping `c = -1 + p * (n + 1.5)`, the sprint's end value (`n + 2`), its amplitude release point (`n + 1.5`) and the full-reveal threshold (`n - .5`) assume eight letters, so change 9.5, 10 and 7.5 for other lengths.
3. Replace the posts. Each post's `--km` is the distance to the next one, and the finish post has none. Keep three to six posts; more crowds the phone column.
4. Replace waves, prices and dates in the markup and in `BASE`, `closeLine()` and the `dob` rule. The bib blocks must not overlap: block size times waves equals the field size.
5. Retime or remove motion. CSS durations are the `--d-*` tokens and `--ease-out`. The load sequence is the `tl` timeline (to drop the sprint, replace the proxy tween with `.call(startLive, null, .1)` and set the bar items to opacity 1, and the word appears whole). Velocity strength, cap and forward throw are in `frame()` (`.0016`, `1.35`, `.0004` up to `1.2` letters), and the squash floor is `MIN_CAP`. Remove the pointer pull by deleting the `if (fine)` and `else` blocks after `indexAt`. Remove the course paint by deleting the S3 block; the CSS start states live under `html.js` and go with it. Remove the magnet by deleting `data-magnet`.
6. In a framework, render the letters, posts and wave rows from data, keep `stretch()` pure, and port the script as one effect that returns the matchMedia cleanup.

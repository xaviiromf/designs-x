## Why it works

**The one bold idea: holding a plate to the light.** The four licences are four glass negatives standing in a rack. Hovering a plate (after a 120ms intent delay), focusing it or tapping it opens it in the rack, and its picture develops from a smoky negative into a sepia-toned positive. The develop is one GSAP `filter` tween in keyframes over `--dev` (1.1s): the negative lifts in the light to `--flare-neg` (0.4 of `--dev`, `power2.in`), its tones reverse in a single cut at the flare peak (a zero-duration keyframe to `--flare-pos`), then its density builds to `--pos` like a print in the tray (0.6, `power2.out`). The tone reversal is never interpolated: any tween across `invert(.5)` maps every tone to the same flat grey, which showed as a blank grey card for two or three frames when the flip was a 0.1s linear step. The flare keeps the contrast (`contrast(1.05)` and `.85`, brightness `.95` and `1.05`), so the drawing's structure is visible on both sides of the cut, and every frame of the develop was sampled in Chromium to confirm no plate ever sits between `invert(.15)` and `invert(.85)`. `--neg` is `invert(1) sepia(0) saturate(.5) contrast(1.25) brightness(.55)`. This differs on purpose from the original brief's `invert(1) sepia(0) contrast(1.15) brightness(.85)`; do not set it back: a negative of these mostly dark scenes is mostly clear glass, which reads dark against the box, so closed plates are smoky glass with silver highlights and the developed plate is the brightest thing in the rack. `--pos` is `invert(0) sepia(.55) saturate(1) contrast(1) brightness(1)`. Its terms appear beside it, and the plate that closes re-inverts over 0.6s as a stepped mirror of the develop: it lifts as a positive to `--flare-pos` (0.25 of `--redev`), cuts to `--flare-neg`, and settles to `--neg` (0.75, `power2.out`). A plate interrupted mid-swap that is already on the target side of the cut settles there without crossing it again. Each plate keeps one develop tween by reference and kills it before starting the next, so a quick sweep across the rack always leaves every closed plate a negative (`gsap.killTweensOf` does not reach a keyframed tween, so it is not used for this). The flare midpoints are kept dim and never near paper white, so a sweep never reads as a camera flash. The first plate (Inside page) arrives in the rack already open, as a negative, with its price and terms readable by about 0.6s; the load sequence ends with it developing (from 0.8s), so a visitor reads the terms and sees the idea before touching anything, and half the rack is never empty waiting for the develop. Development happens only here. The ledger thumbnails and the request sleeve show positives that are already developed, so they support the moment and never repeat it.

- **One plate is always open on wide screens.** Leaving the rack does not collapse it, so the terms you were reading stay put. Closed plates always show their name (vertical, along the plate's edge, like the label on a stored negative) and their price at the foot. Nothing is hover-only. In the list of rows (under 1080px) the open row's negative thumbnail fades out and the licence name slides left over the vacated slot (a 0.4s `expo.out` transform, so nothing reflows and the price stays put), so the open row's name lines up with the drawing below it and the only picture of that plate is the large developing one.
- **The rack moves only transforms, clip-path and filter.** Every plate is laid out at its open width (`--o`), absolutely positioned. A closed plate is clipped to a strip with `clip-path: inset(0 Rpx 0 0)` and moved to its stacked position with `x`; the open plate's clip opens to `inset(0)`, which reveals its terms from the left. The picture inside a closed strip is shifted so the strip shows its centre. One proxy per plate tweens the visible width `w`; each frame sets both the clip (`inset(0 (o - w) 0 0)`) and the picture's offset (`-max(0, img - (w - edge)) / 2`) from it, so the picture's right edge never falls short of the glass mid-swap and no dark band shows down the plate. Text never reflows during the swap, because each panel's width is fixed and the terms (`contain: layout paint`) fade in only after the width has settled. The closing plate's terms and caption vanish at once (no fade-out), so the incoming plate never slides over half-faded text. The clip is a plain rectangle (no `round`) so it stays cheap while it animates; the plate keeps its 3px glass radius from `border-radius`.
- **The filter is cheap to animate.** It sits on `.emul`, a layer that holds only the drawing (`will-change: filter`), never on a panel with text. The grain is a static data-URI noise tile inside that layer, so it is rasterised once with the drawing and never re-blended per frame. Drawings render at 600px wide or less.
- **The palette is the archive's store room and its window.** The ground is the green buckram of a Solander archive box (`--buckram` #1B2723), a chosen dark, not a tinted near-black and not a black with one bright accent; raised surfaces (`--board` #2A3B34) are the same cloth a step lighter. Text is a cool silver-gelatin white (`--gelatin`), not cream; secondary text is a green-grey silver (`--silver` #ADB5AE). The one state colour is north light through the window (`--daylight` #B7D9F5), the light you hold a plate to, and it marks only focus, the selected licence, the nav indicator and the field focus rule. Prices are text-coloured. Colour on the page comes from the sepia prints (`--toner` and the `--img-*` tones, image-only) and the kraft sleeve mixed from them. The ground, raised and state values were checked against every colour token in the collection (Euclidean RGB): `--board` is 10.8 units from footer-record-label's #26332E, the nearest raised dark; the ground's near neighbours are ink colours on light pages; `--daylight` is 21 units or more from any colour in the collection.
- **Why a serif, and why Caslon.** The buyers are picture editors and rights managers at publishers, and Caslon is their trade's face: the English book type of the eighteenth century and still a standard for book interiors. The two cuts split by optical size. Libre Caslon Display 400 sets everything at `--fs-l` (24px) and above: the h2, the section headings, the licence names on the plates, the prices, the dialog and confirmation headings and the plate number on the sleeve. Below 24px its hairlines break up, so Libre Caslon Text (400, 400 italic, 700) sets everything else: 18px body at 1.6 leading, which a serif body needs, bold for the wordmark and the small headings, italic for form errors and the stamp's monogram. `font-synthesis: none` keeps the browser from faking weights. Neither family is used elsewhere in the collection. Figures were checked in the served Google build in Chromium by rendering `1111`, `0000` and `8888` at 48px: Caslon Text's default figures are lining and proportional (80.6, 122.9 and 114.3px); with `tabular-nums` (the `--figures` token) all three are 115.2px; the build has no old-style set. Caslon Display has proportional lining figures only and ignores `tabular-nums`, so it never sets figures that must line up: each price stands alone on its plate, while ledger fees, plate numbers, print runs, the request reference and the clause numbers are Caslon Text with tabular figures, and fees are right-aligned.
- **The decision rule is in the lede, not a badge.** "If you will license more than 80 inside-page images a year, the publisher account costs less" (80 x £85 = £6,800). No "most popular" label.
- **The ruled ledger is deliberate, not a broadsheet default.** It copies the archive's licence day-book: one ruled line per licence granted, written as a record. The only rules on the page are the ledger's and the rack rail (the shelf the plates stand on); there are no vertical rules, no column hairlines and no dense newspaper columns, and the includes strip is three short blocks with no rules at all.
- **The ledger shows what similar uses cost.** Six real-feeling licences, each a two-line record (title, then publisher and print run) with a rule between records, no vertical rules, fees right-aligned in tabular figures. Each row is one link: it fills in the plate number and licence, drops the old plate back into the sleeve, raises the new one, and glides to the form with focus in "Title and publisher".
- **The request ends on the page.** The form has no `action`; the script validates (print run is optional for the publisher account, which has no single edition), shows errors inline in reserved lines (no layout jump) and moves focus to the first invalid field. On success the fields are replaced in place by "Request PD-26-0418 sent". "Edit request" and resending keeps the reference and says "Request PD-26-0418 updated"; a new request started from a licence button or a ledger row gets the next reference (0419). The email check has two messages: empty ("Enter the email we should send the quote to.") and malformed ("That email needs an @ and a domain, like name@publisher.com."). The success panel shows a summary of what was entered, the email that was entered, "Edit request" and "Back to licences", and a blind stamp lands on the sleeve.
- **Honest copy.** The copyright paragraph says what the fee pays for (the scan, the research and permission to reproduce the archive's copy). "Not included" names training AI models. The hint under the button says what sending does: the archivist pulls the plate from cold storage.
- **The drawings are tonal, not clip-art.** Six inline SVG symbols, each built from three to five tonal planes, a horizon, one figure group and a light source, with a vignette and static grain: coal barges and a steam crane at Avonside Wharf (1911), a tram leaving the Brislington depot at night (1923), a wedding on the Clifton steps (1934), stemmers at long tables in a bonded warehouse (1938), snow on Park Street below the university tower (1947), and a diver on the lido's top board (1958). The publisher account's plate is a contact sheet of all six. They are reused through `<use>` in the rack, the row thumbnails, the ledger and the sleeve.

## Do not touch

- **One plate always open on wide screens; development only on open.** No scroll-driven development, no developing thumbnails, no ambient loop.
- **Closed plates show name and price.** Do not move either into a hover state.
- **Daylight is state only.** Never use `--daylight` for prices, links or decoration.
- **Fixed-width panel content.** The terms panel width (`--tw`) and the open width (`--o`) are computed once per resize. Do not animate `width`, `grid-template-columns` or `height` to open a plate; keep the transform-and-clip rack.
- **The filter lives on `.emul` only,** a layer with the drawing and its grain and no text.
- **Focus opens a plate** (wide screens) and closed panels are `inert`, so Tab goes button, its "Request this licence", next button. Known and accepted: Shift+Tab walks the plate buttons only (focusing a button closes the plate behind it, so its link is inert by then); every link is still reached going forward.
- **Real radios, a native `<dialog>`, a real submit button.** Enter submits from any field. The dialog stops Lenis while open, its body has `data-lenis-prevent`, Escape and the backdrop close it, and focus returns to "Read the full licence terms".
- **Text alternatives.** Each exposed drawing has `role="img"` and an `aria-label` that starts "Drawing of plate 1911/0417, ..." and ends with the year, except the publisher-account contact sheet ("Drawing of a contact sheet of six plates ..., 1911 to 1958"); the sleeve's label is rewritten when the plate changes. The thumbnails inside the plate buttons and ledger rows are `aria-hidden` because the button or row text already names the plate. The rack's visible captions are `aria-hidden` because they repeat the drawing's label.
- **No shadows anywhere.** Plates get a 1px highlight on the top edge (light through glass), the stamp's relief is two offset strokes, and the 3px radius belongs to glass only (plates, thumbnails, the sleeve's plate). Everything else is square, except the licence radios, which are round so they read as one choice and not as checkboxes.
- **No eyebrows, middle dots or arrows.** Ledger meta uses commas and its own line.

## Adapting

1. **Tokens first.** Change the colour tokens on `:root`, keeping `--silver` at 4.5:1 or better on `--board` (it is 7.4:1 on `--buckram` and 5.6:1 on `--board` here) and `--daylight` distinct from `--gelatin` so focus and selection read. The image tones (`--toner`, `--img-black` to `--img-paper`) set the print colour; keep them warm, because a warm positive inverts to the cool negative the idea needs.
2. **Tiers.** Each `<article class="plate">` holds a button (name, fee, unit), the drawing, a caption and a terms panel with a "Request this licence" link carrying `data-tier`. The rack expects four plates (its geometry is three closed strips plus one open plate); for three or five, change the `3 *` factors in `geom()` and check the closed width range (`--closed-min`, `--closed-max`) still fits a name and a price. Add a matching radio (`value` equal to `data-tier`, `data-label` for the summary).
3. **Items and images.** Replace the symbols in the sprite and the `PLATES` table in the script (plate number, symbol id, short title, drawing description). Ledger rows carry `data-plate` and `data-tier`. Real photographs can replace the drawings: put an `<img>` inside `.emul` (and the thumbnails), keep it at 600px wide or less, and the develop filter works unchanged.
4. **Copy length.** Terms panels hold three items of up to about 85 characters at 1080px, the narrowest rack (measured in Caslon Text: no overflow on any plate from 1080 to 1600px, which is why `--rack-h` has a 496px floor). Below 1080px the rack switches to rows, because between 992 and 1080px the terms panel would be about 240px wide and clip its own button. Longer terms need a taller `--rack-h`. Licence names longer than about 20 characters crowd the vertical edge label.
5. **Layout.** At 992px and wider the header bar is sticky. At 1080px and wider the rack stands horizontally (HV9). From 900px the ledger is a six-column grid; from 768px the sleeve is sticky beside the form (32px from the top, or 24px under the header bar from 992px), so the plate stays in view while the fields are filled. Under 600px the intro starts 32px under the header, so plate 1's open drawing is in the first screen and its develop is seen without scrolling. From 600 to 1079px the rack is a list of rows with the open plate's drawing and terms side by side; under 600px they stack. From 600px the four licence radios are a 2x2 grid, mirroring the four plates (one column below). Every width from 360 to 1600 has no horizontal scroll.
6. **Retiming motion.** Durations and easings are `:root` tokens that the script reads: `--open` (rack swap, 0.7s), `--dev` (develop, 1.1s), `--redev` (re-invert, 0.6s), `--intent` (hover intent, 120ms), `--t-ind` (nav indicator, 0.4s), `--t-in`/`--t-out` (terms fade), `--t-press`/`--t-release` (button press). To remove an effect: delete the S1 block (ledger rules) or the S2 tween (sleeve rise) inside `mm.add`; set `const first = false` to drop the load sequence (then plate 1 starts open and developed); set `--dev` and `--redev` to `0s` to keep the rack but drop the development (do not, unless the design changes its idea).
7. **Composing into a page.** The title bar and nav sit inside the section here, so the design has no `banner` landmark of its own. In a full page, move the bar into the site-level `<header>` and keep the nav's section spy pointed at these ids.
8. **Contacts.** Replace `licensing@pyneanddaughters.example` and `0117 496 0723` (an Ofcom drama number) in the success panel and the dialog footer. To send requests to a real endpoint, `fetch` from the submit handler and show the confirmation on success; never give the form an `action`.

## Motion

Detailed tier. GSAP 3.15.0 (ScrollTrigger, SplitText) and Lenis 1.3.26 from jsDelivr, loaded with `defer` so the inline script can set `html.js` before first paint; the Lenis CSS is inlined. Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` (`autoRaf: false`, `lagSmoothing(0)`) and every anchor goes through `lenis.scrollTo` with focus moved to the target. Everything sits inside `gsap.matchMedia` with `motion`, `reduce`, `fine` (`(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`) and `wide` (`(min-width: 1080px)`, the rack; the sticky bar is CSS at 992px). Hover-intent and resize listeners are registered with an `AbortController` that the cleanup aborts; the cleanup also kills and clears the rack's tweens, removes the ticker callback, restores `lagSmoothing(500, 33)` and destroys Lenis. Hidden initial states exist only under `html.js` inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, and are released by an `html.shown` class when the load sequence ends or the context changes. If the CDN is blocked, the `js` class is removed, all plates show open and the form and dialog still work; the plate buttons get `aria-disabled` and lose their +/- glyph and pointer cursor, so they no longer look like toggles that do nothing, and each row's name sits over its hidden thumbnail slot as an open row does (the same CSS covers a page with scripting off). The nav's current-section bar is positioned by the script, so it is absent in this fallback. The native cursor is never hidden. House easing: `expo.out` for entrances and opens, `power2.inOut` for development and swaps, `none` for scrubs, `power2.out` for drains and exits; no back or bounce curves.

Under reduced motion there is no Lenis and no split heading; everything is in its final state with plate 1 open and developed; plate swaps, development, the nav indicator, the dialog (opens and closes instantly), field and error reveals are instant or colour-only; the sleeve plate rests out of its sleeve; the stamp appears without scaling.

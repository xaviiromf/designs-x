  - A process of four to six steps that happens in sequence at set times, where the same few roles do something different at each step.
  - Onboarding for a club, guild, crew or team whose activity is a journey (a route, a tour, a shift, a release train), ending in a short sign-up.

## Why it works

**The one bold idea: every step of the night is a jump to another star system.** The right half of the section is a viewport, a canvas of about 242 by 181 pixels shown at three times its size (two times on phones), with a field of pixel stars, the convoy's nine ships flying to the right, and one body ahead of them. When the process moves to another step, every star stretches into a thin stepped line along its ray from the vanishing point, the line doubling in length for five frames (2, 4, 8, 16, 32 pixels), then the field cuts to that step's own seeded system and the lines collapse in two frames. On the frame of the cut the convoy marker hops to its node on the route strip, the HUD relabels and the step opens in the list. Everything else on the page is quiet and exists to feed or explain that viewport.

- **The scene explains the content.** The copy says what three wings do at each step; the viewport shows who they are. Scouts are three darts ahead of the column, haulers are three blocky freighters in line, escorts are three forked ships on the flanks and at the rear. Each system has one distinctive body on the vanishing point, the thing the convoy is flying towards: Halfway Station (a hub with a green beacon), the Twin Lamps (a binary star), Ringfall (a ringed planet), Tallow Dock (an open docking frame with guide lights) and the Quiet Moon. The HUD's second line names it, so the drawing has a text alternative.
- **Every wing chip is a control.** The fifteen chips in the steps and the three in the legend are buttons. Hovering or focusing one moves that wing's three ships 6 viewport pixels ahead in two steps and puts a one-word callsign over each ship; the readout under the viewport names them and says what the wing does. A click, a tap or Enter keeps the wing out until it is pressed again. This is how a visitor learns which ships are which without a diagram.
- **Colour has jobs.** Petrol blue `#0B3350` is the ground, with a darker petrol for the sky, field grounds and the scrim, and a lighter one for quiet separators and night sides. Starlight is text, stars and jump streaks. Drift is secondary text, rules, outlines, dim stars and lit rock or metal. Mint, amber and pink belong to the scout, hauler and escort wings and appear only on that wing's ships, chips, callsigns, sign-up choice and constellation lines. Beacon green is the station and "go": station lights, the current step's numeral and marker bar, the convoy marker on the route, the HUD's square, the sign-up button, lit airlock cells and focus rings. Fault red is for errors only. No hue does two jobs, and the wings are also told apart by shape (their chips carry the ship's own drawing as an icon), never by colour alone.
- **Structure.** On desktop the text column is pinned for five step lengths (60vh each) while the viewport stays beside it; only the current step is open, the other four show numeral, title and time. After the fifth step the text column scrolls on to the sign-up and the viewport stays in place, so the confirmation's constellation happens next to the form. On phones and tablets the viewport and the route strip stick to the top at a reduced height (`--view-h`) and the five steps, all open, scroll under them; each step's own block chooses the step.
- **The stage pins only when it fits.** The script measures the text column with each step open; if any of them would be cut by the window (a narrow or short desktop, for example 1024 by 768) the stage is not pinned and the layout falls back to open steps beside a sticky viewport, with the same jumps. Nothing in the first screen is ever cut.
- **One jump, however far.** The step is read at the position the smooth scroll is heading for, not where it currently is, so a flick of the wheel names its destination at once. While the destination is still changing the streaks hold at full length, and the cut goes to the last step asked for. A route node, the "Sign up for Friday" link and the Tab key behave the same way.
- **Type.** Handjet at ELSH 2, ELGR 1 for the headline, the step numerals and the dialog title: square modules next to square pixels. Host Grotesk for everything that is read. The callsign labels and the HUD are DOM text over the canvas, on a plate of the sky colour, so they stay sharp and keep their contrast.
- **Flows.** The form (callsign, email, wing, headset) validates in JavaScript on submit, with messages that say how to fix each field, a reserved error line under each, focus on the first bad field, and the error cleared as soon as the value is valid. Success replaces the form with the entered values, the wing and the date of the next Friday (computed in the script: this Friday until 20:30, then next week's), moves focus to the confirmation heading and offers "Change these details", which restores the form with its values. Nothing is sent. The rules dialog is a native `showModal()` closable by Close, Escape and a press that starts and ends on the backdrop; focus returns to the button, Lenis is stopped while it is open, and it scrolls inside itself on short windows.
- **Semantics.** A `section` labelled by the `h2`, an ordered list of steps with `h3` titles, a `nav` with five real buttons (`aria-controls` the step, `aria-current="step"` on the current one), wing chips as toggle buttons with `aria-pressed`, a `fieldset` and `legend` for the wing choice, a `dl` for the confirmation, `role="status"` on the HUD and `aria-live` on the readout. The canvas and the callsign labels are `aria-hidden`: the HUD, the readout and the steps say everything they show.
- **Different from its neighbours in the ledger.** `story-speedrun-marathon` scrubs one continuous side-scrolling run with scroll; here scroll only chooses among five states and each change is a discrete, self-timed jump. `hero-racing-arcade` has speed streaks on a headline and a press; here the streak is the stars themselves, radiating from a vanishing point, and it is the transition between systems. `contact-hackerspace` cuts a chart between three ranges on scroll; here the cut is wrapped in the stretch and collapse, and the content is a seeded sky, not a map. `cta-indie-game` lights a lattice under the pointer anywhere; here the pointer only acts through the wing chips.

Deviations from the brief, and why:

- **The viewport stays beside the sign-up on desktop.** The brief pins the stage for the five steps and puts the form after it. The right column is sticky for the whole section instead, so the constellation plays next to the confirmation without scrolling anyone away from it. On phones the viewport is scrolled back under the top edge first, as briefed.
- **On phones the viewport comes before the headline.** It sticks from the first pixel of scroll, and the signature is on screen before anything is read. In the source the headline and the steps still come first, so the heading order and the Tab order follow the content.
- **The step is read ahead of the scroll.** With smooth wheel scrolling a jump starts when the scroll's destination crosses a boundary, slightly before the position does. That is what makes several boundaries one jump.
- **Constellation speed.** Stars move one pixel per frame along the grid when they are within 30 pixels of their cell; farther ones move two or three, so every star arrives within the 0.5 s. In place they are drawn at the brightest class (2 by 2 pixels) so the letters read as stars joined by lines.
- **Initials.** A callsign of several words gives one initial per word (Red Kite 9 gives RK9, Kestrel-7 gives K7); a one-word callsign gives its first three letters.
- **The link's underline also changes colour.** It rests in drift and cuts to starlight when it shoots, because 12 pixels of extension alone were too quiet in a still frame. The extension tapers in the two steps: 6px at 2px, then 6px more at 1px.
- **Reduced motion and no libraries follow scroll too.** The brief asks for the still field of step 1 without the libraries. The viewport does start there, but it also cuts to the step being read, because a sticky viewport showing step 1 beside step 4 would be wrong.
- **A jump is about 0.53 s, not 0.6 s** (seven 75 ms frames to the last change), plus any hold.

## Do not touch

- **Whole pixels.** The canvas is sized in viewport pixels (`ceil(box / scale)`), every coordinate is rounded, the context is never scaled and the element is `image-rendering: pixelated`. Do not draw at device resolution or add anti-aliased shapes.
- **The streaks are thin lines and the sky never changes colour.** One pixel wide, in the star's own colour, on the same `--sky`. Do not fill, flash or fade the frame: the brief's limit of three flashes a second is met because nothing large ever changes brightness.
- **One clock for the jump.** `--frame` (75 ms) times the stretch (five frames), the cut and the collapse (two frames). The marker, the HUD and the open step change in `arrive()`, on the cut, and nowhere else.
- **No parallax.** All stars drift at the same speed (one pixel every sixth of a second); the body and the ships stand still. Do not give the brightness classes different speeds.
- **Wing hues stay on their wing.** Do not use mint, amber or pink for emphasis, states or decoration, and do not colour the step text with them. Beacon green is never a wing.
- **The body sits on the vanishing point** (87% across, 60% down) and the formation is laid out in fractions of the viewport's size, so it fits a 156-pixel-wide phone viewport. The sky above the convoy (the top 36%) is kept free: the constellation is planned there, to the right of the HUD.
- **Stepped corners live on inner spans** (`.chip-rim`, `.chip-in`, `.hull`, `.pick-rim`, `.lock`), never on the `button`, `a` or `label`, so focus rings are not clipped. The hull button moves its `.hull` and `.plume`, not the `button`, so a press never slides the target out from under the pointer.
- **Hidden states exist only under `html.js:not(.go)`,** and only on `.cut` elements. The canvas is never hidden: with the libraries it opens on the full streaks, without them on the still.
- **Folded steps are clipped, not removed.** In the pinned layout the four closed steps keep their content in the accessibility tree and in the Tab order; focusing a chip inside one opens that step. Do not switch them to `display: none`.
- **Loops sleep.** The drift loop is on `gsap.ticker` only while the viewport is on screen and for `--d-idle` (20 s) after the last scroll, pointer movement, touch, key or focus change; the constellation's clock exists only while its stars move or hold. There is no CSS loop at all.
- **Accessibility.** Text contrast is computed: starlight 11.78:1, drift 6.47:1, the wing hues 9.40, 8.10 and 6.19:1, beacon 9.52:1 and fault 5.15:1 on petrol; all higher on the sky. The text fields drop the outline and use a 2px beacon border plus the two closed clamps as their focus ring; every other control keeps the 2px beacon outline at 3px offset. Targets are at least 44px in one dimension.

## Motion

House stepping: nothing eases. Canvas changes are whole-pixel redraws on a 75 ms frame (100 ms at load); CSS changes are `steps()` transitions of `clip-path` or `transform`; colour changes and the opening of a step are instant. Everything is built inside `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, width 960, height 560). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false` and `lagSmoothing(0)`, and `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`; touch keeps native scrolling. The opening waits for the fonts (`document.fonts.ready` plus a Handjet load, raced against 1.5 s) and for a visible page. Durations are read from the `--d-*` tokens and `--frame`.

## Adapting

1. **Find the journey.** The design needs a process whose steps are places or moments on a route, and two to four roles that act at every step. Rename the five steps in the HTML (`h3`, time, paragraph, one line per role) and in the route strip; the script reads the titles from the `h3`s and counts the steps itself. Keep each role line under about 45 characters so it stays on one line in the pinned layout.
2. **Map the colours onto the tokens, keeping the jobs.** `--petrol` is the ground, `--sky` a darker and `--lane` a lighter version of it. `--starlight` and `--drift` are the two text and star tones. One hue per role (`--scout`, `--hauler`, `--escort`), each at 4.5:1 or better on the ground, and never used elsewhere. `--beacon` is the single "go" colour and the focus ring; `--fault` is errors only. The canvas reads all of them from the tokens.
3. **Redraw the roles.** `WINGS` at the top of the script holds each role's name, three callsigns, one line for the readout, its ship as rows of `#` and `.` (nose to the right; the same drawing becomes the chip icon) and its three places in the formation, in units of 8.5% of the viewport's width and 15% of its height. Add a `[data-wing="..."]` rule for a new role's hue.
4. **Redraw the bodies.** `SYSTEMS` lists one body and one HUD line per step; `BODY` draws each body in rectangles around the vanishing point. Keep them within about 20 pixels of it, so they fit the phone viewport, and use beacon green only on things that mean "station". Keep each HUD line under about 24 characters: the constellation is planned to the right of the HUD, shrinks its cell pitch from 6 to 3 pixels to fit, and drops its third letter only if a 3-pixel pitch still does not fit.
5. **Copy.** The H2 is two lines at up to 84px in a 5/12 column: about 25 characters. The lede is two or three lines and carries the two text links. Each step: one or two words, a time, a paragraph of about 150 characters, one line per role.
6. **The sign-up.** `RULES` holds the validation and the messages, `nextFriday()` the date in the confirmation (change the weekday and the cut-off time there) and `initials()` what the constellation spells. The 5x7 alphabet covers A to Z and 0 to 9.
7. **Retime or remove.** `--frame` sets the pace of a jump and `--step-len` how much scroll a step takes. To remove the pin, set `pin` to false in `setPin()`: the open-steps layout is complete on its own. To remove the drift, delete the `driftTick` ticker; the constellation, delete `fx.success` (the still drawing in the submit handler remains); the load sequence, set `opening` to false.
8. **In a framework,** render the markup statically and port the script as one effect that returns the `gsap.matchMedia` cleanup (it removes listeners and tickers, kills delayed calls and the ScrollTrigger, destroys Lenis and leaves the viewport as a still).

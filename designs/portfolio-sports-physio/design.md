## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the gap closes.** Section `#recovery` is a pinned replay of one ACL recovery: the median quadriceps strength of 64 athletes after reconstruction, operated leg as a share of the other leg, tested every two weeks. A tall scroll track holds a CSS-sticky stage (SC3), and scroll moves one value, `week`, from 0 to 40 (`scrub: true`, `ease: none`; the last stage holds about 15% longer so week 40 can be read at rest). `render(week)` draws everything from it. The curve and its middle-half band are revealed left to right with `clip-path: inset(0 Xpx 0 0)`, so the line's leading edge always sits on the current week. A head dot rides the curve from interpolated data. The big tabular week counter updates. The operated-leg bar grows toward a yellow tick at 90% while the other leg's ink bar stays at 100%. Six rail segments fill, and when the stage changes, the caption swaps (TR1). The single beat is the forward crossing of week 32, where the curve meets the yellow 90% line. The chart's floor tape takes one press and a note rises, "Week 32: strength back to 90% of the other leg." Scrolling back below 32 hides it instantly. "Skip the replay" jumps to `#injuries`, and the six rail buttons scroll to each stage, so nobody has to scrub. The stage pins only when motion is allowed and the screen is at least 600px tall; otherwise it is a static, fully drawn chart reading week 38 (the median return to sport), with the week 32 note visible and the six stages as an ordered list.

- **The hero lays the line the replay has to cross.** The hero is typographic, and its only device is the yellow floor tape laid across the page on load, labelled "The return line: 90% of your other side". The same tape is the 90% line in the chart, the tick on the strength bar and the ground of the week 32 note. The page tells one story with one bold element.
- **Two tapes, two fixed meanings.** Cyan kinesiology tape (`--c-kinesio`) means you, and what is current or selected: the operated leg's curve and bar, our middle-half bands, the current nav section, the current stage on the rail, the week tab, the selected injury and its detail panel, and every focus ring. It is never spent as hover decoration: nav links, contact rows, menu links and form focus use the ink underline, and the nav strip never follows the pointer. Yellow hop-test floor tape (`--c-floortape`) means the bar to clear: the return line, the 90% tick, the week 32 note and form errors, which are a bar you have not cleared yet. Yellow against the blue paper is only 1.25:1, so it is always drawn with 1.5px ink edges and always carries an ink label (9.9:1 on yellow). Cyan is 3.28:1, so it carries graphics, focus and large marks only, never text. Kinesio strips use 3px tape-cut corners (physios round tape corners so they do not peel) and a faint woven texture from two repeating gradients at 6%.
- **Typography from instruments, one family.** Nunito Sans at 122 to 125% width and 820 to 860 weight is the voice (H1, H2, wordmark, week counter); 100% and 420 is body and form; 87.5% and 520 is the measurement voice (chart ticks, stage names, table heads, labels). Its softened terminals suit a clinic that talks to injured amateurs. The figures are tabular by default: at 125% and 860, "1111", "0000" and "7777" measure the same width with or without `tabular-nums`, which is declared anyway so the counter and readouts never jitter. No monospace, no all-caps, no eyebrows.
- **Charts are honest.** The injury chart is linear in weeks (0 to 52), so short injuries look short, and every median is printed in a right-hand column so the ankle and hamstring rows still read. Each row compares our middle half (cyan bar) and median (ink dot) with the published range for the same injury (graphite bracket). The ACL curve is straight segments between real two-weekly tests, which is why the head dot and the crosshair sit exactly on it. Both charts have a table behind a disclosure, and every reading from hover is also reachable by keyboard, tap or the table.
- **The results band is a sentence, not stat tiles.** The four figures (412, 379, 92%, 29) are derived from `OUTCOMES`, count up once inside one plain sentence, and get an ink underline that lays from the left. They are not cyan because they are not you. The re-injured number stays in the sentence on purpose. The large week number in the stage is a live readout of the replay, not a stat hero.
- **Defaults avoided on purpose.** The ground is the blue of clinical couch roll (`#E3EDF3`), not cream; ink is a chosen vinyl blue-slate (`#1B2A31`). The detail panel is a ruled block marked by a left kinesio strip because it belongs to the selected row; the booking result simply takes the form's place. There are no radii or shadows on panels: buttons and inputs take 6px and tape takes 3px. No word in a headline is accented, and no text effect runs on headings. Section dividers are couch-roll perforations because each section is a new sheet. The only glyph on a link is the down chevron on "Watch an ACL recovery", which really goes down the page. Stages are numbered because they are a true sequence.

## Motion

Tier: detailed. One easing family: CSS `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)` (= `expo.out`) and `--ease-wipe: cubic-bezier(.87,0,.13,1)` (= `expo.inOut`). GSAP uses `expo.out` for entrances, swaps and counts, `expo.inOut` for wipes and stage jumps, and `none` for the replay clock. TR1's out phase keeps `power2.in`, per its recipe. Everything runs inside one `gsap.matchMedia` with `motion`, `reduce`, `fine` and `pin` (`(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) and (min-height: 600px)`) conditions. Each context owns an AbortController, and cleanup aborts it, removes the ticker callback, restores `lagSmoothing(500, 33)`, destroys Lenis, removes `html.pin`, returns the stage to its static state and puts the instant effects back. Lenis is `{lerp: .1, smoothWheel: true, autoRaf: false, anchors: false}` on `gsap.ticker`, wired with `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`, and stopped while the menu is open. Hidden start states exist only under `html.js`, which is added only when GSAP, ScrollTrigger and Lenis all loaded. Charts, tables, contact rows, the crosshair and the stage buttons are built and wired in plain JS first, so a blocked CDN leaves the complete static stage (drawn to week 40, reading week 38).

## Do not touch

- **The two-tape meaning system** and its contrast rule. Cyan is the operated side, the middle halves, what is current or selected, and focus; it is never hover decoration and never text colour. Yellow is the bar to clear, always with ink edges and an ink label.
- **The hero return line reused as the replay threshold.** If you change 90%, change `RETURN`, the hero label, the bar tick label and the week 32 copy together. The self-check `console.assert` fails if `OUTCOMES` no longer sums to 412 / 379 / 29 or the LSI curve no longer first reaches 90 at week 32.
- **The clip-path reveal tied to weeks,** not path length, so the line's head is always on the current week. The curve is straight segments between two-weekly tests so interpolation matches the drawing. `render(week)` sets the data clip, head dot, tab, operated bar, rail fills, counter and stage, and writes text only when the floored week or stage changes. Do not animate those parts separately, and never tween the week directly while pinned: anything that sets a week scrolls to it.
- **The pin contract.** The stage pins only under `html.pin` (motion allowed and at least 600px tall). "Skip the replay" (`#injuries`) and the six rail buttons, which scroll to each stage's first week, mean nobody has to scrub. Without the pin the stage is static: drawn to week 40, counter and caption reading week 38, the week 32 note visible, and the ordered list `#stage-1` to `#stage-6` shown under the rail (it stays in the DOM, visually hidden, while pinned). The chart (`role="group"`, `tabindex="0"`) reads drawn weeks only and never changes the replay. The weekly table sits behind "Show the weekly numbers", after the track. Touch reading uses `touch-action: pan-y` and waits for 6px of horizontal travel, so vertical swipes still scroll the replay.
- **No dead ends.** Every in-page link is an anchor to an existing id handled by NV6 with focus moved; the form handles submit in JS and shows a result with a way back; WhatsApp, email and social links open with `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`.
- **Robustness.** Without the libraries, `html.js` is never added and the page is complete and interactive in plain JS with the static stage. SVG marks in the injury chart use percentage coordinates, so their build animates CSS variables (`--sx`, `--s`) rather than GSAP's SVG transform math; keep it that way or the dots drift.

## Adapting

Change the `:root` tokens first, then the three data objects at the top of the script, then the copy.

- **Data.** `CONTACT`: one line per network `{label, href, text}`; delete a key to hide its row and its send button (deleting `whatsapp` or `email` also removes that reply option; keep at least one of the two, because the request is sent through it); add a network in one line. `OUTCOMES`: one object per injury, weeks to return to sport, with `lsi` arrays (median, p25, p75 every two weeks) on the injury that gets the replay. `STAGES`: `{name, short, from, to, exit}`; the rail, list and caption follow it. The results sentence numbers are derived; edit the sentence words if the meaning of a figure changes.
- **Another practice or protocol.** A hamstring or Achilles programme works the same way: replace the `lsi` arrays (or any "% of the other side" measure such as hop distance), the stages, `MAXWK` and the 90% threshold. Keep a real, measured criterion; the design argues "return when the numbers say so".
- **Retiming or removing motion.** CSS durations live in `--d-quick`, `--d-out`, `--d-swap`, `--d-in` and `--d-open`. Scroll length per stage is `--per-stage` (80svh, 55svh on touch), and the pinned chart height is `100svh` minus the header and `--stage-chrome`. GSAP literals are in the matchMedia callback: the load timeline offsets (0, .15, .5, 1.2), the last-stage hold (`HOLD = 1.15`), the rail scroll (.9 s), the crosshair `quickTo` (.3), the TR1 timings in `swap`, and the press in `beat`. To never pin, drop the `pin` condition; the static stage then shows everywhere. To drop the magnet, delete the `if (fine)` block. To drop the count-up or the injury build, delete their blocks; the static values and drawn chart remain. Never remove the reduced-motion branch.
- **Layout.** Above 860px: hero H1 spans 10 columns, lede and the actions under it 6; the recovery heading and note sit above the track; the stage is 4 columns of skip link and readout against 8 of bars and chart, with the rail (and, when static, the stage list) across all 12 in six aligned columns; injury rows take 8 columns and the detail panel 4; the form takes 7 and contacts 4. At 860px and below everything stacks, the counter and caption sit side by side, stage names move from the rail into the list, and the week 32 note moves to the empty lower-right corner of the chart. Below 600px injury rows wrap to two lines and the chart is 300px tall. Injury names up to about 32 characters fit one line on desktop.

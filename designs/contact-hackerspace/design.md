  - A place that is hard to find, where the way to the door matters as much as the address and can be told at two or three scales (the town, the block, the last 100 metres).

## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the map is a radar, and the radar pings.** Molo 4 is at the seaward end of a pier, in a pilot station, so the section finds its door the way a harbour finds a ship. The chart on the right is a round scope, 161 chart pixels across (each one 2, 3 or 4 screen pixels), with the door at its centre. Nothing on it sweeps and nothing follows the pointer. A ping is an event: a thin amber ring leaves a point and grows in twelve whole-pixel steps to the range in use, and everything it crosses answers. The door, the tram stop, the ferry landing, the bike racks and the car park are small squares that rest dim, light at full strength when the ring reaches them, show their name and their distance from where the ping started, and step back down to rest over 1.5 s. The walking route and the tram line are single-pixel dots that light the same way, so a ping from the door draws the way to the tram stop as it travels. Everything else on the page is quiet and belongs to this instrument.

- **Three ranges, three drawings, one harbour.** The script holds the harbour once, in metres from the door (a stepped coast, three piers, a ferry pier, a breakwater, a canal, 75 m street blocks, the pilot station, the route). It rasterises that at 25 m, 6.25 m and 1.25 m per pixel into the 2 km, 500 m and 100 m charts. Each range shows only the blips it can resolve: at 2 km the door, the tram stop, the ferry landing and the car park; at 500 m the door, the tram stop and the long bike rack; at 100 m the door, the bike hoops, the five bollards, the landing steps and the pilot boat. That is the order in which a visitor needs them.
- **Scroll changes the range, not the picture's position.** The chart is sticky beside the copy. The copy has three parts with a rule and a small amber note each: "Reach us" (chart at 2 km), "Tell us what you are bringing" (500 m), "Find the door" (100 m). When a part's top edge passes 60% of the window the chart cuts to that range, the four ring labels and the Handjet numeral change, and the door pings once. Three range buttons do the same by hand and show the range in use.
- **The load teaches the instrument.** The chart starts as bare rings. One ping leaves the door, and the coast, the piers and the blips exist only inside the ring. The headline's three lines cut in when the ring is at one third, two thirds and full range, and the rest of the page cuts in when it ends. After 1.2 s the visitor has seen what a ping does.
- **Colour has jobs.** The ground is the oxblood of a red working light (`--watch` #3B1014), which is what a night watch uses to keep its eyes; water is the same hue darker, land and buildings the same hue lighter. Amber (`--sweep`) is the radar's own light and nothing else: ping rings, range labels, the range in use, the primary button, focus. Lime is the door and the way to it. Cyan is public transport: the tram line, its stop, the ferry. Lilac is where you leave wheels. Pink is the visitor and appears only after they write. The fault tone appears only on errors. Bone is text and the marks that belong to text (button rims, the Morse under a hovered link); dust is secondary text, the chart grid and resting rules.
- **Text never sits on the drawing.** Names and distances are DOM text on chips of the water tone, placed beside each blip; the four ring labels run down the south-west diagonal, which is open water at every range. The range numeral, the north mark and the scale note ("1 px = 25 m") live in the corners the round scope leaves free.
- **The form is on the chart too.** A valid message adds a pink blip with the visitor's name at the landward edge, the door pings, the ring lights the blip, and the readout says "Message logged for Tuesday 24 November" (the date they gave, or the next Tuesday). The confirmation echoes the email, the topic, the day and the message; "Write another message" brings the form back with the name and address kept, and takes the blip off.
- **Every small control answers in the radar's vocabulary.** A button's outline comes back as two echoes and they collapse on contact. A link's underline turns into the Morse code of its first letter. A field stands on a strip of noise that settles into a flat line when the value is good. The dialog is uncovered like a clock hand going round. None of these moves more than a few pixels, and all of them are hard cuts.
- **Semantics.** One `<section aria-labelledby>` with an `h2`, three `h3` parts, a `ul` of four channels, a `dl` of facts, a `<form novalidate>` with labelled fields and reserved error lines, and a native `<dialog>` whose title is an `h2` and whose five directions are an `ol`. Both canvases are `aria-hidden`. Their text alternative is a visually hidden list of buttons, one per blip, whose names give the distance and bearing from the door ("Tram stop, 345 m from the door, bearing 108 degrees. Ping from here."), plus the `aria-live` readout under the scope and the directions themselves.
- **No dead ends.** The four channels are real `mailto:`, `tel:` and https links that open in a new tab with `rel="noopener"` and a hidden "(opens in a new tab)". "Write before you come" jumps to the form. Both directions buttons, the Door legend button and the Door blip button open the same dialog, which closes with Close, Escape or a press that starts and ends on the backdrop, and gives focus back to whatever opened it.

**Different from its neighbours in the ledger.** cta-indie-game also has a harbour and a canvas that answers the pointer, but there a field of cells lights in a wake under the hand; here nothing follows the hand, pings are discrete rings from a point, and what lights is a short list of places with names. story-speedrun-marathon aims a lantern beam at the pointer and pricing-net-cafe runs a refresh line; this radar has no beam and no line. team-fighting-game puts lock-on brackets on a hovered button; the echoes here are two whole rectangles outside the tile that collapse on press. The field strips of cta-indie-game (sparks stepping in), hero-racing-arcade (marching dashes) and pricing-net-cafe (a scanline) all answer focus; this one answers validity, and it settles instead of moving.

Deviations from the brief, and why:

- **The ring is drawn on a second canvas.** A ring 2 screen pixels thick cannot be drawn in a picture whose pixels are 2 to 4 screen pixels wide. The picture stays on the 161 x 161 canvas (`image-rendering: pixelated`); the rings are the outline of the same midpoint-algorithm disc, drawn at screen resolution on a transparent canvas above it, so they step on chart pixels and stay 2 px thin.
- **The scope is round inside the square canvas.** The picture is clipped to a pixel disc of radius 80 whose row widths come from the same midpoint algorithm as the rings. The square's corners carry the range numeral, the north mark and the scale note.
- **161 chart pixels, not 160,** so the door is one whole pixel at the centre and the three scales are exact (25, 6.25 and 1.25 m per pixel).
- **Each range shows its own subset of the six blips.** At 2 km the two bike racks would sit on top of the door. Focusing or pressing a blip that the range in use cannot show switches to the range that can.
- **The route and the tram line light as well as the blips.** They are 1 px dots in the door's lime and the transit cyan, inside those hues' jobs, and they make a ping from the door read as "this way".
- **The echoes are 2 px and 1 px thick** (at 3 px and 6 px outside the button), heavier then lighter, so the hover reads as an echo and not as a double border.
- **The Morse is bone, not amber.** Amber is reserved for the radar's own light.
- **Clicking Door opens the directions, not only Enter.** The legend's Door button and the hidden Door blip button are real buttons, so a click, a tap and Enter all do the same thing.
- **With reduced motion, scrolling still changes the range.** The cut is a state change, not movement; the ping that follows is an instant highlight.
- **The dialog's clock wipe is CSS,** so it also plays when the libraries are blocked (it is switched off by `prefers-reduced-motion`). Everything else that moves needs the motion layer.
- **Short, wide windows move the controls beside the scope.** At 1280 x 600 a chart with its controls underneath would have to drop to 2 screen pixels per chart pixel; the script instead keeps 3 and stands the readout, range buttons and legend in a 184 px column to its left. Tablets get the same column on the right.
- **On phones the chart is not sticky,** so the range still follows the scroll but the chart is usually out of view while it does. The confirmation carries a "See yourself on the chart" link there.

## Do not touch

- **The door is the centre.** Every coordinate in the script is in metres from the door (x east, y north), the scope is centred on chart pixel 80, 80, and the range rings, the readout's distances and bearings, and the automatic pings all assume it. Do not re-centre a range for a nicer composition.
- **One harbour, three scales.** The three drawings, the blip positions, the route dots and the distances in the dialog come from the same constants (`COAST`, `PIERS`, `ROUTE`, `BLIPS`). If you move something, move it there, and re-add the five distances in the dialog: they are the lengths of the `ROUTE` segments.
- **Whole chart pixels.** `--k` is 2, 3 or 4, chosen by the script; the canvas is never scaled by a fraction. Rings, the reveal mask and the scope edge are midpoint-algorithm discs. Do not anti-alias, round or blur any of them, and do not give the chart a `border-radius`.
- **Pings, not a sweep.** No rotating line, no beam toward the pointer, no glow. A moving pointer sends at most one ping every 0.7 s and at most three rings are alive.
- **Colour tokens are six-digit hex.** The chart reads `--watch`, `--deep`, `--land`, `--block`, `--dust`, `--sweep` and the four blip hues and mixes its brightness steps from them (`--lit-1` to `--lit-rest`, `--grid-a`). `rgb()` or named colours will break the mixing.
- **Each hue keeps its job.** Amber only for the radar's light, the range in use, the primary button and focus. Pink only for the visitor. The fault tone only for errors. Do not colour a heading or a rule with a blip hue.
- **Labels are DOM chips on `--deep`,** never canvas text and never transparent. That is what keeps 12 px text at AA over land and blocks.
- **Stepped corners live on inner spans** (`.face`, `.inner`, `.rg-face`, `.rg-in`, `.dlg-rim`, `.dlg`), never on the `<a>`, `<button>` or `<dialog>`, so focus rings and echoes are not clipped. The clock wipe is on `.dlg-wipe`, outside the notched rim, for the same reason.
- **Hidden states exist only under `html.js:not(.go)`,** and the first ping replaces them with inline states in the same task before it adds `.go`. With the CDN blocked the class is never added: the 500 m chart is fully drawn, range, legend and blip buttons redraw at once, pings are instant highlights, and the form and dialog work.
- **The loop sleeps.** The chart's frame function is on `gsap.ticker` only while a ring is travelling or a blip is still lit, redraws only when a step changes, and is dropped when the scope leaves the screen. Do not add an idle ping.
- **The form panel and the confirmation share one grid cell,** so nothing below moves when one replaces the other, and the scroll positions that switch the range stay where they were.
- **Accessibility.** The readout is `aria-live="polite"`. Every blip is a button in a visually hidden list; keyboard focus on one draws an amber square round it on the chart. Error lines are reserved, `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` are set and cleared with the message, and focus moves to the first bad field. The confirmation heading takes focus (`tabindex="-1"`). Lenis is stopped while the dialog is open.

## Motion

House stepping: nothing eases. Every change is a hard cut on a clock: ping rings every 70 ms, the first ping every 100 ms, echoes 60 ms apart, Morse elements 40 ms apart, dialog steps 40 ms apart, light steps 500 ms apart. Only canvas pixels, `visibility` and `clip-path` change over time; colour changes are instant. The motion layer is built inside `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce, fine pointer). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false` and `lagSmoothing(0)`, and `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`; touch keeps native scrolling. The first ping waits for the fonts (`document.fonts.ready` plus a Handjet load, raced against 1.5 s) and for a visible page.

## Adapting

1. **Decide what the instrument is for your subject.** The pings only make sense if there is one place to find and a handful of things around it. Rename the readout and the legend to match. If there is nothing to find, do not keep the radar as decoration.
2. **Map your colours onto the tokens, keeping the jobs.** `--watch` is the ground; `--deep`, `--land` and `--block` are the same hue darker and lighter (the chart needs all three). `--sweep` is the instrument's own light and the primary button. Give each kind of blip one hue and add it to `KINDS`, the legend markup and the `[data-kind]` rules for label chips and legend swatches. Keep every colour token as six-digit hex and re-check contrast: text hues need 4.5:1 on `--watch` and on `--deep`.
3. **Redraw the place.** Edit the constants at the top of the script, in metres from your door: `COAST` (a staircase of shore positions), `PIERS`, `BREAKWATER`, `CANAL`, `STATION`, `ROUTE` and `BLIPS` (`on` lists the ranges that show a blip, `tag` the side its label opens on at each range). Change `RANGES` if 2 km, 500 m and 100 m are the wrong scales; the ring labels and the rounding of distances are in the same objects. The 100 m details (mooring posts, bollards, crane rails, landing steps, the boat) are a few lines in the scene builder and can simply be deleted.
4. **Rewrite the directions from the route.** The dialog's five distances are the lengths of the `ROUTE` segments (25, 95, 275, 35, 20 m) and the facts list repeats the total (450 m) and the straight-line distances the chart reports.
5. **Copy.** The h2 is three lines at 88 px in a 5.3 em measure: keep it under 36 characters. Each channel has one line saying when it is answered. Part headings are short; the amber note beside each names the range that part sets (`data-range` on the part).
6. **Form.** Fields and messages are in `F`; topics and the phrases the confirmation uses are in the `<select>` and `TOPICS`. The Tuesday rule lives in `F.when` and `nextTuesday()`. Wire the submit handler to your endpoint where the comment says nothing leaves the page.
7. **Retime or remove motion.** All durations are `--d-*` tokens read by CSS and the script. To remove hover pings, delete the `pointermove` listener. To stop scroll from changing the range, delete the `ScrollTrigger.create` line. To drop the first ping, call `chart.full()` and add `.go` straight away. The dialog wipe is two keyframe rules. Without any of them the chart is still a correct, clickable map.
8. **In a framework,** render the markup statically, run the plain part of the script once (chart, form, dialog), and port the motion part as one effect that returns the `gsap.matchMedia` cleanup (it removes listeners, the ticker function, the IntersectionObserver, the ScrollTriggers and Lenis).

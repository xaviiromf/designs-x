A pocket computer you solder yourself.

Moth-1 is an open-hardware cyberdeck kit from Dusklight Labs, two people in a workshop in Tallinn. Six modules, 62 through-hole joints and about four hours with an iron give you a Linux computer that fits a coat pocket.

moth-1 readyready

  1. 1 Battery sled

    Two cells, about nine hours

    The sled holds two 18650 cells side by side and slides out of the bottom of the deck without tools. Its charge board fills them over USB-C and cuts the power before they run too low.

    2 × 18650 cells (not included), about 9 hours of typing, a full charge in 3 hours

  2. 2 Carrier board

    A carrier for the compute module you choose

    The carrier takes any credit-card-size compute module with the common 200-pin edge connector. It breaks the module out to two USB-C ports, a microSD slot and the display ribbon, so a faster module later is one latch away.

    2 × USB-C, microSD, display ribbon, 5 V at 3 A from the sled

  3. 3 Expansion bay

    An empty bay with 40 pins

    A corner of the deck is left open on purpose. Forty GPIO pins run along its edge, and its cover is a flat printed plate you can cut for a knob, a camera or a second radio.

    40 GPIO pins on a 2 × 20 header, 52 × 38 × 14 mm of free space

  4. 4 Keyboard

    Forty-eight keys in a straight grid

    The keyboard is a 40% ortholinear board: four rows of twelve keys, all the same size. Every switch sits in a hot-swap socket, so you change tactile for linear with a puller, not an iron.

    48 keys, hot-swap sockets, open firmware with 4 layers

  5. 5 Screen

    A screen as wide as the keyboard

    The panel is 7.9 inches and 1280 by 400 pixels, which is two terminal windows side by side at a size you can read. It sits directly above the keys, so your eyes travel one hand's width from typing to text.

    7.9 in, 1280 × 400, IPS, 400 nits

  6. 6 Mesh radio

    A mesh radio for places without signal

    The LoRa board sends short text messages from deck to deck, and every deck passes them on to the next. Two decks reach each other across about 3 km of open ground, with no network and no subscription.

    LoRa on 868 MHz, about 3 km line of sight, antenna on the top edge

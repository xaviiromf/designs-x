1 Mill and mash in

Tuesday 6 October, 06:40

400 kg of grain goes through the roller mill: 360 kg Maris Otter, 25 kg wheat malt, 15 kg light crystal. The rollers crack each husk without shredding it, because the husks become the filter later. Grist and 1,080 litres of water at 72 °C meet in the mash tun and settle at 66 °C for an hour while enzymes turn starch into sugar.

2 Lauter and sparge

Tuesday, 07:45

We open the tap under the false bottom and pump the cloudy first runnings back over the grain bed until they run clear. Then 1,600 L of water at 76 °C rinses the bed for 90 minutes, and 2,250 L of sweet wort reaches the kettle at 1.043. The grain keeps about 400 L, and the spent grain goes to a farm in Ryton the same afternoon.

3 Boil and whirlpool

Tuesday, 09:30

A rolling boil for 60 minutes drives off 150 L of steam and sets the bitterness with 2 kg of Columbus. With less water, the gravity climbs from 1.043 to 1.046. Then the heat goes off, the wort spins, and 10 kg of Citra and Mosaic steep at 80 °C for 30 minutes. That is where the grapefruit and mango come from.

4 Knock out and pitch

Tuesday, 11:15

The wort runs through the plate chiller, from 80 °C to 19 °C in 45 minutes, and into fermenter 3. About 120 L stays behind in the hop bed. On the way in it takes oxygen and 20 litres of our house ale yeast, harvested from batch 211.

5 Ferment and dry hop

Days 1 to 7, Wednesday 7 to Tuesday 13 October

Within a day the yeast throws up a foam head, the krausen, a hand deep. Gravity drops from 1.046 to 1.011 as sugar becomes alcohol and CO2. On day 4 we add 12 kg more Citra and Mosaic. On day 6 we raise it to 21 °C so the yeast clears up the buttery diacetyl it made.

6 Condition and can

Days 8 to 16, Wednesday 14 to Thursday 22 October

We crash fermenter 3 to 2 °C for four days. Yeast and hops drop into the cone, taking 120 L with them, and the beer turns bright. The other 1,880 L moves to bright tank 1 and is carbonated to 2.4 volumes. This morning the canning line filled 4,272 cans of 440 ml.

Questions for finals weekend

Kiln Street Open Autumn Finals, Friday 13 to Sunday 15 November 2026 at Kiln Street Arena, Sheffield. Fourteen answers from the info desk, from ticket names to controller checks.

Tickets

Sorted before you travel.

Weekend pass £64, all three days. It gets you into any unreserved seat in blocks A to F on Friday, Saturday and Sunday, with re-entry on your wristband.

Day tickets are £28 for Friday (quarter-finals), £34 for Saturday (semi-finals and the Iron Circuit top 8) and £34 for Sunday (both grand finals). Floor standing in front of the main stage is Sunday only and sold separately at £12.

Free name change until Thu 23:59. You can change it once, at no charge, until Thursday 12 November at 23:59, with the Change name link in your ticket email.

After that, the name must match your photo ID at the door, and the box office cannot change it on the day.

Under-16s need an adult. Rift Protocol is rated 16 and the broadcast shows realistic weapon violence, so anyone under 16 needs an adult (18 or over) in the same booking. Under-5s go free on a lap, with no seat of their own.

The Iron Circuit open bracket is for players aged 13 and over.

Doors

Friday from 16:00. Saturday and Sunday from 10:30.

Doors Fri 16:00, Sat/Sun 10:30. The first match starts an hour later: 17:00 on Friday, 11:30 on Saturday and Sunday.

Gate 2 on Kiln Street is the quickest way in. Gate 4 is for wristband re-entry only. On Sunday, plan your way home around a late finish: see Will Sunday’s grand final run late?

One bag, up to 40 x 30 x 15 cm. It is checked at the gate. Banners up to 1 x 2 m with no poles, power banks up to 20,000 mAh and sealed water up to 500 ml are fine.

Not allowed: air horns, laser pointers, outside food (except baby food and food you need for medical reasons) and cameras with detachable lenses. Your own controller for the open bracket is welcome: see Can I bring my own controller?

Step-free from Gate 2. Every block can be reached without steps. Wheelchair spaces with a companion seat are in row 1 of blocks C and E; book them through the info desk.

Ear defenders are free at the info desk. The quiet room on concourse level 1 has a live feed and no crowd noise, and the main screen captions every caster segment.

Lights go down at 17:00 on Friday.Lights go down at 17:00 on Friday. Everything below happens inside the bowl.

Watching

Inside the bowl, blocks A to F.

Re-entry until 19:00, Gate 4. Keep your wristband on and scan out and back in at Gate 4, as often as you like until 19:00 each day.

A lost wristband is replaced once at the info desk, with photo ID.

Peaks around 95 dB. The loudest moments are match points and caster hype. Foam earplugs are free at every bar, and the quiet room shows the broadcast without the crowd.

Clips yes, live streams no. Photos and short clips for your own socials are fine, with no flash toward the stages. Live-streaming the match feed is not allowed, because of broadcast rights.

The free Wi-Fi, KSA-Guest, is capped at 5 Mbps.

Signings Sat 14:00, level 1. They run from 14:00 to 15:30 on Saturday on concourse level 1. Queue tickets are handed out at the info desk from 12:00, one per wristband, 300 in total.

Curfew 23:00, last tram 23:40. A best-of-five can run past 22:00. The arena has a hard curfew at 23:00: if the final reaches it, the deciding map is finished and the trophy ceremony moves online.

The last tram from the Kiln Street stop leaves at 23:40.

Playing

Iron Circuit open bracket, side stage.

Entry £10, pools Sat 10:00. Register online until Friday at 18:00, or at the player desk on concourse level 1 until Saturday at 09:30.

Entry is £10 on top of a Saturday or weekend ticket, capped at 256 players, double elimination. Pools start at 10:00 on Saturday on the side stage.

Own pads checked at the desk. Wired pads and arcade sticks are checked at the player desk before your first match. Wireless pads must be paired by the tournament organiser, and turbo or macro buttons are not allowed.

Spare pads are kept at the desk in case yours fails.

Three calls, then a loss. You are called three times over ten minutes, on the side-stage screen and over the PA. After the third call the match is recorded as a loss and you drop to the losers’ side. Miss a second call and you are out.

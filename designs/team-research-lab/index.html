Fourteen people trying to bring the bull kelp back.

In 2014 the purple urchins took over the reefs off Sorrel Point and the bull kelp went with them. We study why, and what it takes to grow it back: divers counting urchins, technicians raising kelp in seawater tanks, and modellers following spores on the current.

Profiles

Dr Lucía Ochoa

Associate professor and principal investigator

Nereocystis luetkeana

Has dived the same 30 m transect at Tanbark Cove every September since 2009.

Lucía started the lab in 2012, two years before the urchins arrived, so her surveys are the only before-and-after record for this stretch of coast. She holds the urchin removal permit with the state and the tribal fisheries office. On Thursdays she answers questions from people thinking about a PhD.

Last paper Ochoa L, Quintero R, Vlahović T (2025). Ten years of urchin barrens at Sorrel Point. Marine Ecology Progress Series 741: 55–71.

Projects Kelp restoration, Urchin barrens, Spore dispersal, Heatwave tolerance

Dr Yaw Boakye

Postdoctoral researcher

Nereocystis luetkeana

Couples a coastal ocean model to the day kelp releases its spores. Does not dive.

Yaw came from physical oceanography and still thinks in currents first. He runs the circulation model on the university cluster and checks it against the drifters Ife releases off the pier. His question is how far a spore can travel before it is too old to settle.

Last paper Boakye Y, Ochoa L (2026). Spore release timing and connectivity among bull kelp reefs. Limnology and Oceanography 71: 812–826.

Projects Spore dispersal

Dr Hana Kirschbaum

Postdoctoral researcher

Nereocystis luetkeana gametophytes

Keeps 1,200 kelp cultures alive at 12, 14, 16 and 18 °C.

Hana grows the microscopic stage of the kelp life cycle in flasks under blue light. She measures which cultures still produce young kelp after six weeks at heatwave temperatures. Her crosses are the starting stock for Ayaan's breeding work.

Last paper Kirschbaum H, Siddiqui A, Whitecloud J (2026). Thermal limits of bull kelp gametophytes from three populations. Journal of Phycology 62: 301–315.

Projects Heatwave tolerance

Rafael Quintero

PhD candidate, year 5

Strongylocentrotus purpuratus

Has measured 41,000 urchins with calipers, one test at a time.

Rafael's thesis asks whether the urchins in the barrens are starving or just waiting. He compares test size and gonad weight inside and outside the removal plots every quarter. He submits in spring 2027 and is writing chapter four.

Mei-Lin Zhao

PhD student, year 3

Nereocystis luetkeana

Outplants juvenile kelp on rope lines at 8 m and counts who survives.

Mei-Lin seeds rope with young kelp in the seawater lab and ties it to anchors on the reef in early spring. Every month she counts what is still attached and what the urchins have eaten. Her plots at Tanbark Cove are the ones you can see from the pier at low tide.

Last paper Zhao M-L, Vlahović T, Ochoa L (2026). Grazing pressure on outplanted bull kelp inside an urchin barren. Marine Ecology Progress Series 760: 41–58.

Projects Kelp restoration

meilin.zhao@sorrelpoint.example

Ayaan Siddiqui

PhD student, year 2

Nereocystis luetkeana

Breeds heat-tolerant kelp crosses from wild parents.

Ayaan crosses gametophytes from the warmest and the coolest reefs on the coast and grows their offspring at 17 °C. He is looking for families that keep growing through a marine heatwave. The best crosses go to Mei-Lin's rope lines next spring.

Last paper Siddiqui A, Kirschbaum H (2026). Heritability of heat tolerance in bull kelp sporophytes. Preprint, bioRxiv.

Projects Heatwave tolerance

ayaan.siddiqui@sorrelpoint.example

Imogen Tallis

PhD student, year 1

Nereocystis luetkeana

Uses kinship in kelp DNA to test where the model says spores came from.

Imogen collects tissue from the kelp that recolonises cleared plots and genotypes it at 12 markers. If the model is right, the new kelp should be related to the reefs up-current. She started in September and is learning to dive this winter.

Last paper Tallis I, Boakye Y (2026). Kinship analysis of recolonising bull kelp: a sampling design. Conservation Genetics Resources 18: 77–84.

Projects Spore dispersal

imogen.tallis@sorrelpoint.example

Ruairidh Fraser

MSc student

Strongylocentrotus purpuratus

Asks whether barren urchins are starving, by weighing their gonads.

Ruairidh dissects 60 urchins a month and weighs their gonads against their bodies. A low gonad index means starving; a high one means they are finding drift kelp somewhere. He shares the calipers with Rafael and the freezer with everyone.

Last paper Fraser R, Quintero R (2026). Gonad index of purple urchins across a barren edge. Western Society of Naturalists, abstract 214.

Projects Urchin barrens

ruairidh.fraser@sorrelpoint.example

Paloma Arce

MSc student

Pisaster ochraceus

Tracks whether sunflower and ochre stars come back to restored plots.

Paloma counts sea stars along every restoration transect, because they eat urchins and their wasting disease helped start the barrens. She photographs each star so the same animal is never counted twice. So far she has found 31 ochre stars and two sunflower stars.

Last paper Arce P, Zhao M-L (2026). Sea star counts on restored kelp plots, 2024 to 2026. Western Society of Naturalists, abstract 187.

Projects Kelp restoration

paloma.arce@sorrelpoint.example

Jonah Whitecloud

Research technician, seawater lab

Nereocystis luetkeana gametophytes

Runs the chillers, the pumps and the 2 a.m. alarm.

Jonah keeps 40 tanks at the temperature each experiment needs, to within a tenth of a degree. He built the alarm that texts him when a chiller fails, and it has saved two seasons of cultures. He also teaches every new student to change a filter without flooding Building C.

Ife Bankole

Undergraduate researcher

Nereocystis luetkeana

Checks drifter tracks against the model's paths.

Ife releases GPS drifters off the station pier and downloads their tracks a week later. She compares each track with the path Yaw's model predicted for the same day. The worst miss so far was 9 km, after a storm the model did not see coming.

Last paper Bankole I, Boakye Y (2026). Drifter checks of a coastal circulation model. Halvorsen Undergraduate Research Symposium, poster 7.

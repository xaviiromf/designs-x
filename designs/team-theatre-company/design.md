## Why it works

**The bold idea: the curtain call.** The section is a top-down ground plan of the fit-up stage, drawn the way a stage manager draws blocking in the prompt book. Every member of the company is a circle with their initials, and a small notch shows which way they face. The eight actors stand on yellow spike marks in the curtain-call line. Stage management are in the prompt corner (on the audience's right, because UK prompt side is stage left) and at the sound and light desk, and the creative team sit in Row A. Pick anyone and three things happen together. A pencilled move arrow unrolls from their mark to the down-centre mark and they walk down it, about 170 plan units, a real cross. The rest of the line closes the gap: everyone on the chosen actor's outer side steps one mark in, and every head turns toward them, nearest first (TR3 ripple by distance). The follow-spot slides onto them and its iris opens. The surtitle caption then changes to their name and parts, and their programme note opens in the list, and only once they arrive does their name tag appear, on the side away from the arrow. Choosing someone from stage management or the creative team sends the cast back to their marks, rippling outward from the centre, the line turns to look at that person, and the spot finds them where they work. "Clear the stage" (or Escape) sends everyone back to their marks, facing the audience. Everything else on the page supports this moment: the page load is the get-in that builds the plan, and scrolling to it brings the house lights down so the spot reads.

- **Light, because a rehearsal room is light.** The obvious theatre page is a dark auditorium with red velvet and gold (default 2, and a cliché). This design lives in the rehearsal room in daylight instead: neutral grey sprung-floor vinyl (`#C9C9C6`, with no green cast so it stays clear of the collection's pale grey-green team sections), white gaffer tape and programme paper (`#F5F6F2`) and a blue-black marker ink (`#1A1F33`). The only dark area is the literal dance floor, which real fit-up companies lay in black so that spike tape shows up on it.
- **Colour encodes department, everywhere it appears.** Yellow is the cast, blue is stage management and pink is the creative team. It appears on the spike marks, the facing notches, the hover ring, the group tape strips, each row's tape strip, the touch tiles and the nav indicator (cast yellow, the tape the company uses most). Colour never carries text: yellow and pink sit next to ink, every strip has a hard ink edge, and the names and jobs are always written out.
- **Type from playbills.** League Gothic revives Alternate Gothic, the condensed wood-type gothic of touring playbills. It sets the headline, the names in the list (in the cast-list convention), the initials and the name tags. Libre Baskerville carries everything else. It revives John Baskerville's types from the 18th century, the age of the printed theatre bill and programme, it holds up at the 13px plan labels, and its wide italic sets the parts each actor plays, as in a printed programme. Baskerville sets wide, so the body is 16px with 1.7 leading, the lead is 19px, and notes are capped at 58ch.
- **Leader dots carry meaning.** Programme rows use name, dotted leader, parts, because the leader pairs a name with its parts. Below 600px the leader drops and the parts sit under the name. Rows are separated by space, not rules.
- **The list and the drawing are one thing.** Hovering or focusing a row lights that person's ring and name tag on the plan, and the spot glances at them at half strength. On touch, each row starts with that person's prompt-book circle, so the list reads against the plan without hover.
- **Everyone has a real place.** An earlier plan used a separate dialog for crew notes. Putting everyone on the plan (prompt corner, desk, Row A) gave one selection model and removed the dialog.
- **Plain copy.** There are no stat tiles, eyebrows or middle-dot meta strings. The facts (17 parts, 41 halls, the 760 mm door at Garsdale) live in sentences, and meta is written as a sentence: "From Sedbergh; with us since 2012."
- **Restored after review.** The critique had cut the line closing the gap as too close to nav-concert-hall. That design lights chairs on a platform and moves nobody, and without the close the signature left a dead spike mark and one moving body. The close is one mark, not a re-spacing of the whole line, and it ripples from the gap.
- **Cut at critique.** The company walking on from the wings at load. CU2 cursor-following portrait cards with giant cropped initials: a stock trope, a second pointer follower beside the spot, and a repeat of the plan's circles. A stat row, a handwritten marker face for the initials, DrawSVG (every tape strip and the arrow are straight `scaleX` strokes; the arrow is dashed with an open chevron head, as moves are pencilled), the vertical "Wings" label, and, as the last accessory removed, a small scale "bow" when the chosen person arrived. The walk down centre already is the bow.

## Motion

Everything runs inside `gsap.matchMedia` with the conditions `motion`, `reduce` and `fine` (`(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`). The house eases are expo.out for entrances and follows (`--ease-out`), expo.inOut for wipes and moves (`--ease-move`), and none for the scrub. A leave runs about 30% faster than its enter. Lenis runs from `gsap.ticker` (`autoRaf: false`, lerp 0.1, `lagSmoothing(0)`). Anchors go through `lenis.scrollTo` with duration `min(1.3, .65 + distance / 4000)`, then focus moves to the target. The page has no menu or dialog, so Lenis never needs stopping; an adapter that adds an overlay must call `lenis.stop()` while it is open. The selection state (`active`) lives outside matchMedia, so when a query flips, `paint()` re-applies it with `gsap.set`. Hidden first-frame states exist only under `html.js` with no reduced-motion preference: `[data-rv]` elements are hidden only while the get-in is due (`html.js:not(.played)`), and the three `.gtape` group strips stay at `scaleX(0)` under `html.js` until the S2 ScrollTrigger lays them. The section starts at h2 (a team section); the host page supplies the h1. If the CDN fails, the `js` class is removed and the page shows its final state, and selection still opens notes and moves people (without animation). The callback's cleanup aborts its `AbortController`, removes the ticker and destroys Lenis.

Touch: the load sequence and scroll moments play the same, on native scroll (Lenis leaves touch alone). Hover effects sit under `@media (hover: hover)` or check `pointerType === 'mouse'`. Under `(pointer: coarse)` each row's strip becomes a 44px prompt-book circle with the person's initials in a ring of their department colour, filled ink when their note is open. A tap selects. Name tags show only for the chosen person. Links keep their underline, and buttons answer with the press scale and an `:active` fill. Keyboard: Tab enters the plan at the chosen person (or the first). Arrow keys, Home and End move between people (roving tabindex). Enter and Space choose. Escape clears the stage from the plan, the caption or the list; from the caption, focus moves to the person who was cleared, since the caption buttons disappear, and from inside an open note it moves to that person's row, since the note hides. Focus outlines are chalk on the stage and ink in the hall and elsewhere.

## Do not touch

- **One selection model.** A circle, a row and the keyboard all call `select(id)`. Choosing the chosen person again clears the stage. Each call jumps the previous timeline to its end (`progress(1)`) before building its own, so rapid taps never stack, and the load timeline is finished first, so nothing waits on the get-in.
- **The line closes by one mark.** `layout(id)` is the only source of positions: the chosen actor goes to down centre and everyone on their outer side (further from the centre line) moves one mark (76 units) in. `select()` tweens to it and `paint()` sets it. The returning actor always leaves down centre at once, and the ripple that reopens their mark starts first, so two circles never sit on one mark.
- **Positions are plan units.** Each person's home is `--x`/`--y` in an 800 x 620 plan (curtain-call line on y 190, down centre at 400, 362), placed with `left`/`top` percentages. GSAP moves them by x/y in pixels, computed as units x width / 800 and re-applied by a ResizeObserver. Keep `aspect-ratio: 800 / 620` on `.plan`, or every position drifts.
- **28px targets on phones.** The curtain-call marks are 76 units apart, and the plan is full-bleed below 600px. That keeps the 28px circles at least 6px apart at 360px wide, with initials at 12.9px. Fewer, wider marks are fine. More marks need fewer people or the list as the main touch path. A move arrow shorter than 16px is not drawn (the walk alone shows the move). Every cast mark, and down centre, stays on the black floor, because the chalk focus ring on cast circles needs the dark ground; anyone placed in the hall gets `data-zone="hall"` and an ink ring.
- **The house never dims below 6:1.** `--house-dim` stops at 0.22 ink, where ink labels on the dimmed floor are 6.5:1 (6.1:1 at 0.25, 5.5:1 at 0.30).
- **Dimmed rows stay readable.** `--row-dim` 0.72 keeps ink on the floor at 4.9:1. At 0.5 it falls to 2.8:1.
- **Only transform, opacity and clip-path animate.** The accordion is a Flip of the row buttons and group heads (only transforms), the pencil-in is a clip-path polygon, and the tape and arrow are `scaleX` strokes. Do not swap in `height`, `grid-template-rows`, stroke-dashoffset or DrawSVG.
- **The caption keeps its height.** `--cap-h` reserves room for a name, a parts line and the two actions, so the sticky stage never changes height and the list never jumps under a finger when someone is chosen.
- **Sticky, not pinned.** The stage uses CSS `position: sticky` (desktop top 24px; below 992px top 0, only when the viewport is at least 700px tall). Anchors into the list use `scroll-margin-top` set from the stage's measured height (`--stick`).
- **Contrast:** ink on floor 9.8:1, on chalk 15.0:1; ink-soft on floor 5.8:1, on chalk 8.9:1; chalk on the dance floor 15.0:1 (9.9:1 under the working-light wash); chalk on blue 5.5:1; ink on yellow 9.7:1, on pink 4.9:1 (measured with the WCAG formula). Yellow on the floor is about 1:1, so yellow strips always carry an ink edge and never stand alone; yellow, blue and pink are never text on the floor.

## Adapting

1. **Tokens first.** Map the room onto `--floor`, `--chalk` and `--ink`, and one colour per group onto `--spike-yellow`, `--spike-blue` and `--spike-pink` (rename them with your groups, and keep `[data-dept]` in step). Check ink over the new floor at `--house-dim` for 6:1 or more, and ink at `--row-dim` for 4.5:1, before keeping either value.
2. **Your people.** Each person is one plan button (`data-id`, `data-dept`, `--x`/`--y`; fixed people get `data-face`, their facing in degrees, and `data-zone="hall"` for an ink focus ring) plus one programme row (`#rb-<id>` with `data-ini` on its strip, and `#note-<id>`). The caption reads the name and parts from the row, the pencil-in runs by distance from down centre, and the button's `aria-label` is written by hand ("Ama Owusu, Hermione and Dorcas"). For a band, a kitchen or a pit crew, redraw the plan's tape and labels (`.t`, `.mk`, `.lb`) and move "down centre" (`DC`) to the place where the chosen person should stand.
3. **Copy.** Names under about 18 characters keep the leader row on one line in the 5-column list. Parts and jobs stay short ("Composer and music director" is the longest that fits). Notes are one or two sentences, and meta is one sentence.
4. **Retime or remove motion.** CSS durations and easings are tokens (`--dur-*`, `--ease-out`, `--ease-move`). Script timings are literals in three places: the get-in master timeline (positions 0 to 1.9s), `select()` and `glance()`. To remove an effect, delete its tween: `select()` and `paint()` still place everything, because a timeline under reduced motion is simply jumped to its end. To drop the pencil-in, delete its whole loop (it also fades the circles in). To drop GSAP and Lenis entirely, remove the five CDN tags: the script falls back to instant selection with CSS `translate`, and the `js` class (and with it every hidden first-frame state) is removed. If you keep GSAP but delete the get-in, also add the `played` class, or the `[data-rv]` elements stay hidden. NV4, HV2, HV4 and HV7 are plain CSS transitions and work without GSAP.
5. **Stack translation.** Keep `layout()`, `select()`, `notes()` and `paint()` as plain functions over a people array, and run the matchMedia block in a `useGSAP` hook. The selection state lives in your store, outside the effect.

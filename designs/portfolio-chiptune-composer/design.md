## Why it works

**The one bold idea: the portfolio is the tracker, and the page is the song.** A chiptune composer works in a tracker: a tall grid of rows (time) by channels (voices), with a bar that marks the row being played. Here the first thing a game developer sees is one real pattern from one of six soundtracks, 32 rows by four channels, written in `DATA` as short strings. "Play the pattern" runs a pink playhead down it at the track's tempo: a hard jump per row, the notes under the bar turning into solid blocks of their channel's hue, each channel's eight-cell meter jumping to the note's volume and falling back one cell at a time. With "Sound on" the same rows are played through the Web Audio API. Everything else on the page borrows the tracker's parts and stays quiet: the page's parts are the song's orders, the yearly output is nine tall meters, the form is a six-row pattern.

- **Not a lit-cell grid, and not a block inspector.** cta-indie-game lights a decaying wake of lattice cells behind the pointer, and crud-memory-card frames the blocks of a save under the pointer. This is neither a trail nor a group selection: the tracker has exactly one playhead and one edit cursor, each a whole row, with no decay and no memory. What moves is a position in time, and the meters above the columns answer it.
- **Three row states, one language.** A row at rest is `--sheet` notes and `--rest` dashes on the `--well`. The row under the edit cursor is inverted as a band (`--sheet` ground, `--aubergine` ink), and the cell under the cursor stays dark with a `--cursor` frame and its note in its channel's hue. The row under the playhead is a solid `--cursor` bar whose notes are blocks of channel hue. The form uses the same inverted band for the row being edited, so "current row" looks the same wherever it appears.
- **Colour has jobs.** The ground is aubergine `#40103C`, with a darker tone (`--well`) for the pattern and field wells and the scrim, and a lighter one (`--beat`) for every fourth row. `--sheet` is text and the inverted band. `--rest` is secondary text, rules and empty cells. The four channel hues appear only on that channel's name, meter, mute button, lit notes and readout: `--pulse-1` yellow, `--pulse-2` cyan, `--triangle` mint, `--noise` coral. `--cursor` pink is the playhead (in the pattern and in the order list), the frame of the edit cursor, the primary button and every focus ring, and nothing else. Errors have no hue: a hatched `--sheet` bar and bold `--sheet` text on a reserved line. The output chart uses three tones of the neutral pair (`--sheet`, `--rest`, `--rest-deep`), not new hues.
- **The page is laid out like the program.** On desktop the order list is a narrow column pinned to the left edge, the pattern sits in the middle with a sticky head (play button, channel names, mute buttons, meters) and a readout that sticks to the bottom of the screen, and the track list, sound switch and help sit in a sticky side column. On phones the order list becomes a five-cell strip that sticks under the top, and the pattern's head sticks under it. From 1024 to 1279px the headline takes one line with the lede beside the two stacked buttons, and the contact links sit above the form until 1200px. At 1440x900, 1366x650 and 1280x600 the head, meters and at least the first fourteen rows are above the fold.
- **Scroll is a second way to play.** While nothing is playing, the row that crosses a fixed line of the screen takes the playhead, so scrolling through the pattern steps it one row per 22px, both ways, with the meters kicking for each row: the pattern scrolls under its bar exactly as it does in a tracker. The order list's marker makes the same hard jump from order to order.
- **Chart A is real data and fully operable.** The pattern is a `role="grid"`: arrow keys move the cursor, Page Up and Page Down move a beat, Home and End go to the first and last channel, Enter or Space plays the row. Every cell has an `aria-label` ("Triangle, row 03: A-2, volume 7"), the readout names the row and what each channel does there, and a disclosure gives the whole pattern as a table.
- **Chart B: nine meters.** Minutes of finished music per year, 2018 to 2026, as stacked columns of cells where one cell is two minutes, in three tones by kind. A year is picked by pointing at it, tapping it or with the arrow keys (a `radiogroup` with a roving tab stop). The readout gives the total in Handjet, the three kinds and the games shipped; a disclosure gives the figures as a table. Numbers change instantly.
- **Sound is strictly optional.** It is off by default, the `AudioContext` is created only after the visitor turns the switch on, the master gain is 0.09, and every call is guarded: a browser without Web Audio unchecks the switch, says so in a line of text and logs nothing. The envelope is stepped (full, 60%, off) because that is how the hardware sounds. The playhead and the sound run on plain timers, so both work when the CDN is blocked.
- **Type.** Handjet is used four times: the H2, the track sheet's title, the row number in the readout and the year total. Everything in the pattern is Host Grotesk with tabular figures at 15px, so 128 cells stay readable. There is no monospace.
- **Flows and semantics.** The section's `h2` is the first heading; the dialog's title is an `h2` in the top layer. The order list is a `nav` with an ordered list of links and `aria-current`. Tracks are native radios, the sound control is a checkbox with `role="switch"`, selected work is a table whose row headers are buttons, contact links come from `CONTACT` and open in a new tab with a hidden "(opens in a new tab)". The enquiry validates in JavaScript with messages that say how to fix each field, keeps a reserved line per field, moves focus to the first bad field, echoes the visitor's values in the confirmation and restores the form with "Write another". "Ask for something like this" in the track sheet closes the dialog, names the track in the empty notes field and puts the cursor in the form.

Deviations from the brief, and why:

- **The readout is the pattern's last row, not a separate sentence.** It uses the same five columns as the pattern (row number, then the note and volume of each channel) and sticks to the bottom of the screen while the pattern is taller than the window, so it stays in view while the playhead moves. The full wording is in each cell's `aria-label` and in the table.
- **A second play control sits in the pattern's head.** On phones the top buttons scroll away before the pattern arrives, so the gutter of the sticky head carries a small play/stop button that mirrors "Play the pattern".
- **The edit cursor's band is inverted in `--sheet`, with `--cursor` only as the frame of the cell.** A second solid pink band would be mistaken for the playhead when both are on screen.
- **The song-load cut is reused when another track is loaded** (four blocks of eight rows, then the meters). It is the same effect on the same event, a song loading; if a pattern is playing the new track simply starts playing.
- **With reduced motion, Play still works** because it is an explicit request, but it runs one pass instead of two and the meters hold the row's level instead of falling.
- **The fanfare comes to rest on its last note** (row 06) after the playhead has run through all eight rows, so the confirmation ends on a lit chord and not on an empty row.

## Do not touch

- **One grid template for the tracker.** `.trk` defines `--cols` (`--gut` plus four equal columns) and the head, every row and the readout use it. Change the columns in one of them and the meters stop sitting over their notes.
- **`--row` is the unit of time.** Row height drives the pattern's height, the scroll scrub (one row of scroll is one row of playhead) and the sticky budget above the fold. It is 22px, 20px on short desktop windows and 24px on phones (a 24px tap target). The beat tint is `:nth-child(4n + 1)`: four rows to the beat.
- **A channel's hue stays on its channel.** `[data-ch]` sets `--ch`; names, meters, mute buttons, lit notes and readout cells read it. Do not use a channel hue for a button, a chart tone or an accent.
- **`--cursor` is the playhead, the edit frame, the primary button and focus.** Giving it to links, headings or chart marks dissolves the one thing the eye should follow.
- **The three row states must stay distinct**: rest, inverted band (edit), solid bar with hue blocks (playhead). The playhead wins when both fall on one row.
- **Patterns are data.** Each track's `pattern` is four strings of 32 tokens; `parsePattern` refuses channels of different lengths, and `runSelfTest()` (load the page with `#selftest`) checks all six, the fanfare, the parsers and the contact links. Keep the test passing when you edit `DATA`.
- **Sound stays off by default and created on a gesture.** Do not move `audio.set(true)` out of the switch's `change` handler, do not raise `SOUND.master` above about 0.12, and keep the `try`/`catch` guards: sound must never throw or log.
- **The playhead stops by itself**: after two passes, when the pattern leaves the screen (IntersectionObserver), when the tab is hidden, when the track sheet opens and when the enquiry is sent. The meter timer stops when every meter is at zero. Do not add a loop that runs at rest.
- **Stepped corners live on `.face` and `.inner`, never on the `<a>` or `<button>`,** so the focus ring is never clipped. The teeth are a sibling of the face, inside the button's own padding (`--tooth` above and below), so the ring encloses them in both phases.
- **Hidden states exist only under `html.js:not(.go)`** (the pattern's rows before the load cut) and are undone under reduced motion. With the CDN blocked the class is never added and every flow, including Play and sound, works in plain JavaScript.
- **Accessibility.** The grid keeps one tab stop and never traps Tab. `.cell:focus` has no outline because the edit cursor (band plus framed cell) is drawn for focus exactly as for the pointer. Form rows keep their label and field in one band; the error line is reserved; `aria-invalid` and the message are cleared as soon as the value is valid. The fanfare is `aria-hidden` with its notes named in a sentence under it.

## Motion

House stepping: nothing eases. Every change is a hard cut on a clock: the playhead jumps a row at a time, meters move a cell at a time, the load, the dialog and the buttons are sequences of cuts timed by `--d-*` tokens, and CSS uses `opacity` with a 0s transition and a delay, or `step-end` keyframes. Only opacity, clip-path and class changes are involved; colour changes are instant. The motion layer is built in `gsap.matchMedia` (motion, reduce). Lenis runs on `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false` and `lagSmoothing(0)`, `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`, and touch keeps native scrolling. The load cut waits for the fonts (a 1.5s cap) and for a visible page. The playhead, the meters, the sound, the order marker, the press timing and all CSS effects are plain JavaScript and CSS and work without the libraries.

## Adapting

1. **Decide what your rows and channels are.** The design needs a real sequence: a pattern of notes, a lighting cue list, a chart of inputs. Four channels fit a phone; with more, drop the mute label to an icon and narrow `--gut`.
2. **Edit `DATA`.** `channels` names the columns and their default volume. Each track has its facts and a `pattern` of four strings: `.` for an empty row, `C5` or `A#3` for a note, `K S H` for kick, snare and hat, `:7` after a token for its volume (1 to 8), bar signs only for reading. `fanfare` is the confirmation's eight rows. `years` holds minutes per kind in multiples of two (one cell). Run the page with `#selftest` after every edit.
3. **Edit `CONTACT`.** One line per link; delete a line to remove it. `email`, `whatsapp`, `github`, `mastodon`, `instagram` and `itch` take a handle or a full URL; any other key with a full URL becomes a link named after the key.
4. **Map your colours onto the tokens, keeping the jobs.** Ground and its two tones first (`--well` darker, `--beat` lighter, never black). One light text colour and one secondary. One hue per channel, each at 7:1 or better on the well, because they are also text. One cursor hue that is not any channel's.
5. **Copy.** The H2 is two lines at 78px beside a three-line lede; keep it under 30 characters. Track titles must fit a 150px option. The dialog takes three credits and two short paragraphs.
6. **Retime or remove.** Tempo comes from each track's `bpm`. Passes are the `2` in `play()`. The meter fall is `--d-fall`. To remove the scroll scrub, delete the `ScrollTrigger.create` block and the hint text. To remove the load cut, delete the `fill()` call in the load promise. To remove sound, delete the switch and the `audio` module; `audio.row` calls can stay as no-ops if you keep a stub.
7. **In a framework,** render the markup statically, keep the tracker as one component created by `makeTracker`, and port the script as one effect that returns the `gsap.matchMedia` cleanup (it kills the timelines and the ScrollTrigger, destroys Lenis and clears inline states).

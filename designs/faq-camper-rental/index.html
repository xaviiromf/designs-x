Booking and paying

Before you leave

A full car licence held for at least two years, and you must be 23 or older on the day of pick-up. Both of our vans weigh under 3,500 kg, so no special category is needed. If your licence was not issued in Chile, bring an International Driving Permit with it: police checkpoints on Ruta 7 ask to see both, and so do we at the handover. Every driver must be named on the contract.

We hold CLP 800,000 on a credit card in the main driver's name when you collect the van; debit and prepaid cards cannot carry the hold. Once the van is back with no new damage, we release it within 3 working days, and your bank may take up to 10 more days to show the money again. If there is damage, we send you photos and the repair quote before taking anything from the hold.

Unlimited kilometres, basic insurance with a CLP 1,500,000 excess, one 5 kg gas bottle, bedding and towels for everyone booked, a kitchen kit, two folding chairs, a 20 L water jerrycan, and our printed notes on ferries and fuel stops.

Not included: fuel, ferry fares, campsites, national park fees and the Argentina permit, which has to be ordered before you arrive.

Cancel 30 days or more before pick-up and we refund everything. From 29 to 8 days before, we keep 30% of the rental; within 7 days, we keep 50%. You can move your dates once for free, as long as a van is available and the new trip meets the minimum length: 5 nights from December to February, 3 nights the rest of the year.

Pick-up

Puerto Montt depot, km 0

At our depot on Camino Chinquihue km 6, on the coast road west of Puerto Montt. If you fly into El Tepual, send us your flight number at least 48 hours ahead and our shuttle meets you at arrivals at no charge; the drive takes about 30 minutes. You can leave one car at the depot for free for the length of your trip.

Pick-up runs from 14:00 to 17:00. Allow about an hour: we walk round the van with you and photograph every existing mark, show you how the water, power and gas work, and go through the paperwork. If you arrive after 17:00, the handover moves to 9:00 the next morning, so book a flight that lands before noon.

In Puerto Montt. Its large supermarkets are the last big ones until Coyhaique, about 630 km south, and choice shrinks while prices rise along the way. Buy dry food, coffee and anything you cannot do without here. Chaitén and La Junta have small shops for bread, fruit and vegetables, but in February they sell out soon after each ferry arrives.

Both vans run on all-terrain tyres and carry one full-size spare. If your trip goes south of Cochrane, we add a second spare, because the next tyre shop can be a day's drive away. Snow chains are free on request from May to September, when the passes near Coyhaique and Cerro Castillo can freeze; we show you how to fit them at pick-up.

Ferries

La Arena to Caleta Gonzalo, km 46 to 205

One of the two, yes. La Arena to Puelche takes no bookings: ferries sail often in daylight, the crossing takes about 30 minutes, and you pay on board.

Hornopirén to Caleta Gonzalo must be booked as soon as your dates are fixed if you travel in January or February. Give the van length from your contract: 5.4 m for the Ducato, 6.9 m for the Sprinter. Fares are paid to the ferry operators and are not part of our rate.

It is two ferries with about 10 km of gravel between them, from Leptepu to Fiordo Largo, and about five hours from ramp to ramp. Be at the Hornopirén ramp an hour before sailing with your booking printed or on your phone. The second ferry waits for the first, but if you miss the first sailing there is no other way across that day.

Not by road on the Chilean side: the fjords south of Puerto Montt cut the Carretera Austral. You can take the overnight ferry from Puerto Montt straight to Chaitén, which skips both crossings, or drive round through Argentina and come back in at Futaleufú. The Argentine route needs the Argentina permit and adds about two days.

Gravel, fuel and nights

Chaitén to the Chile Chico turn-off, km 205 to 900

Our limit on ripio is 60 km/h, and 40 km/h on bends and in rain. When a truck comes the other way, slow to 30 and keep right: that is when the stones fly. A small chip is normal; send the desk a photo and drive on. Windscreen cover costs CLP 9,000 a day; without it, a replacement costs CLP 180,000.

There are fuel stations in Chaitén, La Junta, Coyhaique, Puerto Río Tranquilo and Cochrane, and smaller pumps in between that can run dry in February. Our rule is simple: never drive past a station with less than half a tank. A full tank lasts about 600 km on gravel, but headwinds and hills cut that more than you expect.

Much of the land beside the road is private, even where there is no fence, so use signed campings: most have hot showers, power and a place to empty grey water. In Queulat, Cerro Castillo and Patagonia national parks you may only stay at the designated sites, and in summer they need booking ahead. Empty grey water only at dump points, never at the roadside or into a river.

The 80 L tank lasts about three days for two people who are careful with the tap. The 100 Ah lithium house battery and the 200 W solar panel keep the fridge and lights running on a clear summer day. After a run of grey days, drive for two hours to charge from the engine, or plug in at a camping for the night.

Every van carries a satellite messenger. Use SOS for emergencies only: it goes straight to the rescue services. For anything else, send the preset "Breakdown, not urgent" message, which includes your position. The desk replies within two hours between 8:00 and 22:00 and sends help from our partner workshop in Coyhaique or the nearest town.

Crossing to Argentina

El Maitén junction on Lago General Carrera, km 900 to 970

Yes, with our notarised exit permit and Argentine third-party insurance, which we arrange together. Ask for them at least 10 working days before pick-up; they cost CLP 120,000 per trip. Without the permit, Chilean customs turns you back at the border, whatever the rental contract says, and we cannot fix that by phone.

Futaleufú, Coyhaique Alto, Paso Huemules, and Chile Chico to Los Antiguos. Paso Roballos is not allowed: the road is unmaintained gravel and our insurance does not cover it. Check the opening hours before you set off; most of these posts close at night, and some close for days in heavy snow.

Cross with an empty fridge. Fresh fruit, vegetables, meat, cheese and honey are confiscated when you come back into Chile, and everything has to be declared on the SAG form, the agriculture service's declaration. Declaring something is never a problem; not declaring it can mean a fine. If you are unsure about an item, declare it.

The far south and the way back

Cochrane to Villa O'Higgins, km 970 to 1,240

Yes. South of Cochrane the gravel is narrower and slower, and the road to Villa O'Higgins includes the short, free ferry from Puerto Yungay to Río Bravo, which only sails a few times a day: plan the day around its timetable. Villa O'Higgins is where Ruta 7 ends; from there, the only way on is by boat and on foot.

You can drop it at Balmaceda airport, near Coyhaique, for CLP 450,000, on trips of 10 nights or more. Every other trip ends back at the Puerto Montt depot, where returns run from 8:00 to 11:00. The same return checklist applies at both, and we need to know which one you will use when you book.

Full tank, grey and black water tanks emptied, and the inside swept. Road dust on the outside is expected, so do not pay for a wash. Up to two hours late costs nothing if you message the desk before you arrive; later than that, or without a message, we charge another day. After the walk-round, the deposit is released as usual.
